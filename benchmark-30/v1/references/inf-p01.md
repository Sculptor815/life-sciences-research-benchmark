# inf-p01: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

Two conclusions must be separated: whether the citation is a real source, and whether the analysis supports a causal pathway. The source question is answered unusually clearly. C2 is a signed provenance record stating that record WG-44 was intentionally invented for a training exercise and was never a journal article. That is affirmative evidence that the citation is fabricated for teaching, not merely unverifiable. It is not an accusation against a real person: the names, journal and identifier belong to the synthetic dossier, and the fabrication is of the record, not a claim about researchers. Because the record was invented, a journal correction or retraction state is not applicable; asking for one would be a category error. The supplied title, with its phrase about proof, should not be allowed to imply that the source carries demonstrative force. The analysis question is separate and fails on its own terms. E1 says cases and controls were sequenced at different centers and have different ancestry distributions. This makes sequencing center, ancestry and case status entangled, and when they are completely confounded no statistical adjustment can restore the missing comparison. Regression, principal components, batch correction and integration can reduce measured imbalance, but they cannot manufacture observations in the missing cells of the design; complete batch confounding is not fixed by regression or integration. E2 compounds the problem: the burden test uses variants passing center-specific filters, and coverage and relatedness are not reported. Center-specific filtering means cases and controls may have been evaluated over different variant sets, so a burden difference can reflect differential callability rather than disease biology. Unreported relatedness threatens the independence assumptions of the test and can inflate significance. E3 adds an enrichment problem: enrichment used all annotated genes rather than genes that could actually pass the study's filters. The background for an enrichment test must be the tested or testable feature universe; using an inappropriate all-gene background can bias significance in either direction and can make an artifactual set look enriched. Given all of this, the dossier does not support a causal pathway claim. Even a robust burden association, properly analyzed, would establish a statistical association that would still need functional and independent evidence before it could be called causal, and burden tests cannot by themselves establish direction or molecular mechanism. Several incorrect alternatives recur. Treating a plausible title as evidence of a real paper is backwards. Treating a smaller burden p-value as evidence that sequencing-center bias is gone confuses statistical significance with design validity. Treating enrichment as establishing causation reverses the inferential order. Inferring deliberate misconduct by a real named researcher from a synthetic record exceeds the evidence and misreads the dossier. Discriminating next steps, proposed rather than performed, are an independent, ancestry-balanced cohort with harmonized joint calling and comparable coverage across groups; relatedness-aware mixed models; restriction of the analysis to matched callable regions with sensitivity analyses; a prespecified enrichment universe with a dated gene-set release and negative-control gene sets; and, only after a robust association is established, targeted functional experiments such as perturbation of candidate genes in relevant models. If the association survives harmonization and balanced sampling, the pathway hypothesis gains support. If it disappears when center, ancestry and callable regions are matched, the original signal was likely structural. the citation is a known synthetic fabrication that should be labelled as such without implicating real people, and independently of that, the analysis cannot support a causal pathway claim.
