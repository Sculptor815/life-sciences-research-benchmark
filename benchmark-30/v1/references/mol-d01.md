# mol-d01: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

Proposed design. This is a plan for experiments that have not been performed; every step below is proposed, not completed. The question is whether kinase K's catalytic activity, rather than its scaffolding role, is required for ligand-induced signaling and growth in a cancer cell line. The primary estimand is the acute ligand-induced change in phospho-S relative to total S, compared across rescue conditions in a K-null background; the secondary estimand is growth over several days in the same conditions. Observed starting evidence and assumptions must be kept apart. M1 reports that a pooled K knockout lowers phospho-S after ligand stimulation and slows growth. M2 reports that total S is unchanged. M3 reports that one unvalidated kinase-dead construct is available and that its abundance and folding have not been measured. From M1 and M2 the defensible starting statement is that K loss reduces ligand-induced phospho-S without reducing total S abundance, which is consistent with a requirement for K in the pathway; it does not by itself distinguish catalytic from scaffolding function, and pooled knockout data can mix editing efficiencies, off-target effects and selection of different cell states. M3 is the critical gap: an inactive mutant cannot be used as evidence about catalysis until it is shown to be expressed, folded, soluble and capable of binding the relevant partners, because an unstable or misfolded mutant produces a negative result for trivial reasons. Preparation, identity and quality control. Proposed steps: authenticate the cell line by short tandem repeat profiling and confirm mycoplasma negativity and passage history; for the knockout, verify biallelic disruption by amplicon sequencing or equivalent and confirm loss of K protein by a validated antibody, with a second independent perturbation such as an independent guide RNA or an inducible degradation system to guard against clone-specific effects; validate the phospho-S antibody with phospho-peptide competition and a phosphatase-treated control and validate the total-S antibody against a distinct epitope; for the rescue constructs, verify comparable messenger RNA and protein abundance at near-endogenous levels by quantitative polymerase chain reaction and quantitative western blotting, verify correct localization, confirm that the inactive construct retains binding to at least one known K partner by co-immunoprecipitation, and verify loss of catalytic activity using an in vitro kinase assay or a defined cellular substrate readout. The exact K gene identifier, transcript, antibody details and partner identities are required author inputs; none can be invented here. Experimental unit, allocation and blinding. The independent biological unit is a cell preparation derived from an independent thaw or an independent knockout clone, not a well and not a reading. Technical wells are nested within preparations. Proposed allocation: randomize plate position and treatment order; blind the analyst to condition during image acquisition, plate reading and growth counting; prespecify the primary contrast and the analysis before unblinding. Sample size cannot be set from an unknown effect size. Proposed calibration: run a pilot with at least three independent preparations per key condition to estimate variance components for phospho-S and growth, then compute the number of independent preparations required for 80 percent power at a two-sided 0.05 level for the prespecified wild-type versus inactive rescue contrast; report the assumed effect size and its source as an assumption, and stop or redesign if the observed variance makes the required n infeasible. Intervention and schedule. The ligand identity, receptor, dose and timing must be supplied by the author. Proposed calibration: generate a ligand dose-response for phospho-S in parental cells, choose a prespecified submaximal concentration with adequate measurement range, verify the local dose-response slope, and include additional doses to detect saturation. Proposed acute signaling schedule: measure phospho-S and total-S at baseline and at several early times after stimulation, for example 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 minutes, with exact times adjusted to the author's ligand system and reported; proposed growth schedule: measure viable cell number or confluence at day 0 and every one to two days for five to seven days, keeping control wells below confluence limits. Serum concentration, plating density and vehicle are standardized across conditions. Controls. Proposed controls include parental cells, a non-targeting guide RNA control, knockout plus empty vector, knockout plus wild-type K rescue, knockout plus inactive K rescue, unstimulated and ligand-stimulated arms for each, and ligand-only and vehicle-only wells; an independently derived second knockout or degradation line; if feasible a second inactivating mutation or an analogue-sensitive allele, to avoid relying on one mutation; and general viability or apoptosis measurements to detect nonspecific toxicity from the rescue constructs. Ordered measurements. Proposed order: first identity and quality-control assays; then knockout validation; then rescue expression, localization, inactive-construct integrity and binding; then the acute phospho-S and total-S measurements; then parallel downstream signaling markers; then growth kinetics; then toxicity and compensation checks; and finally an orthogonal in vitro kinase measurement with purified K and S if reagents permit. Analysis and decision rules. Analyze the acute phospho-S over total-S ratio on a log scale with a mixed model treating preparation as a random effect and wells as nested technical replicates, and analyze growth with a repeated-measures model per preparation. Report effect sizes with confidence intervals and prespecify a small family of secondary contrasts with multiplicity control. Proposed decision rules: if wild-type rescue restores ligand-induced phospho-S and growth while the inactive rescue does not, and the inactive protein is validated as stable, folded and binding-competent, the catalytic-requirement hypothesis gains support. If both rescue constructs restore the phenotype, a noncatalytic or scaffolding contribution is favored. If neither rescues, the result is uninterpretable until expression, localization, assay timing and clonal adaptation are investigated. If the inactive mutant is unstable or misfolded, no conclusion about catalysis can be drawn. Troubleshooting. Proposed checks: if wild-type rescue fails, verify construct abundance and localization, ligand dose and timing, antibody performance and whether the knockout clone adapted; if the inactive mutant aggregates, switch to an alternative inactivation strategy such as a second biochemically validated catalytic mutation or selective acute catalytic inhibition; a degron removes both catalytic and scaffold functions and is only an orthogonal loss-of-protein control; if growth changes but signaling does not, separate proliferation from death with labeling and apoptosis assays; if signaling changes at only one ligand dose, re-examine the dose-response. Limitations. The design is limited to one cell line; it can establish requirement but not direct phosphorylation of S by K; necessity does not establish sufficiency; compensation and clonal effects remain possible even with two perturbations; the inactive mutant may retain residual activity or an aberrant conformation; and growth is a pleiotropic endpoint.
