# neu-p01: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

Source identity and the evidential force of the notice are separate questions. On identity, the teaching archive certifies article record NS-12 by R. Vale, 'Projection P in learning', 2023, and the submitted citation matches every field. The source therefore matches an existing record within the supplied archive; it is not a nonexistent article and it is not a fabricated citation. The archive is authoritative only inside this teaching dossier, which bounds how far that verification extends. A retraction notice is also attached. The notice states that animal identities could not be reconciled across acquisition files. That is a specific reliability concern, and it is the reason the record is classed as retracted. The notice does not determine the truth of every biological claim in the paper, does not supply corrected group identities, and does not establish intent or misconduct. Reporting the notice means reporting exactly what it says, with its limits, rather than converting it into a global verdict about the projection. On the evidence, E1 reports lower learned performance after projection inhibition. E2 states that the allocation file and the raw animal identities needed to verify independent groups are unavailable. Together these mean the reported effect cannot be audited: without reliable animal assignment, independence of observations and the composition of the compared groups cannot be checked, so the reported sample size, degrees of freedom and uncertainty cannot be trusted from the summary alone. A small p-value cannot repair a missing allocation record, because the p-value is computed under assumptions about independence and group structure that the missing record is supposed to support. The correct inferential position is that the dossier provides insufficient evidence for the necessity claim, and that this insufficiency does not prove the biological claim false. Absence of trustworthy support is not affirmative evidence of no effect. Incorrect alternatives include saying that the retraction proves the projection has no biological role; that a retracted paper never existed; that the notice supplies corrected group identities; and that a significant summary statistic can substitute for auditable group assignment. Each of these overreads the available material. Discriminating next steps, proposed rather than performed, begin with recovering auditable identity and allocation records if they still exist, and with an independent, preregistered, blinded replication in which animals are the independent unit, groups are balanced and counterbalanced for order, the perturbation is validated for expression, anatomical spread and physiological suppression, and opsin-negative lit controls plus no-light trials are included. A necessity design should inhibit only during acquisition or only during retrieval according to the prespecified question, and should measure locomotion and cue detection so that a performance deficit can be attributed to the circuit rather than to motor or sensory side effects. Even a clean replication would support necessity under the tested conditions, not sufficiency, and not the claim that the projection stores a memory. The notice has a narrower procedural implication: the acquisition records for this study cannot currently establish that the compared groups were independent, so the result is unreliable as evidence until independent records or a new study resolve the question. All human review of this draft remains pending, and no live external verification of the archive was performed.
