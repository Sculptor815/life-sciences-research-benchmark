# mol-k01: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

Observed evidence: the antibody detects a 50-kDa band and a 75-kDa band; loading is comparable; a validated biallelic deletion of the entire target coding sequence removes the 50-kDa band and leaves the 75-kDa band unchanged. The best-supported interpretation is that the 50-kDa band has genetic specificity support in this assay and that the 75-kDa band remains unassigned. The alternative claim is too strong: calling both bands proven target isoforms would require evidence that the 75-kDa species is also encoded by the target locus, such as a defined transcript variant or a mapped proteolytic product, yet the deletion removed the entire coding sequence and the 75-kDa signal survived. A full coding deletion is expected to eliminate the proteins that the locus encodes, so an unchanged band is evidence against, not for, assignment to that locus. The alternative claim confuses two different controls: equal loading establishes comparable sample amount and transfer, not antigen identity. Loading is a property of the lane, whereas specificity is a property of the antibody-antigen interaction, and a loading control cannot license a claim about which protein an antibody recognizes. The alternative claim is also unsupported: a validated biallelic deletion plus disappearance of the predicted 50-kDa band is the opposite of deletion failure, and a failed deletion would be expected to retain the 50-kDa signal. The residual 75-kDa band is more parsimoniously explained by a cross-reactive protein that shares an epitope, a distinct gene product, an aggregated or modified species, or a degradation fragment. The deeper principle is that genetic loss supports but does not prove specificity. A knockout experiment shows that a band depends on the target gene in that particular system; it does not prove that every signal the antibody produces is the target, and it does not transfer automatically to other assays such as immunofluorescence or immunohistochemistry, where fixation, permeabilization and epitope accessibility change the result. It also does not exclude compensation, an indirect effect on a different protein or expression from a separate locus; a truncated product from the deleted coding sequence is not a valid explanation for a confirmed complete coding deletion. Uncertainty here is real. The case is a synthetic teaching item, the supplied key is draft-0.1 and all expert review remains pending, and the antibody-validation source listed in that key is a proposal whose applicability still needs review. We are told loading is comparable but not that transfer, membrane lots and antibody incubations were uniform, and a quantified band is not a raw image, so saturation and background are unknown. Useful discriminating tests each carry a distinct prediction. An antibody against a different, non-overlapping epitope should detect the true target; if the 75-kDa band is target-derived it should also be recognized, and if it is cross-reactive it need not be. Immunogen peptide competition tests competition for antibody binding; it can also block cross-reactivity and does not independently identify a band. A second independent loss-of-function reagent, such as an independent deletion allele or an inducible degron, should reproduce loss of the 50-kDa band and should reduce the 75-kDa band as well only if that band is locus-derived. Epitope-tagged target expression should identify the cognate band by co-migration and anti-tag detection. Mass spectrometry of the excised 75-kDa band can identify the protein directly. Target-null cells and genetic rescue add further constraints. If the 75-kDa band survives all target-specific perturbations, the cross-reactivity explanation gains support; if it tracks the target in every orthogonal test, the isoform hypothesis gains support. the 50-kDa assignment is supported within this western blot context, the 75-kDa band stays unassigned, and broader specificity claims require orthogonal validation. A disappearing cross-reactive protein downstream of the deleted gene could also mimic specificity. Rescue at controlled expression, an antibody against a separate epitope, and direct protein identification address different parts of this ambiguity; peptide competition alone is insufficient.
