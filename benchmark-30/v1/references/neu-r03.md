# neu-r03: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

The unresolved question is whether neural activity represents uncertainty about an outcome separately from its expected value and from a brief prediction error. The original packet describes responses around unexpectedly delivered or omitted rewards. A new experiment can vary cue-conditioned reward probability and examine activity during anticipation separately from cue and outcome transients. A useful hypothesis predicts a nonmonotonic uncertainty-related signal, whereas a value signal should track expected reward under matched outcome magnitudes. These are competing interpretations to test, not results already present in the packet.

Specify an approved behavioral preparation, neural recording method and objective criteria for identifying the relevant neuron population. If physiological features are used without definitive molecular identification, preserve that uncertainty in the claim. Establish reward delivery calibration, cue timing, eye or body tracking and synchronized acquisition clocks. Verify delivered reward volume or magnitude on independent test trials. Record behavioral engagement, licking and movement so that motor preparation and arousal can be considered rather than assigned to neural uncertainty by default.

Train distinct counterbalanced cues associated with a probability grid that includes zero, one and intermediate values. Keep the nominal reward magnitude and cue-to-outcome interval fixed for the primary probability series. Randomize trial order within blocks and ensure enough trials to estimate both rewarded and unrewarded outcomes at intermediate probabilities. Verify learning with independent behavioral measures and model trial history. Do not exclude slow-learning subjects after inspecting the neural effect; define training and analysis rules prospectively.

A fixed-magnitude probability series changes expected value and variance together, so it does not by itself isolate uncertainty. Add a prespecified complementary condition set that matches expected value across different reward distributions, with practical probability and magnitude combinations. Avoid relying only on a visually distinctive cue at probability one half. Counterbalance cue identities across subjects, include familiar sensory controls and check salience. Matching expected value may alter magnitude-related sensory or motivational effects, so compare multiple matched pairs and report remaining nonidentifiability.

Record baseline, cue onset, anticipation, expected outcome time and post-outcome activity in every trial. Choose analysis windows from independent pilot data or timing rules, not the largest observed difference. Quantify phasic cue responses, outcome prediction-error responses and sustained or ramping delay activity separately. Keep rewarded and unrewarded intermediate-probability trials in the anticipatory analysis; splitting only on a later outcome can produce selection artifacts. Track movements and licks with the same clock and examine whether neural differences precede or simply follow them.

Fit explicit models containing expected reward, candidate uncertainty measures, recent outcomes, trial number and measured behavior, allowing appropriate subject and neuron dependence. Compare predictive performance on held-out blocks or sessions. Probability one half maximizes Bernoulli outcome variance for fixed magnitude, but several uncertainty constructs can yield related patterns; distinguish a particular model from a general uncertainty label. Correlated regressors can make coefficients unstable even when a model fits well. Report uncertainty, alternative model comparisons and their sensitivity to behavioral covariates.

Prespecify the primary nonmonotonic anticipatory contrast, such as intermediate probability against endpoints, together with monotonic value-related contrasts. Base sample-size planning on independent subjects and sessions with nested neurons, not solely on thousands of trials. Plan exclusions for failed timing, unstable recordings and failed reward delivery before group decoding. Preserve all valid neurons selected by the independent population-identification rule. Missing behavior tracking limits confound adjustment and must be reported rather than imputed from the neural trace.

A strong supportive outcome is delay activity highest around uncertain intermediate outcomes, replicated across cue identities and informative value-matched comparisons, while phasic responses follow prediction-related patterns. This supports separable neural correlates under the tested conditions. It does not establish that the activity causes learning, risk preference or attention. A causal extension would require a temporally restricted perturbation and controls for movement, motivation and sensory processing, with a behavioral prediction specified in advance. If the apparent uncertainty signal disappears with appropriate timing or cue controls, revise the mechanism rather than selecting a favorable recording subset.

The sealed Fiorillo, Tobler and Schultz paper reports a gradual delay-period response covarying with outcome uncertainty and distinguishes it from phasic responses. A qualified hit requires the uncertainty-versus-value question, probability manipulation, temporally separated neural readouts and a prediction distinguishing intermediate probabilities from endpoints. A name, a citation or a generic proposal to study dopamine is insufficient. Scientifically rigorous alternative next questions can receive quality points without a historical hit. The proposed controls exceed the narrow historical summary and are not attributed to unreviewed author methods.
