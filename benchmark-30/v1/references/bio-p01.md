# bio-p01: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

Two decisions must be separated here: whether the source can be verified, and whether the analysis supports the mechanism claim. On source identity, C1 supplies a local record, P. Orr, 'Q regulates translation', 2025, BC-28, with no DOI. C2 reports that one catalog search found no matching title but explicitly notes that catalog coverage is unspecified. A single empty search in a catalog whose coverage is unknown cannot establish that an article does not exist, and it certainly cannot establish fabrication. Combined with the absence of a persistent identifier, the honest label is that the source is insufficiently verified and that correction and retraction status is unknown. That is a statement about our records, not about the authors, and nothing here should be turned into an accusation against real people. On the mechanism claim, E1 reports that puromycin incorporation falls by 60 percent after Q in cells. Puromycin incorporation is a proxy for nascent polypeptide synthesis, and it can fall for many reasons: reduced global translation, reduced amino acid or energy supply, reduced puromycin uptake, altered cell state, or loss of viable cells. E2 makes those alternatives concrete, because ATP falls by 50 percent and viability is reduced. Translation is energetically expensive and requires ATP and GTP, so a 50 percent ATP drop is a strong confounder; a 60 percent incorporation drop in that setting is not selective evidence for peptide-bond inhibition. E3 confirms that no purified ribosome assay and no selectivity panel are available, so direct interaction with the ribosome and the selectivity of any interaction are untested. The narrower supported statement is therefore that puromycin incorporation is lower under Q in these cells, with energetic and viability changes present; the direct and selective mechanism remains unsupported. Several incorrect alternatives recur. Treating any decrease in incorporation as proof of direct ribosome inhibition ignores the many upstream causes of reduced synthesis. Declaring the paper fraudulent because a catalog search returned nothing overreads an incomplete search and would wrongly implicate real people. Ignoring the ATP and viability data leaves the most plausible confound unaddressed. Asking only for replication in cells would repeat the same ambiguity rather than resolve it, because the same energetic confound would persist. Uncertainty remains high: dose, exposure time and acute versus delayed effects are not reported in the packet, and puromycin incorporation depends on uptake and on the fraction of cells still synthesizing protein. Discriminating tests, proposed rather than performed, are a cell-free or purified translation system with defined ribosomes and mRNA, where peptide-bond formation can be measured directly and the energy-regenerating system is controlled; matched-ATP and viability controls, including nucleoside or metabolite support, to ask whether the incorporation drop persists when energetics are held constant; an acute dose-response and time course measured before viability loss; polysome profiling to locate a defect at initiation versus elongation; a selectivity panel against other nucleotide-triphosphate-dependent processes such as transcription, replication and unrelated enzymes; direct binding measurements such as ribosome binding assays, competition and resistance mutations in rRNA or ribosomal proteins; and orthogonal nascent-protein labeling that does not depend on puromycin uptake alone. If purified ribosomes show direct inhibition at concentrations that leave other enzymes unaffected, the selective peptide-bond claim gains support. If the cellular effect disappears when ATP, uptake and viability are matched, the claim should be replaced by a metabolism-dependent translation phenotype. The source decision stays separate from all of this: obtaining a persistent identifier and a full publication record is required before any bibliographic conclusion, and neither an empty search nor a plausible title settles it.
