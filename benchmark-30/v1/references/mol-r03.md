# mol-r03: first-round reference

This is the unchanged first-round reference text. The owner confirmed careful review on 3 October 2026. Earlier pending-review statements inside the historical text are retained as part of the snapshot, not current metadata. Corrections and expanded execution requirements appear in version two.

The unresolved question is what physical activity the released gasdermin amino-terminal fragment performs: does it directly permeabilize a lipid membrane, or activate another cellular executor? A related mechanistic question is whether the carboxy-terminal region inhibits that intrinsic activity. Cell death after fragment expression cannot distinguish these models. The highest-information next stage is a minimal reconstitution with orthogonal evidence of membrane pores, followed by controlled structure-function tests in cells. All procedures below are proposed; no unreported result or exact author setting is asserted.

Define the species, sequence, cleavage boundary and validated protein constructs before production. Prepare full-length protein, amino-terminal fragment, carboxy-terminal fragment, cleavage-resistant full-length protein and a deliberately inactive control whose folding can be checked. Record tags and purification history. Verify identity, purity, concentration, aggregation state and integrity with independent measurements. Compare tag-free or alternative-tag preparations when the tag could obstruct membrane binding. Use at least two independent protein preparations and several independent liposome preparations; technical wells do not substitute for these units.

Construct a prespecified lipid-composition panel that includes a baseline membrane and membranes differing in candidate charged lipids while controlling total lipid concentration and vesicle size. Validate vesicle size/dispersity and encapsulation of a soluble reporter. Remove unencapsulated reporter before measurements. Include no-protein, buffer-only, inactive-protein and complete-disruption controls to define zero and maximal release. Test protein preparations directly against free reporter to detect fluorescence quenching or enhancement. Any protease used for cleavage requires protease-only and matched carryover controls.

In a pilot, vary protein-to-lipid ratio and observation duration to identify a range where kinetics can be resolved before the reporter saturates. Freeze these settings before the confirmatory comparison. Randomize plate positions and blind the preparation codes. Compare full length, cleaved full length, amino-terminal fragment, carboxy-terminal fragment and cleavage-resistant protein under identical conditions. Follow leakage continuously, subtract matched blanks and normalize against the detergent-defined dynamic range. Record spontaneous leakage and discard only technical liposome failures according to prespecified criteria, not a lack of the desired protein effect.

Leakage is not sufficient evidence of a discrete pore: nonspecific detergent-like disruption or aggregates may also release dye. Add an orthogonal structural readout, such as validated membrane imaging, and a size-selective permeability assay using independently calibrated reporters. Measure membrane association with an assay that separates free aggregates from membrane-bound material and include liposome-free controls. Analyze pore or lesion counts without selecting only illustrative fields. If reliable structural resolution is unavailable, report direct permeabilization and leave pore architecture unresolved.

Test candidate membrane-binding or oligomerization substitutions guided by justified structural information. Confirm equal protein integrity and folding before comparing functional loss; a misfolded mutant cannot identify a specific interface. Assess concentration-dependent inhibition by the carboxy-terminal region and compare with matched unrelated protein. An isolated-domain interaction or artificial in-trans inhibition is not automatically equivalent to native intramolecular autoinhibition. Combining cleavage dependence, full-length inactivity, fragment activity and structurally interpretable mutations gives a stronger argument than any one result.

In a complementary noninfectious cellular model, use matched loss-of-function and rescue backgrounds with expression near the validated endogenous range. Verify localization and cleavage independently. Measure small-solute permeability, morphology, viability and release of a larger cytosolic marker over time. Separate early permeability from terminal rupture; a pore-forming fragment need not execute every subsequent lytic event. Include expression-only, inactive-mutant and instrument-background controls. Rescue differences must be interpreted alongside protein abundance and general cell health, not simply the nominal construct identity.

Prespecify the primary fragment-versus-full-length leakage contrast and the mutant-versus-intact contrast, with preparation-level uncertainty and multiplicity control for the lipid panel. Base sample size on pilot preparation variability and a meaningful permeability difference. Retain negative preparations and confidence intervals. If all samples leak, revisit vesicle stability; if only aggregated protein leaks, improve purification and reassess; if mutants lose folding, redesign the comparison. If cell effects persist but reconstitution fails despite validated positive controls, a missing cofactor or indirect mechanism remains plausible.

The strongest supportive outcome is lipid-dependent permeabilization by purified amino-terminal protein, independently observed membrane pores, and matched structure-function effects in cells. It establishes intrinsic membrane activity in the tested system, not universal physiological targeting or the complete mechanism of terminal rupture. The sealed Ding and colleagues follow-up supports pore formation and gasdermin autoinhibition; shared authors include Shi, Wang and Shao. A qualified hit requires the direct-versus-indirect question, minimal membrane reconstitution, orthogonal pore readout and conditional mechanistic prediction with appropriate artifact controls. The reported erratum concerns the received date and does not itself establish a data-integrity problem.
