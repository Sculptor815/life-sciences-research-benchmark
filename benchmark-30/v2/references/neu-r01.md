# neu-r01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The valuable biological question is whether the neural representation of a neutral context can become associated with an aversive event when that representation is activated internally, without the animal physically occupying that context during learning. The packet shows that stimulating a previously tagged population can influence retrieval-related behavior. It does not establish that the same intervention can supply a cue during acquisition of a new association. The proposal therefore changes the causal question from whether an existing representation can drive behavior to whether its timed reactivation can contribute to learning. These are different claims and require different experimental phases.

The principal hypothesis is that the tagged contextual ensemble can function as an internal conditioned cue. A competing hypothesis is that activation merely produces generalized arousal, immobility, or an acute aversive state. Another is that the tagging procedure captures cells responsive to common handling or stress rather than context identity. A fourth is that the intervention changes later exploration without forming an association. The design must distinguish these possibilities instead of treating any increase in freezing as confirmation. The anticipated result is conditional: it would be informative only if the controls below remain intact.

Stage one is to create a preregistered timeline with separate labeling, association, retention, and testing periods. Before collecting experimental data, specify which event occurs in each context, when labeling can occur, and when optical stimulation is permitted. Context A is initially neutral; context B is distinguishable and contains the conditioning event; context C is a specificity control. Context identities should be counterbalanced across apparatus so that one odor, floor, room, or experimenter is not confounded with the conceptual role of A. Record cleaning procedures and handling histories consistently.

Stage two is to validate population targeting independently of the behavioral endpoint. Confirm the identity of the activity-dependent labeling construct and the anatomical boundaries of the intended dentate-gyrus population. Use histology and a prespecified anatomical inclusion rule to quantify expression, misplaced expression, and the fraction of tagged cells. Confirm that the labeling window closes before conditioning in B. A separate validation cohort can establish whether the stimulation drives the targeted cells with the intended temporal fidelity. Validation should not select only animals showing the desired memory behavior.

Stage three is to calibrate the intervention. Record the optical device, measured output at the relevant connection, stability over the session, and a justified estimate or measurement of exposure at the target. Validate that the selected pattern produces reliable neural activation without broad movement suppression, obvious tissue damage, or excessive heating. The exact numerical settings, targeting coordinates, and tagging schedule are not established by the packet and must be obtained from verified protocols and local validation before execution. They must not be invented and attributed to the historical paper. Use one frozen parameter set in the confirmatory experiment.

Stage four assigns animals to conditions using concealed random allocation balanced for prespecified factors such as sex, cohort, and baseline locomotion. The central group receives ensemble reactivation during the conditioning event in B after neutral tagging in A. Comparison groups include the same temporal sequence without functional optical activation, activation of the tagged ensemble without the conditioning event, and both events delivered with an explicitly unpaired schedule. Include a labeling control that cannot express the active opsin. These controls address intervention hardware, tagging, associative contingency, and the conditioning event separately.

Stage five performs acquisition while retaining a complete event log. Record the exact timing of context entry, optical commands, behavioral stimuli, and exits on a shared clock. Verify that actual optical output follows the command rather than assuming that a software timestamp proves delivery. Keep the observer and subsequent video scorer unaware of group identity where feasible. Avoid using behavior during this phase as the main evidence for the new association because immediate stimulation could alter movement. All departures from the planned sequence should enter a deviation log before group codes are revealed.

Stage six tests retention in A without optical stimulation and without the conditioning event. Also test a distinct neutral context, with order counterbalanced or with separate cohorts if repeated testing changes the memory. Prespecify whether testing in B is primary, secondary, or part of a separate cohort, because exposing the animal to B can itself alter subsequent behavior. The crucial comparison concerns the interaction between training contingency and test-context identity. A generalized increase across every context would support arousal or generalization more readily than a selective association involving the original representation.

Stage seven defines the behavioral measurement before inspecting condition means. Use a validated movement-based freezing classifier with a documented sampling rate, smoothing rule, minimum episode duration, and treatment of missing frames. Manually audit a blinded subset of videos spanning the full range of movement rather than only extreme examples. Record total movement, exploration, and overt motor capability as complementary outcomes. If possible, include a behaviorally distinct fear-related response in a separate confirmation experiment. Agreement between outcomes is stronger than relying on a single immobility threshold.

Stage eight specifies replication and analysis. The experimental unit for the central claim is the animal, not a video frame, tagged cell, or stimulation pulse. Multiple contexts or time windows within an animal are repeated observations. Estimate group contrasts and the contingency-by-context interaction with uncertainty, while accounting for cohort and repeated measures. Determine sample size from a prespecified smallest meaningful effect and variance information independent of the confirmatory outcomes. Report every assigned animal, loss, technical failure, and anatomical exclusion, with exclusion decisions made without seeing the primary behavioral result.

Stage nine tests mechanism more directly without confusing supportive evidence with necessity. A separate acquisition-specific disruption can ask whether activity in the labeled ensemble is required during the proposed association window. A separate retrieval-specific disruption can test later expression. These manipulations must have their own targeting and efficacy controls. They should not be combined into one uninterpretable permanent lesion. A selective effect on one phase could clarify the role of the ensemble, whereas a global behavioral deficit would not. Network measurements may identify candidate downstream regions but cannot establish synaptic routes merely from simultaneous activation.

The decision rule requires a coherent pattern: the contingent group must show a stronger response in A than the unpaired and inactive-intervention groups, while preserving general movement and showing meaningful discrimination from C. A result appearing only during illumination fails the acquisition claim. Equal responding in A and C leaves generalized fear unresolved. Failed targeting or absent physiological activation makes a negative behavioral result technically ambiguous. Conversely, successful targeting with an adequately precise null result constrains the proposed role under the tested conditions without proving that every possible pattern of activation would fail.

The supported conclusion would concern a learned association involving an experimentally activated contextual representation. It would not establish conscious experience, verbal report, the phenomenology of human false autobiographical memory, or a unique necessary circuit for all contextual learning. Other circuits may mediate expression, and activity-dependent labeling samples a population rather than identifying every relevant neuron. The reference answer is a proposed operational design with explicitly identified missing parameters. Its historical-direction score depends on the causal question, intervention timing, context-specific test, and discriminating prediction, not on naming a later paper or repeating its title.

A useful extension addresses ensemble overlap as a measured confound. Quantify how much the A-tagged population overlaps with activity elicited by B or C in a separately allocated validation cohort. High overlap would weaken the assumption that stimulation supplies a distinctive contextual cue. Keep image-registration methods and positivity thresholds fixed, and summarize overlap at the animal level. This measurement does not replace the contingency test; it evaluates an assumption on which that test depends. If overlap varies with targeting or handling, use that information to improve the next preregistered cohort rather than retrospectively excluding inconvenient animals.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Choose an approved activity-dependent tagging model and identify the targeted hippocampal population. Consult tagging-window, optical-activation and behavioral Methods before setting expression schedules or doses. If viral delivery is used, document construct identity, functional dose, spatial distribution and expression stability; pilot anatomy/physiology occur in separate or nonterminal cohorts, with final histology in study animals. Obtain the welfare-approved intervention details before execution; they cannot be derived from this packet.

Counterbalance physical contexts A, B and C. Tag a neutral A representation, close the labeling window, pair A-ensemble activation with the approved conditioning event in B, and test A later without either intervention. Define paired versus unpaired schedules prospectively, matching event counts and handling. Include inactive-tag/opsin-negative lit, activation-without-conditioning, conditioning-without-reactivation and context-specific controls. Quantify residual tagging in B in a separate validation cohort, because broad tagging undermines the internal-cue interpretation.

Calibrate actual optical output, targeting and spike recruitment with a bounded power/duty-cycle series; use the lowest validated activation protocol that passes health, heat and gross motor checks. Define a shared event clock and record actual delivery, not just commands. For behavior, define freezing as a validated immobility threshold/duration chosen on independent blinded videos, with frames/time in the denominator. Report freezing fraction, bout frequency, locomotion and missing-frame fraction per animal. Require agreement with an independent scorer under a predeclared reliability criterion, and add an orthogonal conditioned-response measure where feasible.

The primary interaction compares contingency and test context, accounting for animal/cohort and repeated tests. A selective retained response in A over C, absent in unpaired/inactive conditions, supports a learned artificial association. General immobility, equal A/C responses or an effect only during light does not. Test-order/extinction effects require counterbalancing or independent cohorts. Prespecify training, anatomical and recording eligibility before outcomes, retain behavioral nonresponders, and report all animals. Failed stimulation calls for a new calibration cohort; a technically valid null allows the biological hypothesis to fail. The conclusion concerns experimentally evoked contextual association, not human subjective false memory.
