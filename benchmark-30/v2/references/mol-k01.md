# mol-k01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

Observed evidence: the antibody detects a 50-kDa band and a 75-kDa band; loading is comparable; a validated biallelic deletion of the entire target coding sequence removes the 50-kDa band and leaves the 75-kDa band unchanged. The best-supported interpretation is that the 50-kDa band has genetic specificity support in this assay and that the 75-kDa band remains unassigned. The alternative claim is too strong: calling both bands proven target isoforms would require evidence that the 75-kDa species is also encoded by the target locus, such as a defined transcript variant or a mapped proteolytic product, yet the deletion removed the entire coding sequence and the 75-kDa signal survived. A full coding deletion is expected to eliminate the proteins that the locus encodes, so an unchanged band is evidence against, not for, assignment to that locus. The alternative claim confuses two different controls: equal loading establishes comparable sample amount and transfer, not antigen identity. Loading is a property of the lane, whereas specificity is a property of the antibody-antigen interaction, and a loading control cannot license a claim about which protein an antibody recognizes. The alternative claim is also unsupported: a validated biallelic deletion plus disappearance of the predicted 50-kDa band is the opposite of deletion failure, and a failed deletion would be expected to retain the 50-kDa signal. The residual 75-kDa signal may arise from another gene product, including a cross-reactive or modified species. A fragment or aggregate newly encoded by the fully deleted locus is not a viable explanation. The deeper principle is that genetic loss supports but does not prove specificity. A knockout experiment shows that a band depends on the target gene in that particular system; it does not prove that every signal the antibody produces is the target, and it does not transfer automatically to other assays such as immunofluorescence or immunohistochemistry, where fixation, permeabilization and epitope accessibility change the result. It also does not exclude compensation, an indirect effect on a different protein or expression from a separate locus; a truncated product from the deleted coding sequence is not a valid explanation for a confirmed complete coding deletion. Uncertainty here is real.  We are told loading is comparable but not that transfer, membrane lots and antibody incubations were uniform, and a quantified band is not a raw image, so saturation and background are unknown. Useful discriminating tests each carry a distinct prediction. An antibody against a different, non-overlapping epitope should detect the true target; if the 75-kDa band is target-derived it should also be recognized, and if it is cross-reactive it need not be. Immunogen peptide competition tests competition for antibody binding; it can also block cross-reactivity and does not independently identify a band. A second independent loss-of-function reagent, such as an independent deletion allele or an inducible degron, should reproduce loss of the 50-kDa band and should reduce the 75-kDa band as well only if that band is locus-derived. Epitope-tagged target expression should identify the cognate band by co-migration and anti-tag detection. Mass spectrometry of the excised 75-kDa region can identify proteins present there, but co-migration does not by itself identify the antibody-bound antigen. Target-null cells and genetic rescue add further constraints. If the 75-kDa band survives all target-specific perturbations, the cross-reactivity explanation gains support; if orthogonal assays seem to implicate the deleted target despite persistent signal in the verified full-coding null, resolve the discordance and possible indirect/co-migrating contributions before assigning an isoform. the 50-kDa assignment is supported within this western blot context, the 75-kDa band stays unassigned, and broader specificity claims require orthogonal validation. A disappearing cross-reactive protein downstream of the deleted gene could also mimic specificity. Rescue at controlled expression, an antibody against a separate epitope, and direct protein identification address different parts of this ambiguity; peptide competition alone is insufficient.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Before a validation run, consult the original antibody-validation Methods and lot-specific protocol, and verify transfer to the present species, epitope, lysate and detection system. Define the controls prospectively: the null tests target-gene dependence, an independent antibody tests an independent epitope, total protein tests loading/transfer, and no-primary/secondary-only lanes identify detection background. None alone proves molecular identity. Include parental, verified null, empty-vector null and near-endogenous rescue material in the same randomized blot run.

Quantify background-subtracted integrated intensity within a prespecified molecular-weight window divided by total-lane protein, retaining both numerator and denominator. Run a proposed twofold dilution series and multiple unsaturated exposures; accept a working range only when dilution recovery and residuals meet a prespecified tolerance, for example 15%, and no pixels are clipped. Values below the blank-derived quantification limit are reported as censored, not exact zero. Failed dilution linearity triggers lower loading or detection gain and a fresh calibration, not selection of a favorable exposure. Analyze independent cultures, retaining the culture-level knockout/rescue ratios and confidence intervals.

For identity validation, test a separate-epitope reagent and controlled rescue, then combine immunocapture, competition and mass-spectrometric protein identification with appropriate null and nonspecific-capture controls. An excised gel band can contain co-migrating proteins; identifying one protein there does not prove which antigen the antibody recognized. A rescued downstream cross-reactive protein can also return. Concordant target peptides, genetic dependence and independently validated recognition strengthen the 50-kDa assignment. Persistent 75-kDa binding in the complete coding-deletion line remains incompatible with assigning that signal to a newly synthesized product of the deleted coding region. If the apparent 50-kDa loss vanishes after valid background/linearity correction, revise the specificity claim. If rescue and peptide identification disagree, retain an unresolved assignment rather than calling both bands isoforms.
