# neu-p01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

Source identity and the evidential force of the notice are separate questions. On identity, the teaching archive certifies article record NS-12 by R. Vale, 'Projection P in learning', 2023, and the submitted citation matches every field. The source therefore matches an existing record within the supplied archive; it is not a nonexistent article and it is not a fabricated citation. The archive is authoritative only inside this teaching dossier, which bounds how far that verification extends. A retraction notice is also attached. The notice states that animal identities could not be reconciled across acquisition files. That is a specific reliability concern, and it is the reason the record is classed as retracted. The notice does not determine the truth of every biological claim in the paper, does not supply corrected group identities, and does not establish intent or misconduct. Reporting the notice means reporting exactly what it says, with its limits, rather than converting it into a global verdict about the projection. On the evidence, E1 reports lower learned performance after projection inhibition. E2 states that the allocation file and the raw animal identities needed to verify independent groups are unavailable. Together these mean the reported effect cannot be audited: without reliable animal assignment, independence of observations and the composition of the compared groups cannot be checked, so the reported sample size, degrees of freedom and uncertainty cannot be trusted from the summary alone. A small p-value cannot repair a missing allocation record, because the p-value is computed under assumptions about independence and group structure that the missing record is supposed to support. The correct inferential position is that the dossier provides insufficient evidence for the necessity claim, and that this insufficiency does not prove the biological claim false. Absence of trustworthy support is not affirmative evidence of no effect. Incorrect alternatives include saying that the retraction proves the projection has no biological role; that a retracted paper never existed; that the notice supplies corrected group identities; and that a significant summary statistic can substitute for auditable group assignment. Each of these overreads the available material. Discriminating next steps, proposed rather than performed, begin with recovering auditable identity and allocation records if they still exist, and with an independent, preregistered, blinded replication in which animals are the independent unit, groups are balanced and counterbalanced for order, the perturbation is validated for expression, anatomical spread and physiological suppression, and opsin-negative lit controls plus no-light trials are included. A necessity design should inhibit only during acquisition or only during retrieval according to the prespecified question, and should measure locomotion and cue detection so that a performance deficit can be attributed to the circuit rather than to motor or sensory side effects. Even a clean replication would support necessity under the tested conditions, not sufficiency, and not the claim that the projection stores a memory. The notice has a narrower procedural implication: the acquisition records for this study cannot currently establish that the compared groups were independent, so the result is unreliable as evidence until independent records or a new study resolve the question.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Before an independent test, consult the source's Methods and a validated projection-perturbation protocol, while separately auditing animal and allocation identities. Define positive perturbation-efficacy controls, negative opsin/vehicle controls and matched procedural/light controls before collection. The notice establishes the documented record-integrity failure inside this dossier; it establishes neither a fictional citation nor intentional misconduct.

For audit recovery, preserve original acquisition identifiers, dates, hashes and allocation documents, reconcile them without using the behavioral outcome to infer identity, and report any unresolved animal mapping. For replication, assign independent animals through concealed randomized blocks and retain all assigned animals in an accounting table. Separate inhibition during acquisition from inhibition during retrieval; a later no-intervention retention test is essential for a learning claim. Measure behavior as correct choices or conditioned-response frequency per eligible trial, with animal-level intervals and explicit timeout/missing-video denominators. Validate a blinded scoring subset against independent observers and freeze thresholds before treatment decoding. A proposed disagreement tolerance, set during pilot validation, triggers rescoring or a classifier revision on new validation records rather than selectively correcting one group.

Quantify neural suppression at the relevant site with recovery and spread checks. Also measure cue detection, locomotion, motivation and light detection. Anatomical targeting is insufficient if the manipulated neurons have collateral outputs. Use an orthogonal low-motor-demand behavioral measure or a separately validated intervention to test whether a deficit generalizes beyond one artifact-prone assay. A retrieval-only motor deficit cannot establish impaired acquisition. Reconciled source records could disprove the specific group-identity concern; a new clean replication could support the biological claim without repairing the old records. A precise null with verified targeting constrains necessity under that protocol, whereas failed suppression or wide uncertainty leaves the question open.
