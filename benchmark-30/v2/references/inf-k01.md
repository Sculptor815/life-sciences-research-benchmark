# inf-k01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The item reports a well-controlled genome-wide association result: a lead noncoding SNP near gene G is associated with the trait, and several variants in linkage disequilibrium span both G and H. No functional or colocalization data are supplied. The justified conclusion is the locus is associated, and identifying the causal variant and the effector gene requires further evidence. The claim that G is necessarily causal because it is closest, confuses physical proximity with mechanism. Noncoding variants can act on distant genes through chromatin contacts, enhancers and promoters, and the nearest transcript need not be the target. The claim that the lead SNP must be the only causal variant, ignores linkage disequilibrium. The lead SNP is the most strongly associated marker in the sample, but it is a statistical marker for a haplotype; several correlated variants can carry the same association signal, and any of them, or a combination, could be causal. The claim that whole-genome sequencing alone proves the mechanism, confuses variant discovery with functional evidence. Sequencing can find and genotype variants; it cannot show which one changes a regulatory element or which gene is affected. The key principle is that genetic association through linkage disequilibrium is not causality. Three claims should be kept distinct: the locus is associated; a specific causal variant exists and can be nominated; and a specific effector gene and mechanism explain the association. The supplied evidence supports only the first. Uncertainty is substantial. The lead SNP may be a proxy whose true causal variant is badly genotyped or imputed. Fine-mapping depends on the reference panel matching the study population, and mismatch can misplace credible sets. Multiple independent signals may exist at the locus, and conditional analysis is needed to separate them. Ancestry-specific linkage disequilibrium means a signal that maps one way in one population may map differently in another, and winner's curse can inflate the lead effect. Useful discriminating analyses, proposed rather than performed, include conditional association analysis to test for independent signals; statistical fine-mapping with a matched reference panel to produce credible sets; colocalization between the trait association and expression or protein quantitative trait loci in relevant tissues; functional annotation of credible variants, including chromatin state, transcription-factor motifs and allele-specific reporter assays; chromatin conformation data to link candidate elements to promoters; and, most directly, perturbation of the candidate variant in an isogenic system using base editing or CRISPR with appropriate controls. Replication in an independent, ancestry-appropriate cohort strengthens any nominated variant. Each test has a distinct prediction. If a single variant drives the signal, conditioning on the correctly measured causal variant should remove that signal under a correctly specified model; strong linkage can still leave a broad credible set. An expression-mediated G mechanism predicts informative, context-matched expression/trait and perturbation evidence; an undetected eQTL is not by itself proof against every possible G mechanism. If the effector is H or another distal gene, those same tests should point elsewhere. the supplied evidence establishes a locus association, the linked variants prevent assigning causality to the lead SNP, and G cannot be declared the causal gene from distance or from sequencing alone.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Before analysis, consult the primary fine-mapping and colocalization Methods and current versioned tool documentation to verify model assumptions, allele conventions and parameter meanings. Freeze the genomic build, variant identifiers, effect allele, reference-allele frequency and ancestry-matched LD source. The packet itself supplies none of the numerical association data needed to execute this plan.

Define credible sets and posterior inclusion probabilities conditional on a specified model; a nominal 95% credible set is not guaranteed frequentist 95% coverage in every misspecified setting. Check effect/LD alignment, sample overlap, imputation quality and whether multiple association signals are supported. For a one-causal-variant colocalization model, compare evidence for two distinct causal variants with a shared variant and recognize that multiple signals can violate the assumption; condition or use a suitable multi-signal method when justified. Explore a prespecified range of priors and LD/reference choices and report whether the gene nomination changes. Neither a shared association signal nor a chromatin contact proves that expression mediates disease.

For validation, define an independent cohort association contrast and, separately, an isogenic allelic perturbation in a biologically relevant cell system. Predefine expression and functional endpoints, independent clones/cultures, no-edit controls, verified on-target genotype and an orthogonal rescue. Avoid choosing tissue solely because its eQTL best matches the desired gene. A nominated G allele that changes G expression and the relevant phenotype, with a selective rescue and alternative H measurements, strengthens the effector model but does not remove every pleiotropic route. A well-powered perturbation that leaves the predicted endpoint unchanged can weaken that specific mechanism; low edit efficiency or an irrelevant cell state makes a null inconclusive. Report linkage uncertainty, model sensitivity and unsupported causal steps explicitly instead of upgrading the nearest gene to a conclusion.
