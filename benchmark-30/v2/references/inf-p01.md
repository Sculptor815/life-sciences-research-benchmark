# inf-p01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

Two conclusions must be separated: whether the citation is a real source, and whether the analysis supports a causal pathway. The source question is answered unusually clearly. C2 is a signed provenance record stating that record WG-44 was intentionally invented for a training exercise and was never a journal article. That is affirmative evidence that the citation is fabricated for teaching, not merely unverifiable. It is not an accusation against a real person: the names, journal and identifier belong to the synthetic dossier, and the fabrication is of the record, not a claim about researchers. Because the record was invented, a journal correction or retraction state is not applicable; asking for one would be a category error. The supplied title, with its phrase about proof, should not be allowed to imply that the source carries demonstrative force. The analysis question is separate and fails on its own terms. E1 says cases and controls were sequenced at different centers and have different ancestry distributions. This makes sequencing center, ancestry and case status entangled, and when they are completely confounded no statistical adjustment can restore the missing comparison. Regression, principal components, batch correction and integration can reduce measured imbalance, but they cannot manufacture observations in the missing cells of the design; complete batch confounding is not fixed by regression or integration. E2 compounds the problem: the burden test uses variants passing center-specific filters, and coverage and relatedness are not reported. Center-specific filtering means cases and controls may have been evaluated over different variant sets, so a burden difference can reflect differential callability rather than disease biology. Unreported relatedness threatens the independence assumptions of the test and can inflate significance. E3 adds an enrichment problem: enrichment used all annotated genes rather than genes that could actually pass the study's filters. The background for an enrichment test must be the tested or testable feature universe; using an inappropriate all-gene background can bias significance in either direction and can make an artifactual set look enriched. Given all of this, the dossier does not support a causal pathway claim. Even a robust burden association, properly analyzed, would establish a statistical association that would still need functional and independent evidence before it could be called causal, and burden tests cannot by themselves establish direction or molecular mechanism. Several incorrect alternatives recur. Treating a plausible title as evidence of a real paper is backwards. Treating a smaller burden p-value as evidence that sequencing-center bias is gone confuses statistical significance with design validity. Treating enrichment as establishing causation reverses the inferential order. Inferring deliberate misconduct by a real named researcher from a synthetic record exceeds the evidence and misreads the dossier. Discriminating next steps, proposed rather than performed, are an independent, ancestry-balanced cohort with harmonized joint calling and comparable coverage across groups; relatedness-aware mixed models; restriction of the analysis to matched callable regions with sensitivity analyses; a prespecified enrichment universe with a dated gene-set release and negative-control gene sets; and, only after a robust association is established, targeted functional experiments such as perturbation of candidate genes in relevant models. If the association survives harmonization and balanced sampling, the pathway hypothesis gains support. If the estimate materially attenuates after those corrections with adequate precision, a technical explanation gains support. Loss of significance alone may instead reflect reduced sample size or precision. the citation is a known synthetic fabrication that should be labelled as such without implicating real people, and independently of that, the analysis cannot support a causal pathway claim.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Before reanalysis, consult the original burden-test and enrichment Methods and versioned software documentation, verify callable-region definitions and specify which assumptions the model requires. The dossier's signed record already establishes the citation was invented for teaching. No live search is needed to overturn that supplied provenance, and no real-person allegation is justified.

At the data level, reconstruct sample/center/ancestry/treatment cross-tables, relatedness and per-sample coverage before fitting a model. Define the quantitative endpoint as a prespecified qualifying-variant burden with its tested region and callable denominator, an effect estimate and uncertainty. Document variant annotation, allele-frequency source, genotype-quality rule and relatedness handling. Compare groups over jointly callable regions under harmonized processing. Complete center-by-status confounding blocks a separately identified disease effect; a new balanced replication or balanced resequencing is required for that contrast. Adding covariates to a rank-deficient design cannot solve it.

For enrichment, fix the universe to genes that could have qualified under the same assays and filters, use versioned gene sets and handle gene length, mutability and coverage where relevant. Report odds ratios or an appropriate enrichment statistic with uncertainty and a declared multiplicity family, alongside null analyses preserving the relevant center/ancestry/allocation structure. Unrestricted label permutation is invalid if labels are not exchangeable. Test sensitivity to callable thresholds without selecting the setting with the smallest p-value. Validate in independently collected, ancestry-appropriate samples, with the same prespecified variant and pathway definitions.

Persistence after appropriate balanced sampling and callability controls supports association, not direct pathway causation. Loss of significance alone does not identify the original bias: compare effect estimates, intervals, effective sample size and measurement changes. If a harmonized design attenuates the effect while preserving precision and control sensitivity, a technical explanation gains support. If the new data remain sparse or confounded, the concern is unresolved. Functional perturbation is a further mechanistic stage, not something enrichment has already demonstrated.
