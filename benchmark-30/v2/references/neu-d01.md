# neu-d01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

Proposed design. All procedures below are proposed; none has been performed. The question is whether projection P to Q is necessary during cue retrieval in a learned choice task, while addressing movement and sensory confounds. O1 states that P projects to Q and to another region, so P is not a single-target population; O2 states that an inhibitory opsin can be targeted to P neurons projecting to Q; O3 states that correct choices are the proposed outcome but the task requires locomotion; O4 states that light delivery can be visible to the animal. Necessity here means that suppressing the P-to-Q projection during the retrieval window reduces correct performance under conditions where alternative explanations are controlled. It does not mean that the projection stores the memory, and it does not establish sufficiency. Observed evidence versus assumptions: O1 through O4 are design constraints, not results. The assumption that the targeted opsin reaches only Q-projecting P neurons requires verification, because soma-targeted inhibition of P would suppress all P outputs, including the collateral projection to the other region, and would therefore not isolate P-to-Q. Terminal inhibition in Q is more projection-selective but can affect fibers of passage and can change local Q excitability through presynaptic release, so both strategies carry different confounds and the choice must be prespecified. Preparation, identity and quality control. Proposed steps: define the P and Q region identities and coordinates from the author's atlas and prior tracing, since coordinates cannot be assumed; verify opsin expression and targeting with histology in every animal or in a reported subset, quantifying overlap between opsin-positive somata and retrogradely labeled Q-projecting neurons; verify physiological suppression with ex vivo or in vivo recordings showing reduced activity under the light protocol; confirm that the viral strategy does not label neighboring structures; and confirm behavioral competence, including cue detection and reward consumption, before the main sessions. Experimental unit, allocation and blinding. The independent unit is the animal. Trials and sessions are repeated measures within animals. Proposed allocation: counterbalance light and no-light trials within session in a randomized interleaved schedule, counterbalance session order across animals, blind the experimenter and the outcome coder to condition, and prespecify the primary contrast as accuracy during the retrieval window. Sample size should be calibrated rather than assumed: run a pilot with a small number of animals, estimate the variance of the animal-level accuracy difference, and compute the number of animals required for 80 percent power at a two-sided 0.05 level for the prespecified within-animal contrast; if the pilot variance makes the design infeasible, report that limit instead of asserting a number. Intervention and schedule. The opsin, promoter, viral titer, light wavelength, power, pulse pattern and duration are unknown and must come from the author. Proposed calibration first: in the targeted preparation, measure the minimum light power that produces reliable suppression in the physiological assay, using a power meter at the fiber tip and monitoring for heating and tissue damage, then use the lowest effective power and a matched control protocol in behavior. Proposed schedule: habituate animals to the tether and to light delivery without opsin before training; train to a prespecified performance criterion; then run test sessions with light delivered only during the retrieval window, with the window defined behaviorally by cue onset and choice initiation and reported in milliseconds. Controls. Proposed controls include opsin-negative lit littermates receiving identical light; within-animal no-light trials interleaved in the same session; light-only trials in the absence of the cue to test whether light itself is a usable cue given O4; a control opsin or fluorophore-only group; a group with opsin in a non-targeted population to test anatomical specificity; and counterbalanced light delivery so that light is not confounded with a fixed side, trial position or reward schedule. Because O4 warns that light can be visible, shielding the fiber, using a longer-wavelength opsin, or explicitly measuring light detection in a separate task are proposed mitigations. Ordered measurements. Proposed order: expression and targeting verification; physiological suppression verification; light-cue detection check; habituation sessions; training to criterion; test sessions with interleaved light and no-light trials; concurrent locomotion measurement, including speed, distance and trial-initiation latency; cue detection and reward-consumption measures; and, if deficits appear, an independent low-motor-demand variant of the task. Analysis and decision rules. Analyze trial-level accuracy with a mixed-effects logistic model including light condition as a within-animal factor and animal-level random intercepts, with planned contrasts and confidence intervals; analyze reaction time and locomotion as secondary outcomes; report animal-level effect estimates. Decision rules: a selective retrieval-window deficit in accuracy with preserved locomotion, cue detection and reward consumption, absent in opsin-negative lit controls and absent in light-only trials, supports necessity under the tested conditions. If performance falls together with movement or cue detection, the deficit cannot be attributed to retrieval, and the session or task must be redesigned or a mediation analysis with explicit assumptions used. If no effect appears, check opsin expression, targeting, light power and suppression efficacy before concluding that the projection is unnecessary; a null result with unverified suppression is inconclusive. Troubleshooting. Proposed checks: if suppression fails physiologically, retarget or increase expression and recalibrate power; if movement is reduced, lower light power, shorten the pulse, or use terminal inhibition with the caveats noted; if animals appear to detect the light, shield delivery or switch opsin; if harmful effects or heating occur, stop under the approved protocol and redesign/calibrate in a separate pilot; a wavelength change is not itself a guarantee against heating; if performance is at ceiling or floor, adjust task difficulty before the main test. Limitations. Necessity in a retrieval window does not establish sufficiency, does not show where a memory is stored, and does not exclude a purely performance-related role; P has a collateral projection, so projection specificity depends entirely on the targeting strategy; terminal inhibition has its own confounds; and all parameter values require author input or calibration.  Operational clarification: expression and suppression checks before behavior use a separate pilot or nonterminal recordings; verify anatomical spread in the actual study animals by postmortem histology after testing. Terminal illumination does not automatically isolate synaptic transmission: validate axonal and local physiological effects, collateral recruitment and possible rebound responses with the chosen inhibitory tool. Red-shifting or lowering power does not by itself eliminate visual detection or heating, so repeat matched control measurements. Any parameter change prompted by a failed pilot must be frozen before the confirmatory phase; do not retune it selectively after observing the primary behavioral result. Distinguish motor mediation from a parallel effect and do not assume conditioning on post-treatment speed identifies a causal retrieval effect.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Select an approved animal/atlas preparation and explicitly target Q terminals of the verified Q-projecting P population; soma inhibition alone cannot isolate the collateralized P-to-Q route. Identify the opsin, targeting strategy and construct from validated Methods before surgery. Use pilot/nonterminal physiology before behavior, and postmortem histology for every study animal under a blinded anatomical rule. If viral delivery is used, record functional titer, volume and total delivered dose, target spread, expression interval and toxicity. Species, target regions and tool identity are unresolved in this abstract task, so no universal coordinates or light dose are defensible.

Calibrate measured optical output in mW, irradiance where estimated, wavelength, pulse duration/duty cycle and cue-relative onset in milliseconds. Select the lowest protocol that reliably suppresses terminal transmission in a defined physiological contrast, while measuring local Q effects, collateral recruitment, rebound and heat. A proposed pilot target such as at least 80% suppression must be justified against the behavioral question and confidence interval; it is not a historical result. Reject a tool/protocol that cannot meet specificity and physiological criteria; do not simply increase power indefinitely.

Define retrieval-window accuracy as correct completed choices divided by eligible trials, with omissions, reaction time and locomotion reported separately per animal. Test a blinded classifier on an independent video set and log dropped frames. Use an opsin-by-light contrast with matched visible-light/heat controls, no-light and off-window conditions. Light-only trials without a task cannot substitute for a full matched cue task. Confirm with a low-motor-demand response variant and a separately validated perturbation. A deficit accompanied by motor or sensory failure does not identify retrieval; regressing out post-treatment speed cannot automatically repair that causal ambiguity.
