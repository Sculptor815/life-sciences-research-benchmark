# bio-d03: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

All experiments below are proposed; the 1937 methods and figures are not reconstructed. The goal is to distinguish catalytic recycling of a candidate citrate-cycle intermediate from three alternatives: a pool-concentration effect, stimulation of respiration alone, and a non-cycling activation or bypass. The design uses orthogonal readouts: carbon-isotope tracking, carbon balance, step-specific inhibition followed by validated washout or missing-enzyme restoration, initial pool measurement, and defined reconstitution.

Preparation and QC. Prepare tissue-derived oxidation material from independent biological samples under a single calibrated protocol. Before any intermediate is added, measure the initial endogenous pools of the candidate intermediates and the baseline oxidation rate. If the available preparation contains undefined components, first characterize the baseline carbon balance. QC should verify that the preparation is active, that the substrate is consumed, and that the isotope-labelled substrate is incorporated into the expected products. Because exact intermediates, isotope labels, inhibitors and activities are not supplied, the protocol must specify and validate them before the main experiment. If the candidate intermediate is not chemically pure, purity must be measured and a mock impurity control prepared.

Experimental unit, allocation and blinding. The inferential unit is an independent biological preparation or independent tissue sample, not an individual assay well. Randomize assay wells and positions to conditions with a prespecified allocation table. Blind the person running the assay and the person analysing isotope and carbon-balance data to whether the well received a catalytic amount, a stoichiometric pool amount, vehicle, active inhibitor or inactive inhibitor. Use at least two independent preparations for discovery and a separate set for confirmation.

Handling and analysis sequence. First measure the baseline without added intermediate. Then add the candidate intermediate at a catalytic amount and at one or more stoichiometric pool amounts; the catalytic amount must be calibrated to be small relative to the substrate. In parallel, run a no-added-substrate control, a vehicle control, and a heat-inactivated or otherwise inactive preparation control. Next, pulse the isotope-labelled substrate and follow both the label and total carbon over time. For the cycling claim, track label incorporation into the candidate intermediate and the downstream products, then chase. In a separate arm, apply a stepwise blockade that interrupts the candidate cycle, then restore the blocked reaction and test sustained recovery; downstream metabolite addition is a separate bypass control. Run the same blockade with an inactive analog or without substrate. To separate respiration alone from cycle flux, measure oxygen consumption or an equivalent respiration readout in the same wells, but do not treat it as sufficient evidence of recycling.

The primary quantitative contrast is the oxidation flux and product yield supported by a catalytic amount of intermediate versus the same amount of a non-cycling pool equivalent, after accounting for the initial endogenous pool. The second contrast is label movement from substrate into the candidate intermediate and downstream products in the catalytic arm versus controls. The third is recovery of flux after restoration of the blocked reaction. Carbon balance is a gate: if the measured products and remaining substrate do not account for the added carbon within a prespecified recovery window, no cycle conclusion is made.

Controls. Include no added substrate, vehicle, a stoichiometric pool addition, a heat-inactivated preparation, an inactive structural analog, blockade without intermediate, blockade with intermediate, and a defined reconstituted system containing the key components if available. Measure initial pools in every preparation. Include a respiration-only control in which oxygen consumption is stimulated without label flow through the candidate intermediate. If no defined reconstitution is feasible, state that the design cannot establish closure and treat it as a limitation.

Time and dose calibration. Calibrate the linear window for oxidation and for label incorporation. Calibrate the catalytic dose as the amount that is small relative to substrate and to measured endogenous pools, and the pool dose as the amount that materially changes total pool size. Calibrate the blockade strength so that it inhibits the candidate step without collapsing all oxidation; if the inhibitor is not reversible, use a separate recovery system. If no calibration is possible, specify the required pilot inputs.

Sample-size and power planning. Power independent biological preparations for the catalytic-versus-pool contrast and for the label-flow contrast. Estimate variance from a pilot; if unavailable, prespecify a calibration pilot and a minimum detectable flux or label difference. Do not use assay wells as independent replicates.

Prespecified exclusions and stopping rules. Exclude preparations with poor baseline activity, carbon recovery outside the prespecified window, contamination, or unstable isotope background. Redesign labeling and quantify pool dilution if the endogenous pool obscures the catalytic addition, if the blockade does not inhibit the target step, or if label incorporation is not measurable. Do not exclude a preparation because it lacks the expected effect.

Missing data. Report failed assays and lost preparations. If isotope measurements are missing for a preparation, do not impute a cycle conclusion. Use prespecified handling for samples below detection.

Expected results. A positive cycle-supporting result would show that a catalytic amount of the intermediate increases oxidation and product formation more than a matched non-cycling pool, that isotope label moves through the candidate intermediate into downstream products, that stepwise blockade interrupts this flow, and that restoring the blocked reaction recovers sustained flux within carbon balance. A negative result would show no label flow or no recovery beyond pool effects. An ambiguous result would show increased oxygen consumption without label flow, label flow without catalytic enhancement, or carbon imbalance.

Artifact discrimination. Initial pool measurement addresses pre-existing tissue substrate. Defined reconstitution and enzyme purity checks address contamination. The pool-addition control addresses concentration effects. The respiration control addresses stimulation of respiration alone. The inactive analog addresses nonspecific activation. Stepwise blockade with recovery addresses bypass and allosteric stimulation.

Troubleshooting. If no catalytic effect is seen, verify intermediate purity, dose calibration and baseline pool size. If oxygen consumption rises without label flow, test whether the intermediate is activating respiration or a bypass. If blockade does not recover, verify inhibitor specificity and reversibility. If carbon recovery is poor, improve product separation before interpreting the cycle.

Conditional conclusion. Only if catalytic intermediate addition, isotope flow, step-specific inhibition followed by validated washout or missing-enzyme restoration, and carbon balance all support regeneration of the candidate intermediate should the data support a closed cycle. If the effects are explained by pool size, respiration stimulation, bypass activation or contamination, the result does not establish cycling. The 1937 figures and full text are not available here, so this is a proposed forward design, not a verification of the historical record.

Curator clarification and operational extension. Restoring a blocked cycle requires restoration of the missing reaction. Adding an intermediate downstream of an irreversible block can transiently feed respiration without closing the cycle. Keep this bypass arm separate and test whether activity persists beyond the supplied downstream carbon. Propose a validated isolated-mitochondrial or defined-enzyme preparation, specify a carbon-entry substrate and complementary tracer positions, and inventory oxygen, substrate and cofactor requirements. Use rapid quenching with internal standards, extraction blanks, isotope-purity correction, matrix-matched calibration and recovery controls. Collect densely spaced pilot times, then freeze a sampling schedule spanning initial labeling and several estimated turnovers. Quantify both concentrations and isotopologue distributions of all accessible intermediates, carbon dioxide and major side products. Fit competing cyclic, branched and exchange-only flux models with independently measured uptake/release constraints and identifiability analysis. Label appearing in one intermediate does not prove regeneration; isotope exchange can occur without net flux. Pool dilution is a modeled quantity, not a reason to discard inconvenient tissue preparations. A non-cycling analog cannot be assumed available: use omission and restoration of a defined reaction where chemical analog validation is lacking. Restrict closure claims to the measured reaction system; physiological tissues can run branched or cataplerotic fluxes.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Select an identifiable isolated mitochondrial preparation and a complementary defined-enzyme reconstitution. A defensible proposed tracer route is carbon entry from labeled pyruvate/acetyl units into a defined citrate-cycle scheme, with positional information chosen from explicit atom mapping. Include initial oxaloacetate/citrate and other accessible pools. Verify that the preparation transports and oxidizes the chosen substrate; otherwise a negative tracer result is not a cycle test. Obtain enzyme/cofactor requirements and validated inhibitor specificity from Methods before setting doses.

For pilot sampling, use a geometric early-time schedule spanning mixing dead time to several estimated turnovers, then freeze times based on measured labeling dynamics. Add a small candidate intermediate amount relative to measured available substrate carbon, not an assumed endogenous pool. Quantify concentrations and isotopologue fractions with isotope-labeled internal standards, extraction blanks and natural-abundance/purity correction. Record nmol pools, nmol carbon/time flux, oxygen consumption and evolved carbon dioxide; check a proposed 90-110% carbon-recovery window only if independently measured assay precision supports it. Below-limit metabolites produce explicit uncertainty in closure, not zero pools.

Compare intact, omitted-step and restored-step systems and a validated reversible inhibitor/washout arm. Verify actual inhibitor removal and enzyme recovery; downstream-metabolite add-back is a bypass control that cannot close a permanently blocked step. Fit cyclic, branched and exchange-only models with measured uptake/product constraints and inspect flux identifiability. Define the primary contrast as sustained turnover and regeneration beyond available starting pool/bypass carbon after accounting for side products. A rise in respiration or isotope exchange alone fails that criterion. A failed carbon balance triggers extraction/gas-recovery diagnostics; genuine branched output is retained as biology. Do not exclude preparations solely for rejecting the closed-cycle model.
