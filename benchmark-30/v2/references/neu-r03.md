# neu-r03: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The unresolved question is whether neural activity represents uncertainty about an outcome separately from its expected value and from a brief prediction error. The original packet describes responses around unexpectedly delivered or omitted rewards. A new experiment can vary cue-conditioned reward probability and examine activity during anticipation separately from cue and outcome transients. A useful hypothesis predicts a nonmonotonic uncertainty-related signal, whereas a value signal should track expected reward under matched outcome magnitudes. These are competing interpretations to test, not results already present in the packet.

Specify an approved behavioral preparation, neural recording method and objective criteria for identifying the relevant neuron population. If physiological features are used without definitive molecular identification, preserve that uncertainty in the claim. Establish reward delivery calibration, cue timing, eye or body tracking and synchronized acquisition clocks. Verify delivered reward volume or magnitude on independent test trials. Record behavioral engagement, licking and movement so that motor preparation and arousal can be considered rather than assigned to neural uncertainty by default.

Train distinct counterbalanced cues associated with a probability grid that includes zero, one and intermediate values. Keep the nominal reward magnitude and cue-to-outcome interval fixed for the primary probability series. Randomize trial order within blocks and ensure enough trials to estimate both rewarded and unrewarded outcomes at intermediate probabilities. Verify learning with independent behavioral measures and model trial history. Do not exclude slow-learning subjects after inspecting the neural effect; define training and analysis rules prospectively.

A fixed-magnitude probability series changes expected value and variance in related but nonidentical ways: mean is pm and variance is m^2 p(1-p). These functions are not globally linearly collinear. Complementary distributions and behavioral controls strengthen discrimination beyond a fixed-magnitude series. Add a prespecified complementary condition set that matches expected value across different reward distributions, with practical probability and magnitude combinations. Avoid relying only on a visually distinctive cue at probability one half. Counterbalance cue identities across subjects, include familiar sensory controls and check salience. Matching expected value may alter magnitude-related sensory or motivational effects, so compare multiple matched pairs and report remaining nonidentifiability.

Record baseline, cue onset, anticipation, expected outcome time and post-outcome activity in every trial. Choose analysis windows from independent pilot data or timing rules, not the largest observed difference. Quantify phasic cue responses, outcome prediction-error responses and sustained or ramping delay activity separately. Keep rewarded and unrewarded intermediate-probability trials in the anticipatory analysis; splitting only on a later outcome can produce selection artifacts. Track movements and licks with the same clock and examine whether neural differences precede or simply follow them.

Fit explicit models containing expected reward, candidate uncertainty measures, recent outcomes, trial number and measured behavior, allowing appropriate subject and neuron dependence. Compare predictive performance on held-out blocks or sessions. Probability one half maximizes Bernoulli outcome variance for fixed magnitude, but several uncertainty constructs can yield related patterns; distinguish a particular model from a general uncertainty label. Correlated regressors can make coefficients unstable even when a model fits well. Report uncertainty, alternative model comparisons and their sensitivity to behavioral covariates.

Prespecify the primary nonmonotonic anticipatory contrast, such as intermediate probability against endpoints, together with monotonic value-related contrasts. Base sample-size planning on independent subjects and sessions with nested neurons, not solely on thousands of trials. Plan exclusions for failed timing, unstable recordings and failed reward delivery before group decoding. Preserve all valid neurons selected by the independent population-identification rule. Missing behavior tracking limits confound adjustment and must be reported rather than imputed from the neural trace.

A strong supportive outcome is delay activity highest around uncertain intermediate outcomes, replicated across cue identities and informative value-matched comparisons, while phasic responses follow prediction-related patterns. This supports separable neural correlates under the tested conditions. It does not establish that the activity causes learning, risk preference or attention. A causal extension would require a temporally restricted perturbation and controls for movement, motivation and sensory processing, with a behavioral prediction specified in advance. If the apparent uncertainty signal disappears with appropriate timing or cue controls, revise the mechanism rather than selecting a favorable recording subset.

The published historical-target Fiorillo, Tobler and Schultz paper reports a gradual delay-period response covarying with outcome uncertainty and distinguishes it from phasic responses. A qualified hit requires the uncertainty-versus-value question, probability manipulation, temporally separated neural readouts and a prediction distinguishing intermediate probabilities from endpoints. A name, a citation or a generic proposal to study dopamine is insufficient. Scientifically rigorous alternative next questions can receive quality points without a historical hit. The proposed controls exceed the narrow historical summary and are not attributed to unreviewed author methods.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Choose an approved preparation with an identifiable dopamine-neuron recording strategy and read the reward-delivery, population-identification and behavioral Methods. Quantify delivery magnitude, event timestamps, recording stability and identification error before testing uncertainty. Define a fixed-magnitude primary probability grid p=0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 with a common delay, counterbalanced cues and interleaved randomized trials. This is a proposed grid, not a reconstructed author protocol. Retain omitted outcomes at their expected time; the packet predicts depression there, so an omission-activation requirement would reject a valid preparation.

For fixed magnitude m, mean reward is pm and variance is m^2 p(1-p). Expected value and variance are related across this grid but are not globally linearly collinear: symmetric probabilities have equal variance and different means. Add separately prespecified equal-mean distributions to challenge a value-only account. When magnitude varies to match the mean, the amount variance is not generally maximal at p=0.5; compute each distribution explicitly. Distinguish occurrence uncertainty, amount variance, entropy and subjective learning uncertainty instead of calling them interchangeable.

Define baseline, cue, delay and outcome windows from task timing and independent pilot physiology. Report baseline-corrected spikes/s and a preregistered intermediate-versus-endpoint delay contrast, separately from phasic cue/outcome effects. Quantify licking, movement, pupil/engagement and trial history with synchronized measurements. Compare value and uncertainty models on held-out sessions, with subject and neuron dependence and identifiability checks. Covariate adjustment is a sensitivity analysis, not proof that all motor mediation is removed. Use behavioral-control conditions and temporal dissociations where feasible.

Set minimum usable trials and population/recording eligibility before seeing the neural contrast; include all independently identified neurons, including nonresponses. Quantify uncertainty across subjects/sessions rather than only trials. If cue salience or licking explains the pattern, revise the mechanism and test new counterbalanced sessions. A robust intermediate-peaked fixed-magnitude delay signal with informative value-matched controls supports a separable correlate under that design, not causal necessity for learning. A model failing to find a signal with low precision does not establish absence.
