# bio-r02: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The next question is how ATP turnover couples to elementary rotation: is motion made of reproducible angular steps separated by chemical waits, and does each mechanical event correspond to a nucleotide-dependent transition? The original rotation movie shows a motor, but cannot resolve this coupling or establish efficiency. Competing explanations include continuous rotation blurred by the reporter, discrete chemical stepping, and apparent pauses caused by surface contacts or probe drag. A proposed single-molecule experiment should manipulate ATP availability and mechanical load independently.

Specify the F1 construct, immobilization geometry and reporter attachment sites, then validate bulk ATPase activity before and after labeling. Confirm that the chosen attachment reports the central rotating subunit rather than nonspecific surface motion. Use independent enzyme and flow-cell preparations. Record the fraction of immobilized molecules that are inactive, irregular or trackable; analyzing only attractive rotating molecules biases the population description. Inactive enzyme, ATP-free buffer and nonspecific attachment controls help establish the motion background.

Calibrate angle estimation using a known geometric or driven-motion reference, camera timing, exposure duration and localization noise. Measure photobleaching, drift and tracking errors under the intended illumination. Verify that frame rate and reporter response can resolve the predicted dwell duration. Pilot ATP concentrations from a regime with long resolvable waits to a faster regime, while holding other buffer components fixed. Record free magnesium and nucleotide composition where relevant; nominal ATP alone may not define substrate availability. Do not claim a particular historical concentration from the supplied packet.

Prepare a reporter-load series with measured geometry. Shorter/lighter probes reduce drag but can reduce angular precision; larger probes improve tracking yet may limit speed or distort steps. Randomize measurement order across ATP and reporter conditions and blind trajectory analysis. Image sufficiently long to observe repeated cycles without selecting duration based on a desired step pattern. Use predefined tracking quality and surface-contact rules. Where possible observe the same molecule across controlled solution changes, while retaining an independent-preparation comparison to detect drift or irreversible loss.

Retain raw image sequences, localization outputs and time stamps. Estimate angles and unwrap rotations using a method tested on synthetic trajectories with known steps and on a continuous-motion null. Fit candidate step-and-dwell models and continuous-motion models without fixing the answer to 120 degrees. Assess missed steps, merged waits and apparent backward motion caused by localization noise. Compare inference across frame rates and independent algorithms. A peak created by a three-state prior is not evidence for three catalytic steps.

Estimate angular increments, dwell-time distributions, rotation speed and backward-event frequency at each ATP concentration and load. Under an ATP-binding-limited model, waits should shorten with increasing ATP over the appropriate range while a characteristic angular increment remains. A load-dependent transit time should be distinguished from a chemistry-dependent waiting time. Fit explicit kinetic alternatives and examine residuals and parameter identifiability. Histograms alone cannot show that every wait corresponds to exactly one ATP molecule; compare the mechanochemical model with independently measured turnover where feasible.

For energetics, calibrate rotational drag or applied torque in the actual geometry, including proximity to the surface and reporter shape. Integrate torque over angular displacement to estimate mechanical work with propagated uncertainty. Estimate ATP-hydrolysis free energy using the actual nucleotide and phosphate activities, temperature and solution conditions, not a universal textbook number. Distinguish work per step, dissipated work, average mechanical power and total chemical consumption. Near equality under one load does not establish perfect efficiency at all loads, nor ATP synthesis during reverse rotation.

Use molecule as a nested unit within enzyme/flow-cell preparation. Prespecify a primary test of ATP-dependent dwell times and a separate test of angular-step consistency across valid loads. Base independent preparation numbers and molecule counts on a precision target with between-preparation variability. Report all exclusion reasons, raw traces and sensitivity to step-detection thresholds. If steps disappear only at low frame rate, improve acquisition rather than concluding continuity. If pauses track reporter size or surface position, investigate mechanical hindrance. If both ATP and load vary together, the experiment cannot separate chemical from mechanical limitations.

A robust outcome would show reproducible discrete increments near one-third of a revolution, ATP-dependent waiting behavior and a quantitatively supported work-to-free-energy comparison. It would support sequential coupling in isolated F1 under the tested conditions. It would not automatically establish behavior of membrane-coupled FoF1 in a cell. The published historical-target Yasuda and colleagues paper reports approximately 120-degree steps and work near the free energy of one ATP hydrolysis. A historical-direction hit requires resolved stepping, ATP/load discrimination and the corresponding prediction, with credible measurement controls; reciting the angle or article title alone is insufficient.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Verify an identified F1 construct, central-subunit reporter attachment and ATPase competence, then consult single-molecule tracking and rotation Methods. Use an ATP concentration grid defined relative to a measured kinetic transition: proposed 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3 and 10 times the concentration at which waiting and transit contribute comparably. If that concentration is unknown, locate it with a bounded pilot. Independently vary at least three measured reporter loads, keeping free magnesium and other buffer chemistry controlled. Record nucleotide purity and relevant activities.

Calibrate angular localization with a driven/known-geometry reference and a stationary probe, temporal response with device timestamps and synthetic step/continuous trajectories, and actual probe drag including surface effects. Select frame rate and exposure to obtain a proposed minimum of five samples across the shortest dwell being claimed, then estimate missed/merged events under that acquisition. Very short dwells remain unresolved rather than being assigned an exact count. Freeze filtering, unwrapping and change-point settings using calibration records. Compare a step model with a continuous-rotation null without imposing a three-state answer; test an independent algorithm.

Quantify radians per event, dwell-time distribution, angular transit time and backward/failed tracking fraction for every eligible molecule. Nest molecules within independent enzyme/flow-cell preparations and retain inactive objects in a separate denominator. The primary ATP-by-load analysis asks whether chemical waits change with ATP while mechanically limited transit changes with calibrated drag. Distinguish consistent approximately 120-degree increments from a histogram peak induced by the fitting prior. A concentration-dependent wait supports a chemical association only under explicit kinetic alternatives; it does not prove one ATP per event by itself.

For energy, integrate torque over angle and propagate geometry uncertainty. Compare work to ATP-hydrolysis free energy calculated from the actual ATP, ADP, phosphate, temperature and activities; do not substitute one textbook free-energy value. Independently measured turnover can constrain ATP coupling. If apparent pauses track surface position, replace the attachment or probe and obtain fresh confirmation. If steps disappear at lower temporal resolution, report the bandwidth limitation. The historical direction combines resolved stepping, ATP/load discrimination and supported energetics; reverse synthesis is a different target.
