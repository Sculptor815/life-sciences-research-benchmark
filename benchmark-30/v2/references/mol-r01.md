# mol-r01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The next question should identify the physical step connecting proteolytic activation to loss of membrane integrity. Does the liberated gasdermin amino-terminal domain act directly on a membrane, or does it activate another cellular component that performs the membrane damage? Within the direct-action hypothesis, distinguish regulated pore formation from nonspecific aggregation, detergent-like disruption, and artifacts of the detection system. The original cellular result alone cannot choose among these explanations. A useful answer therefore links a minimal biochemical system to a carefully controlled cellular test rather than simply adding more death-marker measurements.

Stage one defines the estimand and competing predictions. Direct action predicts a protein-dependent change in a defined membrane system containing no cytosol or downstream execution machinery. A pore model additionally predicts organized membrane-associated assemblies or conducting pathways whose behavior is inconsistent with unrestricted membrane dissolution. Indirect execution predicts that the isolated fragment will not reproduce the relevant effect unless a missing cellular component is supplied. However, failure in an artificial membrane could also reflect an inappropriate lipid composition or inactive protein preparation, so a negative result requires validated materials.

Stage two prepares an explicit reagent map. Include intact protein, the amino-terminal fragment, the complementary carboxy-terminal fragment, and an activation-resistant or otherwise inhibited condition where available. Sequence-verify constructs and document the species, boundaries, tags, purification history, and storage history. Confirm identity and purity with independent measurements, and examine aggregation or degradation before membrane assays. Compare protein amounts on a molar basis and record the concentration measurement method. If a mutation is used later, verify that its apparent loss of function is not simply loss of soluble folded material.

Stage three establishes membrane preparations. Define lipid identities and ratios, vesicle preparation, removal of unencapsulated reporter, particle-size distribution, and storage interval. Use a baseline membrane composition and systematically varied alternatives selected to test a specific interaction hypothesis. Do not change charge, curvature, tracer loading, and total lipid simultaneously and then attribute the result to one lipid. Prepare independent batches and retain a batch identifier. Validate the intact-membrane baseline and the assay's dynamic range before introducing the protein. Exact compositions and concentrations must be fixed after source verification and pilot validation.

Stage four measures permeability with a randomized plate or chamber layout. Include vesicles alone, buffer alone, each protein state, protein without reporter-containing vesicles, and a calibrated complete-release control. Collect a time course rather than a single endpoint. Normalize release using appropriate baseline and total-release measurements while retaining raw values. Establish whether the protein changes the reporter's fluorescence independently of release through quenching, scattering, binding, or pH effects. An apparent permeability increase that also occurs in a membrane-free reporter assay is not evidence for a biological membrane mechanism.

Stage five determines membrane association independently. Separate membrane-bound from soluble protein using a validated method and quantify both fractions, including recovery. Include protein-only controls because aggregates can sediment or float anomalously. Compare association and permeability across the same protein and lipid conditions, preferably from paired preparations. Binding without leakage suggests that association and membrane disruption are separable stages. Leakage without detectable association could reflect a transient interaction or an artifact; it should trigger an additional measurement rather than immediate dismissal or acceptance.

Stage six asks whether the observed permeability has properties expected of discrete conducting structures. An orthogonal approach can measure electrical conductance in a defined membrane, while a physical imaging approach can examine membrane-associated assemblies. Each has its own controls: membrane stability and recording noise for electrophysiology, and specimen preparation, field selection, and classification rules for imaging. An image resembling a ring is insufficient if the sample contains detergent-induced aggregates or if the analyst selected only striking particles. Prespecify how all eligible fields or traces will be included and how ambiguous structures will be reported.

Stage seven tests selectivity and mechanism using a panel of probes or conditions that discriminate models. Different probe sizes can constrain the permeability pathway, but probe charge and protein binding must also be considered. Time-resolved assembly measurements can ask whether membrane association precedes leakage. Concentration-response curves should include a range in which the preparation remains interpretable rather than relying exclusively on an overwhelming protein excess. A claim of a defined pore size requires calibrated physical evidence; a leakage cutoff by itself is an imperfect proxy for geometry.

Stage eight develops a separation-of-function test. Select a candidate alteration predicted to change membrane binding or assembly, but do not treat a structural prediction as proof. Measure the altered protein's identity, solubility, folding, cleavage, and abundance alongside its membrane activity. An alteration that broadly destabilizes the protein cannot uniquely implicate a membrane-interaction surface. If feasible, compare more than one independently designed perturbation and a revertant or restoration strategy. The purpose is to connect a specific molecular property to the downstream effect while narrowing generic loss-of-function explanations.

Stage nine returns to a cellular setting. Use a defined cell identity with a documented genetic background and verify the relevant deficiency or endogenous expression state. Introduce the rescue constructs within a prespecified expression range so that massive overexpression does not substitute for normal activation. Measure the timing of proteolytic processing, membrane permeability, and later rupture separately. Pair a permeability reporter with an independent viability or release measurement and inspect whether altered proteins affect upstream activation. A cellular failure caused by absent cleavage cannot be interpreted as evidence about the isolated fragment's membrane activity.

Stage ten specifies controls for the biological bridge. Compare deficient cells with appropriately matched rescue conditions and a functional reference construct. Track baseline viability, expression heterogeneity, and localization. Validate that the intervention or assay reagent is not itself compromising membranes. If the purified mutant loses membrane activity and the matched cellular construct fails to restore the corresponding phenotype despite normal activation and abundance, the combined evidence strengthens the causal link. Neither arm alone would exclude all alternatives. Differences between cell types may identify context dependence rather than experimental contradiction.

Stage eleven defines replication and quantitative inference. Independent protein preparations, membrane preparations, and cell cultures are biological or preparation-level units appropriate to their respective claims. Multiple wells, particles, or images from one preparation are nested technical observations. Prespecify the primary contrast, time window, exclusion criteria, and minimum meaningful effect. Use uncertainty intervals and retain all batches, including inactive or failed preparations with documented quality status. Avoid choosing the one lipid composition, mutant, or time point that maximizes agreement after seeing the result without labeling that analysis exploratory.

The decisive pattern would connect regulated protein activation to specific membrane interaction, direct permeability in a minimal system, orthogonal physical evidence, and a validated cellular consequence. If leakage requires crude lysate, direct sufficiency remains unresolved. If every protein condition damages every membrane only at extreme concentration, nonspecific disruption remains plausible. If a mutant loses both folding and function, its mechanistic interpretation remains ambiguous. The strongest supported conclusion concerns a defined membrane-permeabilizing activity under the tested conditions. It does not establish that this activity explains every stage of terminal cell rupture, cytokine biology, or disease outcome. All operational details above are proposed design requirements, not claims that the historical authors performed each additional control.

Preserve an auditable link between every sample and its measured state. The sample manifest should connect construct sequence, purification batch, membrane batch, activation treatment, acquisition file, analysis version, and exclusion decision. Reserve aliquots for blinded identity or integrity checks when feasible. Analyze raw and normalized signals side by side because normalization can hide baseline differences or saturation. A plate-level positive control that fails its acceptance criterion should trigger a documented batch failure, not selective retention of favorable wells. Publish or retain negative conditions with the same completeness as positive ones so that the proposed mechanism can be assessed against the entire experimental search.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Specify the protein sequence, cleavage boundary and purified full-length, N-terminal, C-terminal and cleavage-resistant forms from verified Methods. Record protein concentration in micromolar and total lipid in millimolar, preserving protein-to-lipid molar ratio. Use a defined phospholipid vesicle panel varying one hypothesized binding component at a time while holding vesicle size and total lipid constant. These are proposed membranes, not a claim that a historical lipid recipe has been reproduced.

Pilot a logarithmic protein-to-lipid ratio series, for example 1:10,000, 1:3,000, 1:1,000 and 1:300 after solubility and sensitivity checks, with an observation window spanning baseline through leakage plateau. Match cleavage-protease carryover. Define fractional release as (F_t minus matched intact-vesicle baseline)/(complete-release signal minus that baseline), but only after testing reporter response in the presence of each protein and lipid condition. A proposed spontaneous-leakage gate below 10% of full release and product-spike recovery of 80-120% identify interpretable batches; lock them on independent pilots. Include vesicles without protein, free dye with protein, active/inactive proteins and controlled complete disruption. Failure of reporter recovery triggers independent chemical/electrical detection rather than ad hoc normalization.

Measure membrane-bound protein with flotation or another validated separation, including protein-only aggregation controls and recovery of both fractions. Use calibrated planar-bilayer conductance or unbiased structural imaging plus size-selective permeability to distinguish discrete pores from generic disruption. Record all eligible fields/traces and physical dimensions with resolution limits. A ring-shaped image or leakage alone is insufficient. A binding/assembly mutant must retain folding, soluble abundance, cleavage and relevant delivery before functional loss identifies an interface.

For a noninfectious cell bridge, compare matched GSDMD-loss and near-endogenous rescue preparations with the same activation conditions. Quantify small-dye uptake per original cell population, time to permeability, subsequent large-marker release and death, with background and positive-damage controls. Independent protein/lipid batches and cultures are replication levels, while wells/cells are nested. Link a mutant's purified phenotype to its cell phenotype under preserved upstream activation. Direct permeabilization plus orthogonal pore evidence and a valid cellular bridge supports the historical direction; it does not establish that pore formation alone explains all terminal rupture or inflammatory outcomes. If only aggregated protein is active, re-purify and repeat on new batches; persistent properly controlled failure requires considering a missing component or indirect executor.
