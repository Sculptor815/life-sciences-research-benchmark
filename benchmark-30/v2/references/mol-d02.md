# mol-d02: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

All experiments described here are proposed; none is a reconstruction of the 1991 methods. The central design is an isogenic same-background comparison with acute p53 loss, near-endogenous rescue, matched DNA-damage load, live-cell tracking, DNA-content plus nucleotide-incorporation measurement, and death measurement.

Preparation and QC. Establish a parental line carrying a verified wild-type p53 locus and a matched derivative for acute p53 depletion. Because the source does not supply molecular reagents or culture conditions, the protocol must first specify and validate the depletion method and the rescue construct. QC should verify p53 transcript and protein at baseline, after damage, and after rescue by methods orthogonal to the one used for manipulation. Near-endogenous rescue means matching the rescued p53 level to the unperturbed parental range rather than overexpressing; if a quantitative range cannot be obtained from the source, measure it in the parental line and set a predefined acceptable interval. Confirm that the damage agent and caffeine are not toxic in the vehicle arm at the chosen exposure and that the readouts are linear over the relevant range.

Experimental unit, allocation and blinding. The inferential unit is the independently manipulated culture population or independent transduction or knockout event, not an individual cell. Cells within a well are observational units and must be summarized or modelled hierarchically. Randomize wells or culture vessels to damage, caffeine, vehicle, p53-status and rescue conditions with a prespecified allocation table. Blind the person performing imaging and the person scoring cycle and death to condition. If clones are used, do not treat clones as independent p53 replicates unless the p53 manipulation is independently introduced into each background; otherwise clone effects remain confounded.

Handling and analysis sequence. Seed parallel cultures at a density that leaves room for division and tracking. Before treatment, record baseline viability and cycle distribution. Calibrate the damage agent by a dose-time matrix that produces a comparable validated damage signal in p53-competent and p53-depleted cultures; if no independent damage readout is available, this is a required input. Then apply a fixed damage exposure and follow live cells by time-lapse microscopy. In parallel, harvest fixed samples for DNA content and nucleotide incorporation. Measure death in the same wells using an independent viability readout and, where possible, live-cell morphology. The analysis sequence is: first verify allocation and depletion or rescue; second exclude failed manipulations only by prespecified criteria; third quantify live-cell cycle state over time; fourth quantify incorporation in live, death-negative cells; fifth compute G1 occupancy together with entry into S phase and competing death outcomes; sixth compare prespecified contrasts.

Controls. Include vehicle-treated p53-competent and p53-depleted cultures, damage-treated p53-competent and p53-depleted cultures, rescue with and without damage, a non-targeting manipulation control, and caffeine as a separate perturbation arm. Caffeine is pleiotropic, so it cannot be the primary causal evidence; use it to test whether the G1 response is sensitive to a secondary perturbation under matched viability. Include a damage-load control and a death control in every contrast. For incorporation, include a no-incorporation control and a background fluorescence control. For live tracking, include a division-arrested control only if it can be independently validated.

The primary quantitative contrast is the genotype-by-damage change in longitudinal G1 residence or entry into S phase in the original tracked cohort, with death as a competing event; the rescue contrast asks whether near-endogenous p53 restores the response. Survivor-only G1 fraction and incorporation are complementary summaries with explicit denominators. Report effect sizes with uncertainty and the full time course, not only one time point. A secondary summary is incorporation among prospectively defined live singlets, reported together with death and original-cohort counts; this is not an automatic causal adjustment for survival. Caffeine effects should be reported as a separate interaction, not merged into the p53 necessity claim.

Sample-size and power planning. Use pilot data from the chosen readouts to estimate variance in G1 fraction, incorporation and death. Power should be based on independent culture replicates, not individual cells. If no pilot variance is available, specify a calibration pilot and a required minimum detectable effect; do not invent a number. Because cell-level measurements can be numerous, report both cell-level distributions and culture-level summaries. For live tracking, account for censoring and competing death.

Prespecified exclusions and stopping rules. Flag technical failures of depletion, rescue or baseline viability using rules fixed before treatment. Do not exclude wells merely because post-treatment damage or death differs; report those differences as biological outcomes or unresolved causal mediators. Exclude cells from incorporation analysis if they are death-positive or have ambiguous cycle status; report all exclusions and their counts. Stop or recalibrate if the damage agent produces a different damage load between p53 arms, if death exceeds a prespecified fraction that would make cycle interpretation unreliable, or if the rescue level falls outside the near-endogenous interval. Do not change gates after seeing the primary contrast.

Missing data. Missing wells or lost tracking fields should be handled by a prespecified model that distinguishes missing at random from condition-related loss. If death causes missing cycle data, use a competing-risk or death-adjusted analysis. Do not complete missing values in a way that assumes no death.

Expected results. A positive result would be: damage causes G1 accumulation and reduced incorporation in p53-competent cultures without a matching increase in death; p53 depletion abolishes the G1 response; near-endogenous rescue restores it. A negative result would be no difference in G1 or incorporation after matching damage load and death. An ambiguous result would be a p53-dependent difference accompanied by unequal death, unequal damage load, or a rescue level outside the endogenous range. Caffeine may modify the response but cannot alone prove p53 necessity.

Artifact discrimination. The isogenic design addresses cell-line background confounding. Death-adjusted denominators and live tracking address the possibility that reduced DNA synthesis reflects death or composition change. Damage-load matching addresses unequal injury. Near-endogenous rescue addresses overexpression artifacts. Blinded gating and prespecified analysis address observer bias.

Troubleshooting. If depletion is incomplete, titrate or use an independent depletion strategy and re-verify. If rescue is too high, lower expression until it falls in the parental range. If initial delivered injury differs for a technical reason, recalibrate in a separate pilot; preserve later repair differences as potential biological mediators. If death is high, reduce damage or switch to a live-cell readout that separates death from cycle. If incorporation is low overall, validate the nucleotide pulse and detection system against a positive control.

Conditional conclusion. Only if isogenic acute p53 loss abolishes the damage-induced G1 response and near-endogenous rescue restores it, with matched damage load and without a death or composition confound, should the data support a necessary under the tested damage conditions and specifically restored by rescue role for p53 in this response. Otherwise the result remains an association or is ambiguous. No historical method is reconstructed here.

Curator clarification and operational extension. Loss plus damage-dependent rescue tests necessity and manipulation specificity; it does not establish that p53 alone is sufficient without damage. A separate p53-induction/no-damage factorial arm is needed for sufficiency, with matched expression kinetics and measurement of off-target stress. Choose fixed irradiation or a specified noninfectious genotoxic exposure only after a pilot, and measure initial injury immediately after exposure. Do not match away later differences in repair, which may be caused by p53. Record the original cohort size and longitudinal transitions into S phase, mitosis, quiescence and death; analyzing survivors alone can introduce selection bias. For cytometry, establish compensation and background on independent controls, remove instrument-time anomalies and debris, identify singlets using pulse geometry, and gate DNA content jointly with incorporation using control-derived thresholds fixed before decoding. Confirm S-phase entry with tracked lineages. For a small number of cultures, aggregate or model cells nested within culture and day, avoiding cell-level pseudo-replication. Use a predeclared genotype-by-damage interaction and a separate rescue contrast. Timing must bracket measured damage response and one untreated cell-cycle duration, rather than choosing the most favorable endpoint. A difference in G1 fraction alone does not establish a longer G1 residence time.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Use an authenticated, p53-competent parental line and a validated acute depletion strategy, with sequence-verified depletion-resistant rescue under tunable near-endogenous expression. Determine the control expression distribution and predeclare an acceptable rescue interval, proposed initially as 0.8-1.2 times the matched parental median if assay precision permits. This interval is a pilot criterion, not a universal definition. Verify depletion kinetics, protein function and off-target perturbation burden before confirmation.

Choose the damage agent from verified source Methods and a noninfectious assay protocol. Pilot at least vehicle and three doses across early exposure and recovery times, measuring initial injury before differential repair develops. Freeze a dose with interpretable parental arrest and tolerable death; do not match away later p53-dependent repair differences. Bracket at least one measured untreated cell-cycle duration in live tracking. Specify nucleotide-pulse duration from pilot recovery and saturation checks and keep it fixed; calibrate DNA detector linearity, singlet gates, compensation and no-pulse background using independent controls.

The primary estimand is a genotype-by-damage contrast in time to S-phase entry or G1 residence for the original tracked cohort, with death as a competing event; G1 percentage and incorporation among live singlets are complementary descriptive endpoints. Report their changing denominators so selective survival cannot masquerade as arrest. Caffeine has its own dose/vehicle calibration and factorial interaction, not the role of a p53-specific inhibitor. Analyze independent treatment allocations clustered by culture/day, report all tracks and attrition, and confirm restored cell-cycle transitions rather than only a rescue band. A technically valid depletion with no effect gives a conditional necessity limit; unequal initial injury, high death or failed rescue makes the mechanism unresolved. Damage-dependent rescue establishes specificity, not p53-alone sufficiency.
