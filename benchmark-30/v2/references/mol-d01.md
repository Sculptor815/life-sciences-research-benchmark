# mol-d01: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

Proposed design. This is a plan for experiments that have not been performed; every step below is proposed, not completed. The question is whether kinase K's catalytic activity, rather than its scaffolding role, is required for ligand-induced signaling and growth in a cancer cell line. The primary estimand is the acute ligand-induced change in phospho-S relative to total S, compared across rescue conditions in a K-null background; the secondary estimand is growth over several days in the same conditions. Observed starting evidence and assumptions must be kept apart. M1 reports that a pooled K knockout lowers phospho-S after ligand stimulation and slows growth. M2 reports that total S is unchanged. M3 reports that one unvalidated kinase-dead construct is available and that its abundance and folding have not been measured. From M1 and M2 the defensible starting statement is that K loss reduces ligand-induced phospho-S without reducing total S abundance, which is consistent with a requirement for K in the pathway; it does not by itself distinguish catalytic from scaffolding function, and pooled knockout data can mix editing efficiencies, off-target effects and selection of different cell states. M3 is the critical gap: an inactive mutant cannot be used as evidence about catalysis until it is shown to be expressed, folded, soluble and capable of binding the relevant partners, because an unstable or misfolded mutant produces a negative result for trivial reasons. Preparation, identity and quality control. Proposed steps: authenticate the cell line by short tandem repeat profiling and confirm mycoplasma negativity and passage history; for the knockout, verify biallelic disruption by amplicon sequencing or equivalent and confirm loss of K protein by a validated antibody, with a second independent perturbation such as an independent guide RNA or an inducible degradation system to guard against clone-specific effects; validate the phospho-S antibody with phospho-peptide competition and a phosphatase-treated control and validate the total-S antibody against a distinct epitope; for the rescue constructs, verify comparable messenger RNA and protein abundance at near-endogenous levels by quantitative polymerase chain reaction and quantitative western blotting, verify correct localization, confirm that the inactive construct retains binding to at least one known K partner by co-immunoprecipitation, and verify loss of catalytic activity using an in vitro kinase assay or a defined cellular substrate readout. The exact K gene identifier, transcript, antibody details and partner identities are required author inputs; none can be invented here. Experimental unit, allocation and blinding. The inferential unit follows independent treatment/manipulation allocation. Preparations, independently generated perturbations, days and any randomized wells have their actual nesting recorded; a well is not an independent K-genotype replicate merely because it was measured separately. Technical wells are nested within preparations. Proposed allocation: randomize plate position and treatment order; blind the analyst to condition during image acquisition, plate reading and growth counting; prespecify the primary contrast and the analysis before unblinding. Sample size cannot be set from an unknown effect size. Proposed calibration: run a pilot with at least three independent preparations per key condition to estimate variance components for phospho-S and growth, then compute the number of independent preparations required for 80 percent power at a two-sided 0.05 level for the prespecified wild-type versus inactive rescue contrast; report the assumed effect size and its source as an assumption, and stop or redesign if the observed variance makes the required n infeasible. Intervention and schedule. The ligand identity, receptor, dose and timing must be supplied by the author. Proposed calibration: generate a ligand dose-response for phospho-S in parental cells, choose a prespecified submaximal concentration with adequate measurement range, verify the local dose-response slope, and include additional doses to detect saturation. Proposed acute signaling schedule: measure phospho-S and total-S at baseline and at several early times after stimulation, for example 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 minutes, with exact times adjusted to the author's ligand system and reported; proposed growth schedule: measure viable cell number or confluence at day 0 and every one to two days for five to seven days, keeping control wells below confluence limits. Serum concentration, plating density and vehicle are standardized across conditions. Controls. Proposed controls include parental cells, a non-targeting guide RNA control, knockout plus empty vector, knockout plus wild-type K rescue, knockout plus inactive K rescue, unstimulated and ligand-stimulated arms for each, and ligand-only and vehicle-only wells; an independently derived second knockout or degradation line; if feasible a second inactivating mutation or an analogue-sensitive allele, to avoid relying on one mutation; and general viability or apoptosis measurements to detect nonspecific toxicity from the rescue constructs. Ordered measurements. Proposed order: first identity and quality-control assays; then knockout validation; then rescue expression, localization, inactive-construct integrity and binding; then the acute phospho-S and total-S measurements; then parallel downstream signaling markers; then growth kinetics; then toxicity and compensation checks; and finally an orthogonal in vitro kinase measurement with purified K and S if reagents permit. Analysis and decision rules. Analyze the acute phospho-S over total-S ratio on a log scale with a mixed model treating preparation as a random effect and wells as nested technical replicates, and analyze growth with a repeated-measures model per preparation. Report effect sizes with confidence intervals and prespecify a small family of secondary contrasts with multiplicity control. Proposed decision rules: if wild-type rescue restores ligand-induced phospho-S and growth while the inactive rescue does not, and the inactive protein is validated as stable, folded and binding-competent, the catalytic-requirement hypothesis gains support. If both rescue constructs restore the phenotype, a noncatalytic or scaffolding contribution is favored. If neither rescues, the result is uninterpretable until expression, localization, assay timing and clonal adaptation are investigated. If the inactive mutant is unstable or misfolded, no conclusion about catalysis can be drawn. Troubleshooting. Proposed checks: if wild-type rescue fails, verify construct abundance and localization, ligand dose and timing, antibody performance and whether the knockout clone adapted; if the inactive mutant aggregates, switch to an alternative inactivation strategy such as a second biochemically validated catalytic mutation or selective acute catalytic inhibition; a degron removes both catalytic and scaffold functions and is only an orthogonal loss-of-protein control; if growth changes but signaling does not, separate proliferation from death with labeling and apoptosis assays; if signaling changes at only one ligand dose, re-examine the dose-response. Limitations. The design is limited to one cell line; it can establish requirement but not direct phosphorylation of S by K; necessity does not establish sufficiency; compensation and clonal effects remain possible even with two perturbations; the inactive mutant may retain residual activity or an aberrant conformation; and growth is a pleiotropic endpoint.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Choose the authenticated parental cancer line and two independently generated K-loss preparations; use a rescue delivery route whose expression can be calibrated without requiring an arbitrary viral protocol. Verify complete sequence of the WT and inactive constructs. Determine transcript length and expected protein size from the chosen accession before designing sequence checks. Validate inactive K with a direct ATP/product assay, solubility/folding, localization and partner interaction; residual catalysis has a quantified detection limit. Similar abundance alone is not preserved scaffolding.

Before confirmation, seed a twofold density series and select a density that remains below confluence through the growth window. Pilot ligand at zero plus a bounded concentration series around a Methods-derived responsive range, with 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 minutes as proposed starting time points. Select a submaximal dose using a preregistered dynamic-range criterion and verify that rescue expression falls within the parental protein distribution. Report background-corrected phospho-S/total-S and log fold growth in viable-cell number, with separate death and proliferation measures. Use a lysate dilution series, phosphatase treatment and matrix standards to verify phospho-detection; clipped or below-quantification signals cannot support a ratio.

The primary difference is ligand response in WT rescue versus validated inactive rescue within the K-loss background; genotype-by-ligand and rescue contrasts are specified before analysis. A second catalytic perturbation with different off-target liabilities tests the conclusion orthogonally. Preserved signaling and growth with inactive but structurally intact K supports a noncatalytic contribution; failure of both rescues invokes expression/adaptation checks. A growth-only change requires a separate survival/cell-cycle interpretation. Even successful catalytic rescue does not show that S is K's direct substrate.
