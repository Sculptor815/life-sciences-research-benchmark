# mol-r02: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The useful next question is whether the checkpoint can be initiated and released using existing cellular proteins, or whether new protein synthesis is required. The original observations establish a RAD9-dependent protective delay, not the biochemical switching mechanism. The competing models predict different responses to an independently verified translation block. This proposed experiment should measure checkpoint initiation and recovery separately; failure to divide during drug exposure alone is not evidence of checkpoint function because translation inhibition can arrest growth for unrelated reasons.

First assemble matched wild-type, rad9 loss-of-function and near-endogenous complementation strains. Confirm strain identity, genotype, basal growth, DNA content and viability. Use independent cultures initiated separately, with culture/day as the replication level and cells as nested observations. Include an independent genetic perturbation or allele when feasible. Document the exact strain background and complementation expression before choosing exposure conditions. Confirm that fluorescent tags or reporters do not change the baseline damage response. No historical dose, strain sequence or timing is available in the packet; those are prerequisites to record, not numbers to invent.

Calibrate a noninfectious DNA-damage exposure in a separate pilot to produce a detectable, reversible checkpoint response in wild type. Measure initial injury and viability before substantial genotype-dependent repair occurs. Independently titrate a translation inhibitor, such as cycloheximide, to suppress labeled-amino-acid incorporation over the intended observation window while preserving recoverable viability. Measure residual translation quantitatively and report the assay detection limit. Confirm that the inhibitor does not interfere with the damage assay or fluorescent reporters. Maintain a vehicle/no-damage control for every genotype and inhibitor condition.

Randomize cultures within day to a genotype-by-damage-by-translation-block factorial design. For initiation, establish translation inhibition before the damage challenge and verify it throughout the window. For maintenance and recovery, first allow checkpoint arrest to develop, then apply translation inhibition in a separate arm. Follow matched undamaged cultures under the same drug schedule. A third schedule removes the inhibitor after the observation period to establish recoverability. If a physical cell-cycle synchronization procedure is used, validate its own stress response and include asynchronously growing cultures as an orthogonal check.

Acquire time-lapse records with a validated cell-cycle readout and parallel DNA-content and budding/morphology measurements. Score first division, nuclear segregation, arrest duration and death for the original cohort, not only surviving cells. Collect damage-repair measurements at prespecified times alongside translation assays. Set sampling intervals from the pilot so several measurements span the shortest relevant transition. Blind tracking and morphology scoring to genotype and treatment; retain raw movies, field-selection rules and failed tracks. Resolve ambiguous events with a second blinded scorer rather than choosing favorable cells.

The initiation contrast is the damage-specific additional delay in translation-blocked wild type relative to its undamaged drug control, compared with the corresponding rad9 contrast. The recovery contrast asks whether cells exit the checkpoint as damage resolves despite persistent verified translation suppression. Because the drug can suppress general cell-cycle progression, an interpretable checkpoint-specific marker or an orthogonal reversible perturbation may be necessary; merely seeing no division under drug cannot identify checkpoint maintenance. Quantify recovery after drug removal separately from recovery during the block.

Use survival or multistate analysis with death as a competing event and clustering by independent culture. Prespecify the primary interaction, a biologically meaningful delay and a precision or power target from pilot variance. Do not count hundreds of cells as hundreds of independent cultures. Report every treatment trajectory and uncertainty. Technical exclusions include failed strain identity, failed exposure or unusable imaging under rules fixed before unblinding. Incomplete translation suppression and excessive nonspecific toxicity make the mechanistic test inconclusive; they are not grounds to replace inconvenient outcomes with favorable repeats.

Checkpoint initiation and damage-associated recovery during a sufficiently sensitive translation block, with RAD9 dependence and appropriate drug controls, support regulation of pre-existing proteins. They do not identify phosphorylation or prove that absolutely no new protein is needed below the assay detection limit. Failure under the inhibitor could instead reflect toxicity, loss of energy or depletion of a short-lived permissive factor. Use an orthogonal acute translation perturbation and measure metabolic competence before attributing necessity to new synthesis. Constitutive RAD9 transcript would complement this argument but would not by itself establish posttranslational control.

The published historical-target later study by Weinert and Hartwell reports arrest induction and recovery in the presence of cycloheximide and interprets this as posttranslational regulation. A qualified historical-direction hit requires the biological switching question, a verified synthesis perturbation, separate initiation/recovery readouts and the appropriate conditional prediction. Naming RAD9 or the later article without this experiment is not a hit. A different well-identified mechanistic proposal can earn scientific-quality credit even if it does not match this historical direction. Full original methods remain unaudited, so the operational plan above is explicitly a proposed design.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Use an identified S. cerevisiae background with WT, rad9 loss and near-endogenous RAD9 complementation. Verify genotypes, growth and reporter behavior before exposure. Read primary checkpoint and translation-inhibition Methods and resolve which damage, checkpoint and protein-synthesis assays can be measured under the same drug conditions. A proposed synthesis inhibitor is cycloheximide, but its working dose and strain susceptibility are measured, not imported as universal facts.

In an independent pilot, titrate drug concentration and preincubation duration while quantifying labeled-amino-acid incorporation relative to vehicle, corrected for uptake and cell number. A proposed residual-synthesis target below 5% is useful only if the assay can resolve that level and recovery/viability are adequate; report the detection limit instead of asserting zero synthesis. Independently calibrate noninfectious damage to give a reversible WT response, measuring initial injury before differential repair. Use early sampling that brackets the measured untreated division time, then freeze the window.

Run separate initiation and recovery schedules: establish translation block before damage for initiation; allow arrest, then add the block for maintenance/recovery. Cross each with no damage, WT/rad9/rescue and vehicle. Track original cohorts for nuclear division, budding, checkpoint-state measurements, repair and death. Drug-induced general growth arrest makes division alone nonidentifying; require a separately validated checkpoint-state readout or an orthogonal reversible synthesis perturbation. Recovery only after washing out the translation inhibitor does not show recovery while synthesis remains suppressed.

Estimate the RAD9-dependent damage-specific delay or checkpoint-state contrast relative to each drug/no-damage baseline, clustering by independently allocated culture/day. Follow the original denominator, not surviving cells only. Recheck actual translation suppression over the entire key interval. Initiation and checkpoint release despite sensitive verified suppression support regulation of existing proteins under the tested range, not a specific phosphorylation event or absolute independence from every newly made molecule. Failure may reflect depleted permissive proteins, altered energy or toxicity. Use an orthogonal inhibitor/acute perturbation with separate liabilities and measure metabolic competence. If those cannot be separated, label the switching mechanism unresolved rather than converting drug-induced arrest into a historical hit.
