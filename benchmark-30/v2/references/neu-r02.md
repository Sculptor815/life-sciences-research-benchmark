# neu-r02: revised reference

Version: reference-v2.1. All procedures and numerical pilot criteria are proposed, not claims of historical execution. New revisions await owner review. Scientifically equivalent alternative designs can satisfy the rubric.

The useful biological question is whether early patterned visual experience helps establish the distribution of cortical responsiveness to the two eyes. Adult organization alone cannot distinguish an experience-independent developmental program from experience-dependent modification. A discriminating proposal compares controlled alteration of one eye's visual experience with matched rearing controls and tests each eye independently after optical access is restored. The endpoints are cortical response distributions and competing peripheral explanations, not an assumption that all reduced responses reflect cortical rewiring.

Before work, justify the model and developmental stage using available evidence and obtain an approved welfare-reviewed protocol with veterinary endpoints. Do not reconstruct historical deprivation procedures from an abstract. Specify the approved reversible manipulation, monitoring schedule and stopping criteria from that protocol. Consider whether existing data or a less invasive validated manipulation can answer the question. The packet does not supply animal numbers, exposure duration, anesthesia, recording coordinates or optical parameters; these must be justified and documented before execution.

Allocate independent animals within litter and developmental-stage blocks to altered visual experience, sham handling and normal experience, balancing sex or other justified covariates. Counterbalance which eye is affected. A bilateral-experience-control arm may help separate interocular competition from general visual deprivation, but its interpretation and welfare cost require a specific justification. Animals, not recorded neurons, are independent treatment units. Randomize recording order and maintain blinded group codes for stimulus presentation, unit selection and analysis.

Document baseline ocular health and the effectiveness of the experience manipulation using independent observations. Monitor optical clarity and general development throughout. At the prespecified endpoint, verify that both eyes can transmit the test stimulus comparably; persistent obstruction cannot be called cortical plasticity. Record refraction or alignment where relevant and control illumination, contrast and retinal stimulus position. Use independent retinal or visual-pathway measurements to evaluate peripheral function when the cortical responses differ. A normal-looking eye does not by itself prove normal retinal signaling.

Map receptive fields before presenting a randomized, counterbalanced stimulus set to each eye separately. Match stimulus luminance, contrast, orientation coverage and timing across eyes. Include blank trials, repeated stimuli and eye-isolation controls to prevent unintended stimulation of the other eye. Record neural signals using fixed depth/position sampling rules that do not depend on finding a desired ocular preference. Establish unit isolation, response reliability and baseline stability criteria before decoding treatment labels. Retain neurons without a visually evoked response in a separately reported category rather than silently deleting them.

For each qualified unit, quantify baseline-corrected responses from each eye, orientation tuning and reliability with uncertainty. Define a bounded ocular-dominance index only when its response denominator is sufficiently measurable; weak responses need an explicit category rather than an unstable ratio. Report distributions per animal and the proportions of contralateral, ipsilateral, binocular and unresponsive units. Use a hierarchical model or animal-level distributional contrast, with sampling location as a documented nuisance variable. Do not let animals with many recorded neurons dominate the treatment estimate.

Prespecify a primary shift in the ocular-dominance distribution and a meaningful animal-level effect. Use pilot between-animal variability to plan power or precision. Record litter, developmental stage, recording site and all technical losses. Blinded technical exclusions may address recording instability or failed stimulus isolation; an animal with a weak deprivation effect must remain an outcome. Report deviations from the approved protocol. If peripheral deficits prevent an equivalent retinal stimulus, the localization question is unresolved even when the cortical group contrast is large.

A systematic shift toward the experienced eye, with validated input to both eyes and controlled cortical sampling, supports experience-dependent organization. It does not by itself identify a synaptic site, eliminate thalamic contributions or establish the full developmental sensitive period. To localize further, compare cortical effects with upstream physiological measurements. To test timing or recovery, run separately powered developmental-onset or restoration cohorts; neither can be inferred from one endpoint. A null group contrast may reflect insufficient manipulation or low precision and should be interpreted with manipulation checks and intervals rather than as proof of an innate immutable circuit.

The published historical-target Wiesel and Hubel follow-up measured cortical responses after monocular visual deprivation. A qualified historical-direction match requires the developmental-experience question, controlled unilateral manipulation, eye-specific cortical recording and the predicted relative ocular-response change with peripheral and sampling controls. Merely proposing more receptive-field recordings or naming a famous critical period does not qualify. Equivalent approved designs can receive quality credit. This is a proposed modern audit of causal logic, not a claim that all historical animal procedures or all supporting methods have been reviewed.

## Strict second-round operational extension

Operational contract for this proposed protocol. Before collecting confirmatory data, consult the relevant primary Methods, supplements and validated assay/tool protocol. Build a transfer table containing each reagent or tool, source/version, source condition, proposed local condition, reason for changing it and pilot result. This is a required future action, not a claim that unavailable historical procedures have been recovered. Unknown identities or settings remain unresolved prerequisites until the table is complete. Do not replace absent sources with invented precision.

Define positive, negative and procedural controls before treatment: specify which comparison establishes technical responsiveness, which estimates background, and which separates the manipulation from its delivery or handling. Name the competing explanation each controls. A positive control's pass threshold is fixed on independent calibration, not on whether the main treatment gives the hoped-for effect. Use the assay-specific controls below; do not demand unrelated PCR, viral or imaging procedures. If genetic material is amplified, record template/amplicon length, validated primers, reagent amounts, polymerase-specific extension logic and sequence identity. If viral delivery is essential, record the actual construct, functional titer, delivered amount, target-cell number or tissue volume, expression interval, distribution and toxicity; vector-genome concentration alone is not a functional infection dose. These details become obligations only for the chosen route.

Use a separate development pilot to estimate nuisance variability and operating ranges. A proposed minimum of three independent preparations is a variance-discovery starting point, not an automatically adequate powered experiment. Choose a smallest meaningful effect and then simulate or calculate the number of independently allocated units required for a stated interval-width or power target, propagating uncertainty in pilot variance. Fix the maximum sample size, intermediate technical checks and stopping rule before confirmation. If resources are insufficient, report the detectable effect and uncertainty instead of counting subsamples as new replication. Randomize independent units within justified day/batch blocks, balance processing order, conceal allocation where possible and keep outcome scoring and data processing blinded. If an element cannot be blinded, document why and substitute automated locked processing or an independent coded assessment.

Keep a registered primary contrast and multiplicity family. Prespecify how censoring, weak signal, missing units and technical losses are handled. Retain raw and normalized measurements, original assigned-unit denominators and every nonresponse. A failed instrument or sample-identity check can invalidate a measurement under a prospective rule; a biologically negative result cannot. Analyze independent-unit summaries or a justified nested model, verify its assumptions and report effect sizes with intervals. Use equivalence bounds only when an adequately precise absence claim is intended. A non-significant comparison is otherwise inconclusive.

Troubleshoot in the order identity and delivery, reference/control performance, measurement dynamic range, then the biological hypothesis. Record each change, its reason and all failed runs. Change only the implicated variable in a bounded pilot or an explicitly designed factorial pilot; freeze the revised procedure and obtain fresh independent confirmation. Do not retune on the confirmatory outcome. Alternative mechanisms and outright failure of the hypothesis are valid results. The audit trail links sample/unit IDs to raw files, calibration records, timestamps, deviations, exact analysis versions, settings and outputs. A second analyst should be able to reconstruct the reported contrast from those records. No part of this reference claims an experiment has already succeeded.

Case-specific implementation.

Select a model and developmental window justified by visual-system evidence and an approved welfare-reviewed protocol; compare reversible alteration of one eye's patterned input with sham and normal rearing. Consult the actual manipulation, ophthalmic and cortical-recording Methods before setting duration, care or recording conditions. Historical matching does not require copying historical welfare practices. Define monitoring and veterinary stopping rules before any animal work and consider existing data or validated less invasive alternatives.

Block randomized animals by litter/age, counterbalance affected eye and recording order, and blind stimulus/neuronal selection. Calibrate delivered visual contrast and luminance, alignment, optical clarity and eye isolation. Choose a stimulus matrix covering orientations and spatial frequencies appropriate to the model, using an independent pilot. Validate both eyes' optical access at the endpoint and measure peripheral/upstream function; normal surface appearance does not establish retinal or thalamic normality.

For each sampled neuron, estimate baseline-corrected response per stimulus to each eye and define ocular dominance, for example (R_c-R_i)/(R_c+R_i), only when the total evoked response exceeds a predeclared noise-derived denominator threshold. Report weak/unresponsive units separately, never drop them merely to sharpen a dominance distribution. Select recording sites by a fixed anatomical sampling grid and blinded unit-quality criteria. Animal-level distributions, not the number of neurons, carry treatment replication. Quantify eye-specific response magnitude, ocular-dominance shift, reliability and sampling location with uncertainty.

A shift toward the experienced eye after valid manipulation, restored optical access and reliable peripheral inputs supports experience-dependent organization. It does not uniquely locate a changed synapse. Compare upstream and cortical responses for localization, and use separately allocated restoration/onset cohorts for recovery or developmental timing. Incomplete manipulation, unequal sensory delivery or broad developmental illness makes an outcome ambiguous. If technical checks pass and a precise null remains, constrain the experience-dependent effect rather than deleting weakly affected animals. Preserve a complete per-animal timeline, sampling log and response table with the approved protocol version.
