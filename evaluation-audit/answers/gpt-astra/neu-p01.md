## 4. Explicit evidence-to-error-to-conclusion chain

### A. Reliability of the group comparison

1. **Evidence:** N1 reports unreconciled animal identities. E2 states that the allocation file and raw animal identities needed to verify independent groups are unavailable.
2. **Potential error mechanism:** If records attributed to different animals actually came from the same animal, an analysis treating them as independent could overstate the effective sample size and precision. If animal-to-group assignments were wrong, the estimated inhibition effect could also be distorted.
3. **Inference:** These are plausible error pathways, not demonstrated errors. The missing records prevent checking whether they occurred.
4. **Conclusion:** E1’s reported reduction in learned performance is not independently validated by this packet. Its existence, magnitude, and uncertainty remain unresolved at the underlying-data level.

The key distinction is between **“independence cannot be verified”** and **“independence was violated.”** The packet supports the former, not the latter.

### B. From reduced performance to necessity for learning

1. **Evidence:** E1 says the article reports lower learned performance after projection inhibition.
2. **Potential inferential error:** A performance decrement is not automatically a failure of learning. It could reflect impaired acquisition, but it could also reflect altered expression or retrieval of what was learned, or another impairment affecting the measured behavior.
3. **Unreported parameters:** The packet supplies no inhibition timing, efficacy or selectivity measurements, behavioral controls, effect size, or evidence about how much learning remained.
4. **Inference:** Even if the reported comparison were valid, E1 alone would not distinguish these explanations.
5. **Conclusion:** The assertion that projection P is **necessary for learning** is not established.

The scope of “necessary” also matters. “Necessary for any learning” is stronger than “necessary for normal performance under this task and intervention.” Lower performance alone does not establish the stronger claim. A rigorously controlled decrement could support a contribution to normal task performance, but the required validation is absent here. Conversely, residual performance would not by itself disprove necessity if inhibition were incomplete; inhibition efficacy is unreported.
