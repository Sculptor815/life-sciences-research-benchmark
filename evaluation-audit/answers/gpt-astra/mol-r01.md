# GPT-6 Astra / mol-r01

Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

## Recommended next action and decisive question

**Test whether purified, liberated GSDMD amino-terminal domain can permeabilize a protein-free lipid bilayer.** Pair this sufficiency test with cellular separation-of-function experiments and, if necessary, biochemical identification of an additional effector.

The unresolved question is:

> **Does the liberated amino-terminal domain itself create the membrane lesion that causes lytic death, or does it activate another cellular effector that creates that lesion?**

A positive minimal reconstitution would establish direct membrane-permeabilizing capacity. It would **not**, by itself, establish that this activity explains death in cells, that no cellular cofactors participate, or that the lesion is a structurally defined pore.

All experiments and outcomes below are **proposed**, not reported results.

---

## 1. Evidence, inferences, and competing mechanisms

### Evidence-to-inference chain

Evidence locations refer to the supplied packet.

| Evidence location | Supported inference | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| **E1:** “A genetic screen identifies GSDMD as necessary…” | GSDMD is required for the tested inflammatory-caspase-associated lytic-death pathway. | Necessity does not establish whether GSDMD is the membrane-damaging effector or an upstream activator. |
| **E2:** “Proteolytic cleavage separates two domains.” | Cleavage changes the molecular species available to act. | Cleavage alone does not identify the physical execution mechanism. |
| **E3:** “The liberated amino-terminal domain can induce cell death, whereas the intact protein is autoinhibited.” | The amino-terminal domain is a candidate active species; cleavage can relieve inhibition. | Killing in cells may still require another cellular effector. |
| **E4:** “Cytokine processing and cytokine release can be separated experimentally.” | Processing and release require separate measurements. | Neither cytokine maturation nor extracellular cytokine abundance alone establishes membrane permeabilization. |
| **E5:** The evidence does not determine the physical mechanism. | Direct membrane action remains an experimentally testable hypothesis. | Pores, other bilayer defects, and indirect execution remain open. |

### Competing models and predictions

1. **Direct membrane execution:** The amino-terminal domain acts on lipids to produce a permeability defect.  
   **Prediction:** Purified domain plus an appropriate protein-free bilayer is sufficient. This could involve discrete pores, transient defects, or extensive membrane disruption.

2. **Indirect execution:** The domain activates another cellular effector, designated **E**, which damages the membrane.  
   **Prediction:** Domain alone is insufficient under relevant conditions; adding E restores damage, and activated E can be shown to execute membrane damage independently of continued domain action.

3. **Cofactor-dependent direct execution:** A cellular factor recruits, concentrates, modifies, or organizes the domain, but the domain remains the membrane-damaging component.  
   **Prediction:** A factor may be required in cells or in some reconstitutions, but that factor does not become an independently membrane-damaging effector.

4. **Mixed or parallel execution:** Direct domain activity and another effector both contribute.  
   **Prediction:** Minimal direct activity coexists with a cellular requirement for E, potentially with partial or context-dependent phenotypes.

The study must distinguish a **terminal effector** from a permissive cofactor; genetic requirement alone cannot do this.

## 2. Stage 0 — Qualify reagents and reproduce the cellular phenotype

### 2.1 Protein preparations

Prepare:

- Full-length GSDMD.
- The amino-terminal domain corresponding to the experimentally established cleavage product.
- The complementary carboxy-terminal domain as a domain control; its isolated behavior is not specified by the packet.
- Amino-terminal domain generated by cleavage of purified full-length protein, followed by separation from protease, uncleaved substrate, and the other domain.

Use independently prepared recombinant amino-terminal domain and cleavage-generated domain as complementary routes. Agreement would reduce concerns about artificial termini or preparation-specific contamination.

**Required qualification**

1. Establish the cleavage boundary and construct sequences in the tested system; these are absent from the packet.
2. Verify identity and cleavage state by mass analysis and/or sequence-appropriate immunodetection.
3. Use orthogonal purification steps, including separation by size, to assess purity and aggregation.
4. Remove affinity tags where feasible, or demonstrate that the tag does not determine activity.
5. Exchange all preparations into matched assay buffer.
6. Assess secondary-structure integrity and aggregation before and after the assay incubation.
7. Test multiple independent preparations.

Residual detergent, solvent, protease, or copurifying membrane-active material must be excluded as the cause of leakage. Include a **mock purification**, protease-only preparation subjected to the same cleanup, and matched buffer controls. Ask whether activity tracks the domain across purification fractions.

If a purified preparation is inactive in reconstitution, test its activity after controlled delivery into cells, with delivery-only controls. Cellular activity would support functional competence, but would not prove that the protein has the correct membrane-interacting state in the cell-free assay.

### 2.2 Cellular reference system

Use the cell type and inflammatory-caspase stimulus underlying E1, if available. Re-establish:

- Upstream inflammatory-caspase activation.
- GSDMD cleavage.
- Membrane permeabilization and terminal lysis.
- Loss of the lytic phenotype after GSDMD disruption.
- Rescue with appropriately expressed full-length GSDMD.

**Missing information:** Cell identity, stimulus, cleavage site, construct sequences, expression levels, and timing are unreported. These must be established before definitive experiments; findings from a replacement system would have a different scope.

**Causal purpose:** This stage prevents biochemical inactivity caused by a defective reagent, or an irreproducible cellular phenotype, from being mistaken for evidence about mechanism.

## 3. Stage 1 — Minimal protein–lipid reconstitution

### 3.1 Membrane preparation

Prepare vesicles from chemically defined synthetic lipids, with **no cellular proteins or cell extract**.

Use a prospectively specified composition panel rather than a single assumed “correct” membrane:

- A neutral-headgroup baseline.
- Bilayers containing a defined anionic-headgroup component.
- Additional compositions chosen to test membrane physical properties or approximate the experimental cell membrane, if that composition is measured.

Lipid identities and molar fractions are **design variables requiring validation**, not established GSDMD preferences.

For each composition:

1. Prepare lipid films or use another validated vesicle-formation method.
2. Remove preparation solvent sufficiently to eliminate solvent-dependent leakage.
3. Hydrate with buffer containing an encapsulatable fluorescent solute.
4. Generate a reproducible vesicle-size distribution using a validated extrusion or equivalent procedure.
5. Remove unencapsulated solute by size-exclusion separation.
6. Measure lipid concentration, vesicle-size distribution, baseline leakage, and encapsulation stability.

Prepare separate matched vesicle batches containing small and larger soluble tracers. Tracer identity and loading concentration must be validated for retention, fluorescence behavior, and lack of interaction with the protein.

### 3.2 Reaction conditions

Measure baseline signal, add protein, and acquire a time course.

Run a protein-concentration series and more than one lipid concentration. Report:

- Soluble protein concentration.
- Total lipid concentration.
- Estimated accessible-leaflet lipid concentration.
- Protein:lipid molar ratio.
- Measured membrane-bound protein fraction.
- Buffer composition, pH, ionic strength, temperature, and mixing conditions.

Choose the initial range from protein stability and assay sensitivity. Subsequently compare activity with the membrane-associated domain abundance measured in cells. Activity restricted to extreme, aggregating, or clearly supraphysiological conditions should be treated cautiously.

### 3.3 Required controls

For every lipid composition include:

- Vesicles plus buffer.
- Mock-purification material.
- Full-length GSDMD.
- Amino-terminal domain.
- Carboxy-terminal domain.
- Cleavage-generated and independently produced amino-terminal domain.
- Protease-only cleanup control.
- A terminal detergent treatment defining complete releasable tracer.

Also test free tracer plus each protein preparation without vesicles. This identifies quenching, fluorescence enhancement, or other optical interference.

A cleavage-resistant full-length construct is useful only after its resistance, stability, and expression have been verified.

### 3.4 Leakage measurements

For a dequenching assay, normalize against matched spontaneous leakage and maximal release:

\[
L(t)=\frac{F_{\mathrm{test}}(t)-F_{\mathrm{blank}}(t)}
{F_{\mathrm{max}}-F_{\mathrm{blank}}(t)}
\]

Apply this only after validating fluorescence behavior and the maximal-release condition. Retain raw signals as well as normalized values.

Measure:

- Lag before leakage.
- Initial leakage rate.
- Final released fraction.
- Dependence on protein concentration, lipid concentration, and composition.
- Differences between small- and larger-tracer release.

Confirm selected conditions by physically separating vesicle-retained and released tracer. This guards against a fluorescence change being misidentified as membrane leakage.

### 3.5 Membrane association

In parallel, use vesicle flotation or another separation that distinguishes lipid-associated protein from soluble protein. Include protein-only controls to detect aggregation.

**Interpretation:**

- **Binding plus leakage:** Consistent with direct membrane action.
- **Binding without leakage:** The domain reaches the membrane but does not form a detectable lesion under that condition.
- **No binding and no leakage:** Does not test downstream membrane-disruption competence; lipid composition or protein state may be inappropriate.

### Stage 1 decision

**Conditional positive:** Reproducible leakage caused by clean amino-terminal domain in protein-free vesicles, absent from matched controls, establishes direct membrane-permeabilizing capacity.

**Conditional negative:** No leakage establishes only that the domain is inactive across the tested conditions. It does not establish an indirect mechanism.

**Ambiguous:** Leakage only in contaminated, aggregating, unstable, or extreme-dose preparations requires reagent or condition revision.

**Causal purpose:** Removing all other cellular proteins tests whether another cellular protein is obligatorily required for the membrane lesion in this defined system.

## 4. Stage 2 — Orthogonal physical measurements

Use conditions selected from Stage 1, including active, inactive, and borderline conditions.

### 4.1 Electrical measurement in a protein-free bilayer

Form a planar lipid bilayer of defined composition and verify stable baseline current and capacitance. Add amino-terminal domain to one compartment while recording current under controlled voltage.

Include matched buffer, full-length protein, mock purification, and protein-free bilayer controls. Validate recording sensitivity and baseline membrane stability independently. Test access from the opposite compartment where relevant.

Record:

- Appearance and latency of conductance.
- Sustained versus transient events.
- Step-like versus continuously increasing current.
- Bilayer survival versus catastrophic rupture.
- Concentration dependence.

**Interpretation:**

- Reproducible conductance events provide an electrical readout independent of fluorescent tracer behavior.
- Discrete events are consistent with localized conducting lesions, but do not alone prove a particular protein-pore architecture.
- Abrupt membrane failure supports direct disruption but does not distinguish a large pore from generalized rupture.
- Negative conductance despite vesicle leakage requires testing membrane geometry, lipid composition, and assay artifacts.

Voltage settings, electrolyte composition, bandwidth, sampling rate, and event-detection thresholds are unreported and require validation.

### 4.2 Single-vesicle content and membrane imaging

Image vesicles carrying separate lumen and membrane labels.

Determine whether content loss occurs:

- While an identifiable membrane boundary remains.
- Through localized events.
- With vesicle collapse, fragmentation, or membrane disappearance.

Combine this with the tracer-size series.

**Interpretation:**

- Solute loss from otherwise persistent vesicles supports permeabilization without gross solubilization.
- Size-dependent passage constrains effective permeability, but should not be converted directly into an exact pore diameter.
- Fragmentation or disappearance supports destructive remodeling or solubilization.
- A mixture may reflect concentration-dependent mechanisms.

Membrane-associated oligomerization can be measured as supporting evidence, but neither binding nor oligomerization alone proves membrane perforation.

**Causal purpose:** These measurements distinguish genuine bilayer damage from an optical artifact and separate “direct membrane action” from the narrower claim of a defined pore.

## 5. Stage 3 — Conditional search for another effector

Undertake this stage if minimal reconstitution is negative or equivocal, or if cellular experiments reveal a requirement not explained by direct activity.

### 5.1 Controlled cellular add-back

Prepare soluble and membrane fractions from **GSDMD-deficient cells**, avoiding introduction of endogenous GSDMD. Compare appropriately controlled unstimulated and stimulated sources.

Test:

1. Vesicles alone.
2. Domain alone.
3. Each fraction alone.
4. Domain plus each fraction.

Track both fluorescence-independent membrane damage and tracer leakage. Match added lipid, buffer, and total material where possible.

If a fraction enables activity:

- Fractionate it and track the enabling activity.
- Distinguish contributions of its lipid content from its protein content.
- Use protease or other selective treatments with subsequent reagent removal and treatment-only controls.
- Identify and purify candidate components.
- Rebuild activity from defined components.

A crude fraction that restores leakage does **not** establish an alternative effector: it could change lipid composition, improve domain binding, or supply a permissive cofactor.

### 5.2 Test whether candidate E becomes the membrane-damaging agent

For a candidate E:

1. Incubate purified E with the amino-terminal domain under conditions that support the reconstructed activity.
2. Separate E from the domain using independently distinguishable purification handles or another validated method.
3. Quantify residual domain.
4. Test the isolated E preparation on fresh protein-free bilayers.
5. Compare with E processed identically without domain exposure.
6. Use residual-domain spike-in controls to determine whether carryover could explain the activity.

If E requires substrates or cofactors, add them as defined components with individual controls; their identities and concentrations cannot be assumed.

**Strong indirect-mechanism result:** Domain exposure creates an active E state; separated E subsequently damages fresh membranes; residual domain is insufficient to explain the effect.

**Cofactor-dependent direct result:** E enables domain membrane binding or activity, but E does not acquire independent membrane-damaging activity.

**Ambiguous result:** Damage requires a persistent domain–E complex. This establishes cooperation, not which component forms the lesion.

Failure to isolate an independently active E does not exclude an unstable activated state or an obligate complex. Those possibilities require component-specific functional perturbations.

**Causal purpose:** This tests the distinction between “another component is needed” and “another component actually executes membrane damage.”

## 6. Stage 4 — Cellular validation and causal separation

### 6.1 Genetic framework

Compare matched cells with:

- Endogenous GSDMD.
- GSDMD disruption.
- Disruption plus wild-type full-length rescue.
- Disruption plus validated cleavage-resistant rescue.
- Controlled expression of the liberated amino-terminal domain.
- Empty-vector or delivery-only controls.

Use expression near the relevant endogenous abundance where possible. Match domain expression to the cleavage-product abundance measured during stimulation rather than relying on uncontrolled overexpression.

If E is identified, add E depletion or knockout and rescue with:

- Wild-type E.
- A stable, appropriately localized E variant defective in its established effector activity.

The design of that variant depends on E’s experimentally demonstrated function.

### 6.2 Readouts and ordering

Collect a time course spanning the period before cleavage through terminal lysis.

Measure separately:

1. Upstream inflammatory-caspase activity.
2. GSDMD cleavage and fragment abundance.
3. Domain membrane association/localization.
4. Early membrane permeability, using membrane-impermeant dye entry and, where feasible, an electrical readout.
5. Terminal lysis, using extracellular release of a normally retained cytosolic marker.
6. Cytokine precursor and mature species in cell-associated and extracellular fractions.

Use processing-sensitive assays for cytokines; total extracellular cytokine signal cannot distinguish processing, release, and passive liberation during lysis. Record terminal lysis independently of cytokine release.

### 6.3 Separation-of-function domain variants

Develop variants empirically rather than assuming particular residues are responsible.

The most informative variant would:

- Remain stable and properly cleaved.
- Retain relevant cellular localization and, ideally, lipid binding.
- Lose permeability-generating activity in minimal reconstitution.
- Retain any identified E-activation function.

A reciprocal variant retaining direct membrane activity but losing E activation would also be valuable.

**Conditional interpretations:**

- Loss of reconstituted permeabilization and cellular lysis, despite preserved cleavage, localization, and E activation, would strongly support direct membrane activity as necessary for execution.
- Retained cellular killing by a direct-activity-deficient variant, accompanied by E activation and E dependence, would support indirect execution.
- Loss of both membrane activity and E activation would be non-discriminating.
- Mutations affecting folding, abundance, or trafficking would not qualify as mechanistic separation-of-function tests.

### 6.4 Epistasis with E

Ask whether E loss blocks death caused by the liberated domain while preserving domain abundance and membrane access.

Also ask whether a validated activated E state bypasses GSDMD loss. Bypass is informative only alongside evidence that GSDMD normally activates E; nonspecific killing by overexpressed E would not establish pathway order.

**Causal purpose:** These experiments establish whether the activity demonstrated in vitro is used in cells and whether E acts upstream of cleavage, downstream as an effector, or as a permissive factor.

## 8. Parameters, rigor, and limits

The packet provides **no numerical assay settings**. Before confirmatory work, validate and report:

- Protein concentrations, purity, stability, and aggregation limits.
- Lipid identities, molar fractions, vesicle sizes, and concentrations.
- Tracer identities, sizes, loading concentrations, and detection limits.
- Buffer, pH, salts, cofactors, temperature, mixing, and incubation duration.
- Electrical recording settings and imaging acquisition parameters.
- Cellular stimulus, expression range, sampling schedule, and assay sensitivity.
- Independent sample number, effect-size threshold, and statistical analysis.

Use pilot data to set quantitative detection and decision thresholds. Randomize conditions across preparation batches and blind event or image scoring where feasible. Independent protein preparations, vesicle preparations, and cell cultures—not individual traces or vesicles alone—must support reproducibility.

**Bottom line:** The decisive strategy is to establish **biochemical sufficiency without cellular proteins**, then test **cellular necessity of that specific biochemical activity**. If another component is required, demonstrate whether it becomes an independently active membrane-damaging effector rather than inferring indirect execution from add-back or genetic dependence alone.
