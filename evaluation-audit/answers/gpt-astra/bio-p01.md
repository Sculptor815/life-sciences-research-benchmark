## 3. Alternatives and consequential limits

The following are **candidate explanations, not reported findings**:

- **Indirect energy-dependent suppression:** ATP depletion could impair translation without Q acting on the ribosome.
- **General cellular injury:** Reduced viability could lower total incorporation, especially if results were not normalized to viable cell number.
- **A different translation target:** Q could affect initiation, tRNA charging, or another translation process rather than peptide-bond formation itself.
- **Labeling or detection effects:** Altered puromycin uptake, intracellular availability, or assay interference could reduce signal without an equivalent reduction in protein synthesis.
- **Genuine direct inhibition with downstream injury:** Translation inhibition could precede and contribute to ATP and viability changes. E2 therefore does not exclude the proposed mechanism.
- **Mixed action:** Q could inhibit the ribosome while also affecting other targets. Direct action would not establish selectivity.

**Unreported parameters** include Q concentration, exposure duration, temporal ordering, normalization, controls, replication, and measurement uncertainty. These omissions prevent assessment of statistical robustness and whether the translation effect occurs before substantial injury. The 60% incorporation decrease and 50% ATP decrease cannot be subtracted or compared proportionally to isolate a “ribosome-specific” component.
