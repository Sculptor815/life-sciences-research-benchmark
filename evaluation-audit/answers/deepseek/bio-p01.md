**Claim decomposition**

The claim has three separable parts:

1. **Direct**: Q acts on the ribosome itself, not through upstream cellular toxicity or energy failure.
2. **Selective**: Q inhibits peptide-bond formation preferentially over other targets/processes.
3. **Mechanism**: the inhibited step is ribosomal peptide-bond formation specifically, not initiation, elongation, tRNA charging, ribosome biogenesis, or substrate availability.

The packet tests none of these three parts directly.

**Benign alternatives**

Several benign or indirect explanations fit the packet at least as well as the proposed mechanism:

1. **Energy-depletion indirect effect**: Q impairs mitochondrial function or ATP production; ATP falls 50%; translation falls because translation is ATP-dependent.
2. **Viability/cell-number artifact**: Q reduces viability; fewer cells or less protein per well lowers puromycin signal.
3. **Uptake/labeling artifact**: Q alters puromycin uptake, labeling efficiency, or assay normalization rather than peptide-bond formation.
4. **Different translation step**: Q inhibits translation initiation, elongation factor function, tRNA charging, or amino acid availability, not peptide-bond formation.
5. **General stress/toxicity response**: Q triggers a global stress response that suppresses translation.
6. **Source benign explanation**: The citation may be a legitimate preprint, thesis, local teaching record, book chapter, or non-indexed work; the title may differ in punctuation, translation, or version; the catalog coverage may simply be incomplete.

These alternatives are not proven, but they are plausible and are not excluded by the supplied evidence.

**Checks that could disprove the concern**

**Mechanistic check (proposed experiment)**: The decisive test is a purified ribosome peptide-bond assay. Use washed 80S ribosomes with mRNA/tRNA substrates and measure peptide-bond formation directly, e.g., via the puromycin reaction or dipeptide formation. Include an ATP/GTP regenerating system and test Q at concentrations below those causing ATP collapse or viability loss. If Q fails to inhibit peptide-bond formation in this cell-free system, the direct claim is disproved. If Q inhibits purified ribosomes, then run a selectivity panel: cytosolic 80S vs mitochondrial ribosomes vs bacterial 70S, plus unrelated enzymes and translation factors. If Q inhibits all comparably, selectivity is disproved. If Q selectively inhibits only the claimed ribosome target, the claim gains support.

**Source check (proposed verification)**: Retrieve the full article through multiple routes: DOI/Crossref, PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, WorldCat, publisher site, institutional repository, and direct contact with P. Orr or the local library. Check title variants and the 2025 date. Check journal/publisher correction notices and Retraction Watch/PubMed retraction status. If the article is found and matches, the source concern is reduced or disproved. If a comprehensive search fails and the author/institution cannot confirm it, the concern increases—though still without proving intent.

**What would change the recommendation**

- **Support the claim if**: a purified ribosome assay shows direct inhibition of peptide-bond formation at non-cytotoxic concentrations, and a selectivity panel shows no comparable inhibition of mitochondrial/bacterial ribosomes or unrelated enzymes.
- **Disprove the direct claim if**: Q does not inhibit purified ribosome peptide-bond formation, or inhibits only when ATP is depleted/viability is compromised.
- **Disprove selectivity if**: Q inhibits multiple ribosome types or unrelated enzymes with similar potency.
- **Change source status if**: the full article is verified with a DOI and no correction/retraction, or if a retraction/correction is found. Until then, the citation remains unverified.
