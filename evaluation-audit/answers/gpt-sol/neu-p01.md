## Evidence-to-inference-to-conclusion chain

### 1. Source identity

- **Evidence:** The dossier archive certifies record NS-12 as R. Vale, “Projection P in learning,” 2023, and the submitted citation matches every field (**C1**).
- **Inference:** The citation refers to the intended dossier record; there is no citation mismatch within this teaching archive.
- **Limit:** The archive is authoritative only inside the dossier. Thus C1 does not independently authenticate an external publication, identify R. Vale beyond the recorded name, or validate the article’s data or conclusions.
- **Conclusion:** **Dossier-level source identity is established; broader authenticity and validity are not.**

### 2. Data-identity problem and possible error mechanism

- **Evidence:** Animal identities could not be reconciled across acquisition files (**N1**), and the allocation file and raw animal identities needed to verify independent groups are unavailable (**E2**).
- **Inference:** It is impossible from the available record to determine whether observations assigned to different experimental groups came from distinct animals or whether repeated observations were handled at the correct animal level.
- **Potential error:** If an animal appeared in multiple nominally independent groups, if sessions were duplicated, or if repeated measurements were analyzed as independent animals, group means and uncertainty estimates could be distorted. The apparent inhibition effect could then be exaggerated, generated by misallocation, or assigned to the wrong comparison.
- **Important qualification:** N1 and E2 establish that this error **cannot be excluded**, not that it definitely occurred.
- **Conclusion:** The reported group comparison is not independently auditable, so E1 cannot securely support a causal claim.

### 3. From reported result to “necessary for learning”

- **Evidence:** The article reports **lower learned performance** after projection inhibition (**E1**).
- **Permissible inference, conditional on a valid design:** Projection inhibition was associated with poorer measured performance and might indicate that projection P contributes to the measured behavior.
- **Unsupported steps:**  
  1. “Lower” performance is not the same as complete failure to learn.  
  2. Performance measured after inhibition does not, without further timing information, isolate acquisition of learning from expression or retrieval.  
  3. The packet does not report inhibition efficacy or specificity, behavioral controls, effect magnitude, sample size, analysis unit, randomization, blinding, or whether inhibition occurred only during acquisition.
- **Conclusion:** E1 is compatible with projection P contributing to learning, but it does not establish that P is necessary for learning. The identity problem further weakens even the narrower interpretation.

A suspicious feature or notice supports concern about reliability, not an inference of misconduct intent.

## Benign alternatives

The identity discrepancy could arise from non-intentional causes such as file renaming, clerical relabeling, loss of an allocation key, incomplete migration of metadata, or legitimate repeated sessions that were not clearly linked to individual animals. These alternatives avoid an inference of intent, but they do not restore confidence unless the assignments and dependencies can be reconstructed.

Likewise, a genuine performance decrease could reflect effects on movement, motivation, perception, retrieval, or behavioral expression rather than learning acquisition. These alternatives are not resolved by E1.

## Disconfirming checks

### Proposed provenance check

Recover an independently timestamped allocation or acquisition audit trail and require:

1. one-to-one linkage of every acquisition file to a unique animal;
2. demonstration that experimental groups are independent or that repeated measures are explicitly modeled;
3. exclusion or proper clustering of duplicate sessions; and
4. animal-level reanalysis showing that the inhibition effect persists with materially similar uncertainty.

Passing all four checks would disprove the specific concern that unidentified overlap or misallocation generated the reported comparison. **Current limitation:** E2 says the necessary allocation and raw identity records are unavailable, so this check cannot presently be completed from the dossier.

### Proposed direct test of necessity

Conduct an identity-secure replication with unique immutable animal IDs, verified effective and specific inhibition during acquisition, testing without inhibition, and separate controls for sensory, motor, motivational, and retrieval effects. Normal acquisition despite effective inhibition would disprove necessity. Failure of acquisition under those conditions would support necessity, although the exact operational definition of “learning” and adequacy of inhibition would still need to be specified.
