## 2. Strength of the rare-variant analysis

A burden result can at most support an association if its null distribution and variant ascertainment are valid. Pathway enrichment is a further statistical aggregation. Neither step, by itself, proves that a pathway causes disease.

### A. Center and ancestry confounding

- **Evidence:** Cases and controls were sequenced at different centers and had different ancestry distributions [E1].
- **Inference:** Disease status is entangled with at least two non-disease variables: sequencing center and ancestry.
- **Potential error:** A difference in observed rare-variant burden could result from technical center effects, ancestry-related allele-frequency differences, or their interaction rather than disease biology.
- **Consequence:** The observed burden is not identifiable as a disease effect from the supplied information.
- **Conclusion:** The burden association is vulnerable to confounding and cannot support a causal pathway claim.

The direction of bias is not known: these effects could inflate, attenuate, or otherwise distort the signal.

### B. Differential variant ascertainment and unassessed dependence

- **Evidence:** Variants were selected using center-specific filters; coverage and relatedness were not reported [E2].
- **Inference:** Cases and controls may not have had equal opportunities for variants to be detected and pass filtering. If related individuals were included without an appropriate model, the effective number of independent observations may also be overstated.
- **Potential errors:**
  - Differential callability or filtering can create an artificial burden difference.
  - Unmodeled relatedness can miscalibrate standard errors and significance.
- **Consequence:** Nominal burden-test significance may not correspond to the stated false-positive probability.
- **Conclusion:** The supplied burden test is not shown to be calibrated.

“Not reported” does not prove that coverage or relatedness was ignored. It means the packet provides no basis for ruling these problems out. Center-specific filtering, however, is affirmatively reported and therefore requires direct validation.

### C. Incorrect enrichment universe

- **Evidence:** Enrichment used all annotated genes rather than the set of genes capable of passing the study’s filters [E3].
- **Inference:** The tested genes and the background genes had unequal opportunities to enter the burden analysis.
- **Error:** The enrichment denominator does not represent the study’s ascertainment process. Pathways containing more callable or filter-eligible genes can appear enriched relative to a background containing untestable genes.
- **Consequence:** Pathway-level significance can be driven by coverage, gene properties, or filter eligibility rather than disease biology.
- **Conclusion:** The reported enrichment does not reliably establish pathway-specific excess.

### Combined causal conclusion

The chain required for the claim is:

> valid variant ascertainment → calibrated gene burdens → valid pathway enrichment → causal interpretation.

E1–E3 undermine the first three links. Moreover, even a technically valid burden and enrichment result would ordinarily establish **genetic association**, not by itself prove that the pathway is mechanistically causal. No independent replication or functional causal evidence is supplied in the packet.

Therefore, the defensible conclusion is:

> **The available evidence does not establish pathway Z as causal. It leaves pathway Z as a possible biological hypothesis whose apparent support may instead reflect confounding and ascertainment.**

## 3. Benign alternatives and limits

Several benign explanations remain possible:

- Harmonized processing, ancestry adjustment, or coverage restriction may have been performed but not described in the packet.
- Center-specific filters may, after validation, have produced equivalent effective callability.
- Relatedness may have been absent or correctly modeled.
- Using all annotated genes may fortuitously have approximated the testable set, although E3 indicates a formal mismatch.
- Pathway Z may genuinely be involved even though this analysis does not establish it.

Thus the flaws do not prove that the result is false. They show that the reported evidence cannot distinguish a true pathway effect from plausible technical and population alternatives.

Consequential parameters not supplied include center-by-phenotype counts, depth and callability distributions, relatedness estimates, effect sizes, calibration diagnostics, and replication results. Their absence limits any quantitative assessment.

## 4. Proposed check that could falsify the methodological concern

### Proposed analysis/replication

Reprocess raw sequence data with:

1. A single phenotype-blinded calling and filtering pipeline.
2. A common callable-region requirement and comparable depth/quality thresholds for all samples.
3. Removal of close relatives or a kinship-aware burden model.
4. Ancestry matching or appropriate ancestry adjustment.
5. Center-stratified analysis or, if center and phenotype are inseparable, blinded resequencing of a balanced subset at the same center.
6. Permutations constrained within center and ancestry strata to test null calibration.
7. Pathway enrichment restricted to genes actually eligible for the burden test, preferably with matching for relevant detection properties.
8. Independent replication in a cohort where cases and controls are co-sequenced and ancestry-balanced.

**Falsifying prediction:** If the same gene burdens and pathway enrichment remain well calibrated, directionally consistent, and independently replicated after these changes, the concern that the signal is caused by center, ancestry, differential callability, relatedness, or the wrong enrichment universe would be substantially undermined. If the signal disappears, tracks center or ancestry, or loses enrichment under the eligible-gene background, the concern is supported.

Even a positive result from this check would strengthen association rather than, by itself, prove a mechanistically causal disease pathway.
