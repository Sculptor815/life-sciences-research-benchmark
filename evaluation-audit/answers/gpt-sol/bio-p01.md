Reduced signal can result from fewer viable cells, ATP depletion, impaired initiation, aminoacyl-tRNA supply, elongation or translocation defects, altered puromycin uptake, or assay/detection interference.  
   **Conclusion:** E1 supports a cellular translation-associated effect, but not a direct molecular target or a particular translation step.

2. **Evidence:** ATP falls by 50% and viability is reduced (E2).  
   **Supported inference:** Q causes substantial metabolic stress and/or toxicity under the tested conditions.  
   **Limit/error risk:** These changes are major confounders because cellular translation requires energy and viable translational machinery. The packet does not establish whether translation inhibition precedes ATP loss, whether measurements were normalized to viable cell number, or whether comparable ATP depletion alone reproduces the puromycin effect. Conversely, E2 does not prove that ATP depletion causes E1; both could be parallel consequences of Q.  
   **Conclusion:** A secondary reduction in translation is a strong benign alternative and must be excluded before assigning direct ribosomal action.

3. **Evidence:** No purified-ribosome assay or selectivity panel is available (E3).  
   **Supported inference:** Direct inhibition of ribosomal peptide-bond formation has not been tested in an isolated system, and competing targets have not been excluded.  
   **Error:** Inferring “direct” from a cellular proxy is a level-of-mechanism error. Inferring “selective” without counterscreens is an unsupported comparative claim.  
   **Conclusion:** Both the **directness** and **selectivity** components of the claim are unsupported.

4. **Overall conclusion:** The defensible statement is:  
   **“Q reduces cellular puromycin incorporation in association with ATP depletion and reduced viability.”**  
   Calling Q a direct and selective inhibitor of ribosomal peptide-bond formation exceeds the evidence.

## Proposed decisive checks

### Mechanistic check capable of disproving the confounding concern

Test Q in a **purified or reconstituted ribosomal peptide-bond formation assay** that measures formation of a defined peptide bond, independent of cellular ATP depletion and viability.

The concern that the cellular result is merely secondary would be substantially weakened if Q:

1. inhibits the purified peptide-bond reaction with a reproducible concentration–response;
2. acts at concentrations comparable to those producing the cellular translation effect;
3. does not simply interfere with product detection;
4. retains activity when ATP and other energy substrates are experimentally controlled; and
5. shows kinetics or competition behavior consistent with action at the peptidyl-transferase step.

A matched time-course in cells should additionally show reduced translation **before** major ATP loss or loss of viability. An ATP-depletion control matched to the 50% fall would test whether metabolic stress alone can account for E1.

To support **selectivity**, Q must also be tested against relevant alternatives: other translation stages, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, energy metabolism, representative enzymes or macromolecular processes, and—where relevant—different ribosome classes. If Q broadly inhibits these systems at similar concentrations, the selectivity claim is disproved even if direct ribosomal activity is confirmed.
