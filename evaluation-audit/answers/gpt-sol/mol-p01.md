## 2. Evidence supporting the biological claim

### What E1 establishes

Tagged A and B were overexpressed and co-immunoprecipitated in three independent cultures, with input and IgG controls (E1).

This supports:

- expression of the assayed proteins, as assessed by the input controls;
- enrichment beyond an IgG immunoprecipitation control;
- reproducibility across three cultures;
- association of tagged, overexpressed A and B under the tested co-IP conditions.

These are meaningful observations, but they concern an engineered assay context.

### What E1 does not establish

#### Direct binding

Co-immunoprecipitation shows that two proteins occur in the same recoverable complex; it does not determine whether they contact each other directly. A could associate with B through:

- another protein;
- a larger protein complex;
- nucleic acid or another molecular scaffold;
- aggregation or high-concentration effects caused by overexpression;
- tag-dependent interactions.

E2 confirms that no purified-protein binding experiment is supplied. Thus, the step from **co-association** to **direct binding** is unsupported.

#### Binding in unmodified cells

The tested proteins were tagged and overexpressed. That context is not equivalent to endogenous proteins at native abundance in unmodified cells. Tags or overexpression could alter concentration, localization, folding, stoichiometry, or complex assembly.

E2 states that no endogenous co-IP is supplied. Therefore, the experiment does not demonstrate that endogenous A and B associate in unmodified cells.

## 3. Explicit evidence-to-error-to-conclusion chain

1. **Evidence:** Tagged, overexpressed A and B reproducibly co-immunoprecipitate (E1).
2. **Valid inference:** A and B are associated, directly or indirectly, in this engineered co-IP system.
3. **First inferential error:** Treating co-IP as proof of direct molecular contact.
4. **Second inferential error:** Generalizing from tagged, overexpressed proteins to endogenous proteins in unmodified cells.
5. **Missing evidence:** No endogenous co-IP and no purified-protein binding experiment are supplied (E2).
6. **Conclusion:** The full claim is **not supported by the evidence packet**. It is not thereby shown to be false; it remains unverified.
7. **Supported replacement:** “Tagged, overexpressed A and B co-immunoprecipitated reproducibly in three independent cultures.”

## 4. Alternatives and their evaluation

A benign biological possibility is that A truly binds B directly at endogenous levels and that the tagged overexpression assay faithfully reflects the native interaction. Nothing in the packet excludes that possibility. However, E1 alone cannot distinguish it from indirect complex membership or assay-induced association.

The three independent cultures and controls reduce concern about a one-off result or nonspecific IgG recovery, but they do not resolve directness or native context. Input and IgG controls are not substitutes for endogenous validation, tag controls, or purified binding.

## 5. Proposed checks

### Citation check

Verify the original deposited item and audit trail for LS-M17. This could resolve whether C1 is a clerical mismatch or whether a documented identifier relationship is missing.

### Experimental check for the full claim

A two-part validation is needed:

1. **Endogenous-context test:** Demonstrate association of endogenous A and B without introduced tags or overexpression, with appropriate negative and reciprocal controls.
2. **Direct-binding test:** Show binding using purified, untagged A and B in a defined system, preferably with an orthogonal quantitative assay.

A negative endogenous result under validated assay conditions, or failure of purified A and B to bind across appropriate conditions while a positive control succeeds, would argue against the claim. Positive results in both tests would substantially answer the two current evidentiary gaps.

“Unmodified cells” is interpreted as cells without introduced tagged constructs or overexpression. Tag identity and position, expression levels relative to endogenous proteins, lysis conditions, reciprocal co-IP, tag-only controls, nuclease treatment, and quantitative binding parameters are unreported. These omissions limit interpretation but do not themselves establish error or intent.
