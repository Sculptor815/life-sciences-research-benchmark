# Recommended next biological question

**Does the inflammatory-caspase-generated amino-terminal fragment of gasdermin D (GSDMD-NT) directly permeabilize membranes in the absence of any other cellular protein, or does it require another cellular component to execute membrane injury?**

The highest-value next experiment is therefore a **defined, purified-protein membrane reconstitution**, linked to cellular validation. This directly addresses the central gap in the packet: genetic loss and fragment-expression studies establish that GSDMD cleavage and its amino-terminal portion are important for pyroptotic death, but they do not establish the physical executor of membrane damage.

# Competing mechanisms and discriminating predictions

## Mechanism 1: Direct membrane-permeabilization model

**Proposal:** Caspase cleavage liberates GSDMD-NT, which itself binds to, inserts into, or otherwise disrupts lipid bilayers. Membrane injury is executed directly by GSDMD-NT.

### Predictions
1. Purified, authentic GSDMD-NT will cause leakage from **protein-free, composition-defined lipid vesicles**.
2. Activity will occur without cell lysate, cytosol, organelles, or another protein.
3. Leakage will depend on GSDMD-NT concentration and time of exposure.
4. Membrane association will correlate with leakage.
5. A membrane-conductance assay should detect increased permeability if lesions permit ionic flux.
6. GSDMD-NT generated by caspase cleavage of full-length GSDMD should behave similarly to independently purified GSDMD-NT, provided both preparations are authentic and comparably active.
7. Full-length uncleaved GSDMD, the carboxy-terminal fragment, inactive caspase, and buffer should not reproduce the effect under matched conditions.

### Important qualification
A positive result would show that GSDMD-NT is **sufficient** for membrane permeabilization in the tested artificial membranes. It would not alone prove:
- that the same membrane composition is relevant in cells;
- that the observed lesion is a defined protein pore rather than another direct bilayer-disrupting action;
- that cellular cofactors do not regulate localization, threshold, kinetics, or physiological selectivity.

---

## Mechanism 2: Cellular-executioner model

**Proposal:** GSDMD-NT is cytotoxic because it recruits, activates, relocalizes, or derepresses another cellular component that physically damages membranes. GSDMD-NT itself is not membrane-permeabilizing in isolation.

### Predictions
1. Purified GSDMD-NT will not reproducibly permeabilize protein-free defined membranes at concentrations comparable with its active cellular exposure.
2. Addition of a cellular fraction may confer or strongly enhance membrane-permeabilizing activity.
3. Activity should track with a fraction containing the required factor.
4. Depletion of the candidate factor in cells should reduce membrane injury while leaving inflammatory-caspase activation and GSDMD cleavage intact.
5. Reintroduction of the candidate factor should restore injury.

### Important qualification
Failure of GSDMD-NT to damage one artificial membrane composition would not establish this mechanism. The reconstitution may lack a required **non-protein membrane feature**, such as a particular lipid composition, membrane curvature, asymmetry, ionic condition, or other physical property.

---

## Mechanism 3: Indirect cellular-stress model

**Proposal:** GSDMD-NT causes death through an intracellular signaling or organelle-stress event, and plasma-membrane injury is secondary rather than a direct result of GSDMD-NT acting on the plasma membrane.

### Predictions
1. No direct membrane permeabilization in defined bilayers at biologically plausible exposure.
2. In cells, measurable intracellular changes precede bulk plasma-membrane permeability.
3. Cell death may depend on cellular metabolism or signaling conditions absent from cell-free membranes.
4. GSDMD-NT expression may be cytotoxic without a close quantitative relationship between GSDMD-NT abundance and immediate membrane leakage.

---

## Mechanism 4: Experimental-overexpression or protein-quality artifact

**Proposal:** Fragment-expression cytotoxicity reflects nonphysiological concentration, misfolding, aggregation, tagging, or another artifact rather than the normal activity of the caspase-generated fragment.

### Predictions
1. Independently purified GSDMD-NT preparations vary in activity according to aggregation or contaminants.
2. Activity is not reproduced by caspase-generated GSDMD-NT.
3. Apparent membrane leakage occurs only at very high protein concentrations, coincides with visible protein aggregation, or is reproduced by unrelated aggregated protein preparations.
4. Cellular expression causes toxicity only at levels far exceeding endogenous caspase-generated GSDMD-NT.

# Proposed research plan

## Overall design principle

The plan proceeds from the least ambiguous question—whether GSDMD-NT is sufficient for membrane permeabilization in a protein-free system—to more complex cellular experiments. Each stage has explicit quality-control gates. No result below is reported; all are proposed tests.

## Unreported prerequisites and assumptions

The packet does not provide:
- species or GSDMD sequence;
- cleavage-site identity;
- identity of the relevant inflammatory caspase(s);
- fragment boundaries;
- expression system;
- purification method;
- cell type or inflammatory-caspase activation stimulus;
- physiological concentration of cleaved GSDMD-NT;
- membrane composition relevant to the cells in which the original genetic or expression observations were made.

These parameters must be established or selected prospectively and reported. The recommended approach is to use the same species and, if identifiable, the same experimental system underlying the supplied genetic and fragment-expression observations. If that information is unavailable, the study should explicitly define the chosen GSDMD ortholog and model and avoid generalizing beyond them.

## Stage 0. Prespecification, audit trail, and decision criteria

### 0.1 Prespecify primary question and endpoint

**Primary endpoint:** fractional leakage of encapsulated marker from protein-free lipid vesicles after exposure to authenticated GSDMD-NT.

For each vesicle condition:

\[
\text{fractional leakage} =
\frac{F_{\text{protein}} - F_{\text{buffer}}}
{F_{\text{complete lysis}} - F_{\text{buffer}}}
\]

where:
- \(F_{\text{buffer}}\) is spontaneous leakage from matched vesicles;
- \(F_{\text{complete lysis}}\) is fluorescence after an independently validated full-lysis calibration treatment;
- all values are corrected for baseline fluorescence and dilution.

The exact fluorophore, vesicle composition, temperature, buffer, and sampling times are unreported in the packet and must be prespecified before data collection.

### 0.2 Define the primary direct-sufficiency criterion

Conclude that GSDMD-NT is **biochemically sufficient to permeabilize the tested membranes** only if all of the following are met:

1. The GSDMD-NT preparation passes identity and purity criteria.
2. Protein-free vesicles show reproducible leakage above matched buffer and control-protein conditions.
3. The effect is reproduced with independently prepared protein and independently prepared vesicle batches.
4. Leakage is associated with GSDMD-NT concentration and/or exposure time in a prespecified model.
5. Controls—uncleaved full-length GSDMD, carboxy-terminal fragment, caspase alone, inactive caspase, and buffer—do not produce a comparable effect under matched conditions.
6. At least one orthogonal membrane-injury measurement supports the fluorescence result, such as electrical conductance or direct visualization of release from individual giant vesicles.
7. The active protein state is not explained by gross aggregation, residual detergent, or a purification contaminant.

This criterion deliberately establishes **sufficiency in vitro**, not exclusive physiological necessity.

### 0.3 Independent units and replication

- **Biochemical independent unit:** an independently purified protein preparation, prepared on a different occasion from a separate expression culture or purification batch.
- **Membrane independent unit:** an independently prepared vesicle batch.
- **Cellular independent unit:** an independently initiated cell culture experiment performed on a separate day.
- Technical replicate wells or repeated measurements from the same protein/vesicle preparation are not independent biological replicates and will be analyzed as nested measurements.

Minimum exploratory replication should include at least three independent protein preparations and three independent vesicle preparations. Formal sample size should then be set using pilot estimates of batch-to-batch variance and a prespecified effect size of biological interest. The packet provides no variance estimates, so a defensible power calculation cannot be completed now.

### 0.4 Allocation and blinding

- Randomize vesicle batches, protein preparations, concentrations, and control conditions across plates or instrument runs.
- Use coded sample identifiers for imaging and electrophysiology.
- Blind the analyst to condition identity until prespecified quality-control checks and analysis scripts are complete.
- Keep a date-stamped experimental ledger recording protein batch, vesicle batch, operator, instrument, temperature, buffer lot, and deviations.

Blinding is especially important for image-based assessments and manual electrophysiology event calling.

## Stage 1. Generate and authenticate relevant GSDMD species

### Goal
Ensure that the molecule tested in a defined membrane assay is genuinely the caspase-generated amino-terminal product rather than a truncation, tag artifact, contaminant, or aggregate.

### 1.1 Prepare four core reagents

1. **Full-length GSDMD**
2. **GSDMD-NT**, expressed or otherwise isolated as the cleavage-defined amino-terminal product
3. **GSDMD-CT**, the complementary carboxy-terminal product
4. **Caspase-generated GSDMD-NT**, produced by cleavage of purified full-length GSDMD with the relevant inflammatory caspase

The exact fragment boundary should be defined experimentally from the cleavage product. If the cleavage site is known from the originating system, it should nevertheless be verified for the purified preparation.

### 1.2 Authenticate each reagent

For every protein batch, document:

- apparent molecular mass by gel-based analysis;
- intact mass and/or peptide mapping sufficient to verify fragment identity and boundary;
- relative purity;
- concentration measured by a calibrated method;
- presence or removal of purification tags;
- aggregation state, for example by size-based separation or an orthogonal particle/aggregation measurement;
- buffer composition and any detergent or surfactant carried through purification.

**Critical control:** If a tag is used for purification, compare tagged and tag-removed protein where technically feasible. A tag can alter membrane interactions.

### 1.3 Validate caspase processing

Incubate full-length GSDMD with:
- active inflammatory caspase;
- matched inactive or inhibited caspase;
- buffer alone.

Measure loss of full-length GSDMD and appearance of the expected amino- and carboxy-terminal products. Do not proceed to mechanistic membrane interpretation if the cleavage reaction cannot be shown to produce the intended species.

### Stage 1 stop rule

Pause the membrane study if:
- fragment identity cannot be verified;
- substantial aggregation or residual detergent cannot be excluded;
- active caspase does not reproducibly generate the intended cleavage product;
- independently prepared GSDMD-NT batches differ grossly in integrity.

In that case, optimize protein production before interpreting any membrane result.

## Stage 2. Protein-free membrane leakage assay

### Goal
Test whether authentic GSDMD-NT alone is sufficient to release solute from lipid bilayers.

### 2.1 Construct a prespecified membrane panel

Prepare **protein-free**, composition-defined vesicles. Because the packet does not identify the relevant membrane composition, use a staged panel rather than a single membrane type:

1. A simple baseline bilayer composition.
2. A composition intended to approximate a generic cellular plasma membrane.
3. A composition differing in charge or headgroup content.
4. A composition differing in membrane order or curvature propensity.

The exact lipid identities and molar ratios must be selected before the experiment and reported. They should not be changed after observing initial results without labeling the expanded panel as exploratory.

This panel is necessary because a negative result in only one bilayer composition would be weak evidence: GSDMD-NT could act directly but require a particular physical membrane environment.

### 2.2 Calibrate the vesicle assay

For every vesicle batch:

1. Encapsulate a fluorescent soluble marker.
2. Remove external marker.
3. Measure baseline fluorescence and spontaneous leakage over the full planned assay duration.
4. Apply a validated complete-lysis treatment to define maximal release.
5. Confirm vesicle concentration, approximate size distribution, and reproducibility of encapsulation.
6. Confirm that the assay responds appropriately to the complete-lysis calibration treatment.

If size-selective leakage is to be measured, encapsulate or co-encapsulate markers of different hydrodynamic sizes. This helps distinguish small-ion or small-solute permeability from larger discontinuities, but does not by itself prove a pore architecture.

### 2.3 Core comparison matrix

For each membrane composition, test a concentration and time series of:

| Condition | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Buffer only | Defines spontaneous leakage. |
| GSDMD-NT | Primary test of direct sufficiency. |
| Full-length uncleaved GSDMD | Tests whether cleavage-dependent release of the amino-terminal region matters. |
| GSDMD-CT | Tests specificity for the amino-terminal portion. |
| Active inflammatory caspase alone | Excludes caspase-mediated vesicle effects. |
| Inactive/inhibited caspase alone | Matched enzyme control. |
| Full-length GSDMD + active caspase | Tests whether cleavage generated in situ produces activity. |
| Full-length GSDMD + inactive/inhibited caspase | Controls for nonproteolytic effects. |
| Caspase-generated GSDMD-NT after cleavage verification | Compares authentic cleavage product with separately prepared GSDMD-NT. |

All protein conditions should be concentration-matched as far as molecular form allows. The total added volume and buffer composition must be matched between conditions.

### 2.4 Measurements

**Primary measurement**
- Fractional dye leakage at prespecified time points.

**Secondary measurements**
- Initial rate of leakage.
- Concentration-response relationship.
- Time to a prespecified leakage level.
- Size-selective release, if multiple markers are used.
- Protein association with membrane fractions after flotation, sedimentation, or a comparably appropriate separation method.
- Protein behavior in the absence of vesicles, including aggregation after incubation under assay conditions.

### 2.5 Analysis

Use a hierarchical analysis that accounts for the nested structure:

- repeated time points nested within technical replicate;
- technical replicates nested within vesicle batch;
- vesicle batches crossed with protein preparations.

Estimate effect sizes and confidence intervals for GSDMD-NT relative to buffer and each control. Model concentration and time as prespecified continuous variables where possible rather than performing only many pairwise comparisons. Treat plate, vesicle batch, and protein batch as random or blocking effects as appropriate.

The primary analysis should remain focused on the predeclared membrane composition(s). Results from additional compositions should be clearly identified as confirmatory only if prospectively included, otherwise exploratory.

### Stage 2 troubleshooting

| Observation | Likely concern | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| All conditions leak strongly | Vesicle instability, solvent/detergent contamination, handling damage | Reprepare vesicles; measure spontaneous leakage; inspect buffer components; verify detergent removal. |
| Only one protein batch is active | Aggregation, contaminant, batch-specific proteolysis | Re-authenticate active and inactive batches; compare aggregation; repeat purification. |
| Active caspase alone causes leakage | Enzyme preparation or buffer effect | Purify or buffer-exchange caspase; repeat with inactive enzyme and matched storage buffer. |
| Full-length plus caspase is active but isolated GSDMD-NT is not | Fragment isolation may damage protein; cleavage mixture may contain another active feature | Compare cleaved product identity, concentration, purity, and aggregation; isolate cleavage product if feasible. |
| No leakage in any condition | Incorrect fragment, inactive protein, inadequate membrane panel, unsuitable buffer | Verify fragment activity in cells; test broader prespecified membrane conditions; confirm assay calibration; assess protein state. |
| Leakage only at high concentration with aggregation | Non-specific disruption possible | Quantify aggregate fraction, test monomer-enriched fraction, and compare exposure with cellular GSDMD-NT levels. |

## Stage 3. Orthogonal physical tests of membrane injury

A fluorescent leakage assay alone can be distorted by fluorophore effects, vesicle instability, or optical artifacts. At least one orthogonal assay should be used.

### 3.1 Single-vesicle imaging

Use large, individually observable vesicles containing a fluorescent lumenal marker. Add coded protein samples and record:
- time of protein association, if trace-labeled protein has been validated not to alter activity;
- time and extent of lumenal release;
- vesicle collapse, rupture, or stable partial permeability.

**Control:** The primary conclusion must also be supported with unlabeled GSDMD-NT. Fluorescent labeling is only a localization aid unless validated as functionally neutral.

### 3.2 Electrical conductance across a planar bilayer

Measure current before and after addition of authenticated GSDMD-NT and controls.

Relevant measurements:
- appearance of conductance events;
- conductance magnitude and lifetime;
- dependence on protein concentration;
- ion-condition dependence;
- comparison with full-length GSDMD, GSDMD-CT, and caspase alone.

This assay can support formation of ion-permeable lesions. It cannot, by itself, specify protein stoichiometry or prove that every event in cells has the same physical structure.

### 3.3 Membrane-bound GSDMD-NT state

Assess whether membrane exposure changes the physical state of GSDMD-NT, for example by membrane association and an orthogonal assessment of membrane-associated protein assemblies. This is supportive rather than decisive: association or oligomerization may accompany direct injury, but neither alone proves causation.

## Stage 4. Establish biological plausibility in cells

### Goal
Determine whether the concentration and behavior of membrane-active GSDMD-NT in vitro are compatible with the cellular phenomenon described in the packet.

### 4.1 Cellular system

Use a cell system in which:
- inflammatory-caspase activation can be induced by a laboratory-validated method;
- endogenous GSDMD status can be measured;
- GSDMD genetic loss or engineered depletion is available;
- membrane injury can be monitored over time.

The packet does not identify a cell type or activation protocol. The chosen model and stimulus are therefore proposed parameters, not reported methods.

### 4.2 Core cellular conditions

In a GSDMD-deficient background, compare rescue with:
1. full-length cleavage-competent GSDMD;
2. a cleavage-resistant full-length GSDMD, constructed only after the cleavage site is experimentally defined;
3. GSDMD-NT;
4. GSDMD-CT;
5. empty-vector control.

For full-length constructs, induce inflammatory caspase activation. For GSDMD-NT, use controlled expression or delivery and avoid assuming that overexpression level is physiological.

### 4.3 Cellular measurements

Measure in the same experimental series:

- full-length GSDMD and fragment abundance;
- timing and extent of GSDMD cleavage;
- membrane permeability using a membrane-impermeant marker;
- release of a cytosolic marker, if technically available;
- cell morphology and kinetics by live imaging;
- protein abundance of GSDMD-NT relative to the amount produced after inflammatory-caspase activation in matched cells.

The key calibration is to compare fragment abundance in the GSDMD-NT rescue condition with the abundance of caspase-generated endogenous or rescued GSDMD-NT. Without this comparison, fragment-expression toxicity may remain an overexpression artifact.

### 4.4 Cellular controls and interpretation constraints

A cellular loss-of-function effect on membrane injury supports GSDMD pathway importance only if:
- inflammatory-caspase activation remains intact;
- GSDMD cleavage is preserved where appropriate;
- the perturbation does not simply prevent GSDMD-NT production or grossly impair cell viability before pathway activation.

If a future candidate cofactor is depleted, these upstream checks are essential before assigning it a direct executioner role.

---

## Stage 5. Conditional cofactor-discovery branch

This branch is justified if Stage 2 finds no robust direct activity, or only weak activity at implausibly high concentrations, while cellular GSDMD-NT remains clearly cytotoxic.

### 5.1 Test for complementation by cellular material

Add defined amounts of cellular fractions to the otherwise protein-free vesicle assay:

- cytosolic fraction;
- membrane-associated fraction;
- fraction-depleted controls;
- each fraction alone;
- GSDMD-NT alone;
- GSDMD-CT plus fraction;
- buffer plus fraction.

Fractions should be prepared from a source compatible with the cellular system and should be tested for intrinsic membrane-disrupting activity before interpretation.

### 5.2 Fractionation logic

If a fraction confers GSDMD-NT-dependent leakage:

1. Confirm reproducibility with independent fraction preparations.
2. Determine whether the activity is sensitive to conditions expected to affect a protein component versus a non-protein component.
3. Fractionate while tracking the **combination-dependent** activity, not merely total fraction toxicity.
4. Identify enriched candidate components.
5. Test purified or recombinantly produced candidate(s) with GSDMD-NT in the defined membrane assay.
6. Test necessity in cells by depletion and rescue.

### 5.3 Stringent criterion for a cofactor conclusion

A cofactor should be called a required executioner only if:
- it restores activity with GSDMD-NT in a defined system;
- it is necessary for the relevant cellular membrane-injury phenotype;
- depletion does not block upstream inflammatory-caspase activity or GSDMD cleavage;
- re-expression restores the phenotype.

A fraction that merely enhances leakage is not enough to establish necessity or direct execution.

# Conditional outcomes and strongest justified conclusions

## Outcome A: GSDMD-NT reproducibly permeabilizes protein-free bilayers

**Pattern**
- Authenticated GSDMD-NT causes concentration- and time-dependent leakage.
- Full-length GSDMD, GSDMD-CT, caspase alone, inactive caspase, and buffer do not.
- Caspase cleavage of full-length GSDMD generates a similarly active product.
- Orthogonal conductance and/or single-vesicle experiments agree.
- Activity occurs at an exposure compatible with cellular GSDMD-NT abundance.

**Strongest justified conclusion**

GSDMD-NT is **biochemically sufficient to directly permeabilize the tested lipid membranes**. This supports the direct membrane-permeabilization model and argues against an obligatory additional cellular protein for the basic physical injury measured in vitro.

**Not justified**
- That GSDMD-NT necessarily forms one specific pore structure.
- That all membrane damage in pyroptotic cells is produced without regulatory cofactors.
- That the tested artificial membrane is identical to the relevant cellular membrane.

---

## Outcome B: GSDMD-NT is inactive alone but becomes active with a reproducible cellular fraction

**Pattern**
- No direct leakage in a broad, quality-controlled membrane panel.
- GSDMD-NT-dependent leakage appears only in the presence of a specific fraction.
- The fraction alone is inactive or much less active.
- Fraction activity tracks through purification.
- A candidate reconstitutes the effect and is required in cells without preventing upstream cleavage.

**Strongest justified conclusion**

The data support a model in which GSDMD-NT requires at least one additional component for the membrane-permeabilizing activity measured under these conditions. If the candidate is fully reconstituted and genetically validated, that component can be assigned a necessary mechanistic role.

**Not justified before full reconstitution**
- That the active fraction contains a single required protein.
- That the factor is necessarily the direct membrane-damaging entity rather than a regulator of GSDMD-NT.

---

## Outcome C: No direct activity in vitro, but GSDMD-NT remains cytotoxic in cells

**Pattern**
- Purified protein is authentic.
- Multiple defined membrane compositions show no convincing leakage.
- GSDMD-NT expression or endogenous cleavage remains associated with cellular death.

**Strongest justified conclusion**

The supplied evidence plus the proposed result would not establish direct membrane permeabilization by GSDMD-NT. The remaining possibilities include a missing membrane property, a required cellular cofactor, inappropriate purified-protein conformation, or indirect cellular stress.

**Not justified**
- “GSDMD-NT does not directly damage membranes.”
- “A cellular cofactor is proven.”
A negative cell-free result is only as strong as the breadth and quality of the reconstitution conditions.

---

## Outcome D: Direct leakage occurs only at very high concentrations or with aggregated protein

**Pattern**
- Leakage requires concentrations greatly above cellular GSDMD-NT estimates.
- Activity tracks with protein aggregation or a minor impure fraction.
- Monomer-enriched or tag-removed protein is inactive.

**Strongest justified conclusion**

The experiment would show possible non-specific membrane disruption by the preparation, but would not support a physiologically relevant direct-executor model. Protein quality and cellular concentration matching become the next priority.

---

## Outcome E: Results differ by membrane composition

**Pattern**
- GSDMD-NT is active only on a subset of defined bilayers.

**Strongest justified conclusion**

GSDMD-NT may directly permeabilize membranes in a composition-dependent manner. The next biological question becomes which membrane feature confers susceptibility and whether that feature is present and accessible in the relevant cells.

This result would be more informative than a result from one membrane composition, but it still would not identify the lesion architecture or prove physiological localization.

---

# Final conclusion

The packet supports the conclusion that inflammatory-caspase cleavage of GSDMD and the resulting amino-terminal region are linked to pyroptotic death. It does **not** identify the physical membrane-damaging mechanism.

The most valuable next question is therefore whether authentic GSDMD-NT alone can permeabilize a defined lipid bilayer. A rigorously controlled purified-protein reconstitution—using authenticated cleavage products, protein-free membranes, matched inactive controls, orthogonal physical readouts, independent preparations, and cellular concentration calibration—most efficiently separates direct membrane execution from cofactor-dependent or indirect models.
