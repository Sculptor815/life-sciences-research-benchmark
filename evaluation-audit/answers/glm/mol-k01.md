Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

# Recommendation

The **50-kDa band** carries genetic specificity support. The 75-kDa band must remain unassigned. The best-supported interpretation of the full experiment is: the antibody detects the target protein (true signal) at 50 kDa and a non-target band at 75 kDa whose persistence is unexplained by this experiment alone.

---

## 1. The observation-to-inference chain

**Observation (E1a):** A validated biallelic deletion of the entire target coding sequence exists, and loading is comparable across lanes.

**Observation (E1b):** Loss of the 50-kDa band in the knockout (KO); the 75-kDa band is unchanged.

**Inference chain for the 50-kDa band:**

1. The genetic perturbation is specific: biallelic, validated, and it removes only the target's coding sequence.
2. Loss of the target gene should abolish the target protein itself (or at minimum the full-length epitope-containing species), because there is no template to produce it.
3. The 50-kDa band disappears under exactly the condition that should abolish the target product, with loading controlled.
4. Therefore, the 50-kDa band's presence depends on the target gene — the minimal definition of genetic specificity for an antibody signal. The band is *causally coupled* to the target locus.

**Inference chain for the 75-kDa band:**

1. The 75-kDa band is unaffected by removal of the target coding sequence.
2. Therefore, the 75-kDa signal is not dependent on the target gene.
3. Therefore it cannot be assigned to the target protein on genetic evidence; it is, at best, an unassigned band and, conservatively, treated as non-specific until proven otherwise.

**Conclusion:** Gene-dependence is the gold-standard criterion for antibody specificity in immunoblotting. Only the 50-kDa band meets it.

## 2. Loss of antigen vs. indirect effects — the critical distinction

The KO experiment directly demonstrates **loss of the antigen** (the molecular species the antibody binds) for the 50-kDa band. This is stronger than showing a downstream phenotypic effect. But the chain deserves scrutiny:

- **What the deletion proves:** absence of a 50-kDa immunoreactive species. This is consistent with the target protein being the antigen.
- **What it does not prove:** that the 50-kDa band runs at the target's expected molecular weight for reasons of identity rather than coincidence, nor that the band is the full-length protein (it could be a target-dependent fragment — still target-dependent, but worth noting).
- **Indirect-effect alternative for the 50-kDa band:** in principle, deleting the target could secondarily eliminate an unrelated 50-kDa protein — e.g., if the target stabilizes that protein or regulates its expression. This alternative is logically possible but *less parsimonious* here: antibody staining detects epitopes, and co-regulation producing an exactly co-migrating band is an unparsimonious coincidence. Still, it is the main residual caveat on the 50-kDa assignment.
- **Indirect effect does NOT rescue the 75-kDa band:** even if the deletion caused broad transcriptional or proteomic changes, the 75-kDa band is *unchanged*. No indirect mechanism is needed or invoked for it; the experiment simply provides no positive evidence linking it to the target. A band that survives deletion of the target locus is, by construction, not the target gene product itself (the target cannot be produced from a deleted coding sequence).

**Key conceptual point:** genetic specificity is established by *coupling between the genotype and the signal*. The 50-kDa band is coupled; the 75-kDa band is decoupled. Loading comparability (E1) rules out the trivial alternative that the 50-kDa loss reflects unequal protein amounts.

## 3. Why the 75-kDa band remains unassigned

- It persists without its candidate source: no target-coding template exists in the KO, yet signal remains.
- Plausible but **unproven** explanations include: cross-reactivity of the antibody with an unrelated 75-kDa protein sharing an epitope; a dimer/complex, isoform, or post-translationally modified species whose epitope does not require the deleted coding sequence (e.g., generated by another gene product); or a detection artifact. The evidence packet contains no data distinguishing among these.
- Common practice would tempt one to label the 75-kDa band a "dimer" or "isoform." That inference is **not licensed** by deletion data alone: a genuine dimer of the target should also disappear (or collapse) when the target is absent. Assigning it would require positive evidence, which is absent.
- Therefore the correct status is **unassigned non-specific signal** — reported honestly in figures, not quantified as target.

## 4. Orthogonal validation with interpretable outcomes

Each proposal below is labeled as a **proposed experiment** with predicted outcomes and their interpretations.

**A. Knock-in of an epitope-tagged target (tag at endogenous locus).**
- *Predicted if 50-kDa = target:* anti-tag antibody detects a band at 50 kDa; anti-target antibody signal at 50 kDa shifts by tag mass or is immunoprecipitated by anti-tag.
- *Interpretation:* co-migration/co-capture of the target-antibody signal with the tag is near-conclusive identity evidence.
- *If anti-tag shows nothing at 50 kDa:* the 50-kDa band is target-dependent but not the full-length target (fragment or indirect effect) — this would materially change the conclusion.

**B. Independent antibody raised to a non-overlapping epitope.**
- *Predicted if 50-kDa = target:* the second antibody detects a 50-kDa band that is likewise absent in the KO.
- *Predicted for the 75-kDa band:* if the second antibody also detects a 75-kDa band absent in KO, it may be a genuine target species (e.g., modified form); if not, the 75-kDa signal is antibody-lineage-specific cross-reactivity.
- *Interpretation:* concordance across epitopes strengthens identity; discordance localizes the artifact.

**C. Knockdown/rescue or graded expression.**
- Partial depletion of the target should reduce the 50-kDa band dose-dependently; rescue with a deletion-variant cDNA should restore it. *Interpretation:* dose-response plus rescue closes the "indirect effect" loophole, since an unrelated co-regulated protein would not be restored by re-expressing the target.

**D. Size-mapping of the 75-kDa species (deglycosylation, denaturing conditions, IP-MS of the 75-kDa band).**
- Mass spectrometry of the excised 75-kDa band directly identifies its protein. *Outcome:* either an unrelated protein (band formally assigned as cross-reactive) or a target-related species, which would then require explaining how it survives coding-sequence deletion (e.g., another locus encoding a similar epitope).

**Priority:** A and B give the cleanest yes/no answers for the 50-kDa band; D gives a definitive answer for the 75-kDa band.

## 5. Limits and consequential uncertainties

- "Validated biallelic deletion" is taken as given; if validation were incomplete (e.g., an in-frame residual fragment), the 50-kDa inference would weaken. The packet states the deletion spans the entire coding sequence, so this risk is low.
- Loading control comparability rules out gross loading artifacts but not protein-transfer differences; a stripped/reprobed membrane or total-protein stain would fully close this.
- The packet provides no expected molecular weight of the target; if 50 kDa conflicts with prediction, a target-dependent fragment or processing event must be considered (still genetically specific, but a different molecular model).
- **Unreported parameters (assumptions stated explicitly):** antibody epitope location, expected target size, membrane/transfer method, and whether the same lysate was used across conditions are all assumed but not evidenced.

**Bottom line:** report the 50-kDa band as the target-associated signal (with the standard caveat that knock-in tagging would make this conclusive), report the 75-kDa band as unassigned, and use experiments A/B (for the 50-kDa band) and D (for the 75-kDa band) to convert inference into identity.
