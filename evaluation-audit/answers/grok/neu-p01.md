The dossier matches the cited article record, but it does not establish that projection P is necessary for learning. Treat the necessity claim as unsupported and currently unauditable. The retraction notice documents an unresolved animal-identity failure; it does not show that the biology is false, nor does it establish intent.

## What the notice does and does not establish
N1 states that animal identities could not be reconciled across acquisition files. That establishes a traceability failure for subject linkage. Analyses that require each acquisition file to map to a known animal, and each animal to one independent group, cannot be verified from the packet.

N1 explicitly does not determine the truth of every biological claim. A notice, missing file, or suspicious feature alone does not establish intent, fabrication, falsification, or that the reported performance difference is false. The direction of any bias is unknown: an identity mix-up could inflate a difference, shrink it, or leave a real effect intact. E2 makes the gap operational: the allocation file and raw animal identities needed to verify independent groups are unavailable, so the concern cannot be closed from the supplied evidence.

## Evidence → inference → conclusion
1. **Identity of the source record.** Evidence: C1, full citation match to NS-12. Inference: the claim being assessed is the claim in that dossier-certified article, not a mismatched source. Conclusion: record identity inside the dossier is adequate; dataset integrity is a separate question.

2. **What the article actually reports.** Evidence: E1, lower learned performance after projection inhibition. Inference: a decrease is compatible with P contributing to performance. It is not evidence that learning cannot occur without P. Strict necessity would require failure of acquisition, not merely lower performance, plus timing that hits learning rather than expression, specificity controls, and independent experimental units. Those parameters are unreported. Conclusion: even if E1 were auditable, necessity would not follow from “lower learned performance” alone.

3. **Why E1 cannot currently carry the claim.** Evidence: N1 (identities not reconciled) and E2 (allocation file and raw identities unavailable). Inference: the reported difference presupposes that animals were correctly and independently assigned. That presupposition is unchecked. Non-independence or crossed identities would make the statistical comparison uninterpretable. Conclusion: E1 is not a reliable premise for a causal necessity claim until group independence can be verified. This is a verification failure, not a demonstrated biological refutation.

4. **Joint conclusion.** Source-record identity is matched (C1). The necessity assertion is not established (E1 is too weak; N1 and E2 remove auditability). The notice limits confidence; it does not license a finding of misconduct or a finding that P is unnecessary.

## Error, separated from intent
The primary error risk is inferential: an unverifiable experimental unit. If the same animal, session, or mislinked file entered more than one “group,” the comparison in E1 would not estimate an effect of inhibiting P. A second, independent error is logical overreach: moving from a performance decrement to necessity. These can be true even if every investigator acted in good faith. Intent is not established and is not required to withhold the necessity conclusion.

## Benign alternatives
For the notice and missing files: renamed acquisition exports, a lost crosswalk after a system or personnel change, cage-card or tag replacement, an incomplete archive transfer, or a conservative retraction after honest inability to reconcile IDs. Any of these can produce N1 and E2 without a false biological result.

For a real performance drop, if one exists: non-specific effects of inhibition (movement, motivation, sensation, arousal); impaired expression or retrieval rather than failed learning; a contributory but redundant projection; or sampling variation. “Lower” also leaves open residual learning, which would count against strict necessity.

## What would change the recommendation
- Reconciliation that shows unique animals, one arm each, assigned before outcomes, with the analyzed cohort matching the allocated cohort: the identity concern for that dataset would be disproved. Necessity would still be unproven without acquisition-specific, controlled evidence.
- Reconciliation that shows duplicates or crossed arms: treat the reported difference as unreliable.
- A pre-registered independent replication with locked identities that abolishes acquisition while sparing performance controls: would support necessity without rehabilitating the original unauditable comparison.
- Such a replication that finds preserved learning: would disconfirm necessity as a general claim.

## Check that could disprove the concern
**Proposed audit, not a completed result:** recover the allocation file and raw identity keys and build a reconciliation table from each acquisition file to one animal ID and one arm assigned before outcome measurement. The identity concern is disproved for this dataset only if every file maps to exactly one animal, no animal appears in more than one arm, arm labels match the pre-outcome allocation, and the analyzed set equals the allocated set without undeclared exclusions. Failure to recover the files does not confirm misconduct or falsify the biology. Passing this check does not prove necessity.

**Assumption:** “independent groups” means animals, not sessions or neurons, are the experimental units for the learning comparison. If the article used a different unit, the same check must be restated for that unit. Sample size, inhibition method, timing, outcome definition, and exclusions are unreported and are not filled in here.
