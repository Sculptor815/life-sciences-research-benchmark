# Grok 4.7 / mol-r03

Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

The most valuable next question is whether the amino-terminal gasdermin D fragment is itself sufficient to injure a protein-free lipid bilayer, or whether membrane injury still requires another cellular component. That question is the missing branch point: cellular genetic-loss and fragment-expression results can link cleavage to death without identifying the molecule that breaks the membrane.

Current justified conclusion from the packet alone: inflammatory-caspase cleavage of gasdermin D is linked to pyroptotic death, and the amino-terminal portion is cytotoxic in the cellular expression context. Those results do not establish the fragment’s physical action and do not show that the fragment, rather than another cellular component, executes membrane injury. No purified-protein membrane result is available to close that gap.

## Evidence, inference, and limits

**E1.** Inflammatory caspases cleave gasdermin D.  
**I1.** Cleavage can generate defined gasdermin D products during inflammatory-caspase activity.  
**Limit.** The packet does not name the caspase, the cleavage site, the ortholog, the cell type, stoichiometry, or whether other substrates are cleaved in parallel. “Inflammatory caspases cleave gasdermin D” is not evidence that cleavage is the only caspase function required for death.

**E2.** Genetic-loss and fragment-expression experiments link this processing to pyroptotic cell death.  
**I2.** In the systems those experiments used, gasdermin D processing is causally implicated in pyroptotic death, not merely correlated by biochemistry.  
**Limit.** The packet asserts that link but does not report alleles, effect sizes, kinetics, or how membrane failure was measured. The strength of necessity cannot be re-graded from this packet. “Link” should not be upgraded to “sole cause in every context.”

**E3.** The amino-terminal portion carries cytotoxic activity.  
**I3.** An amino-terminal fragment is sufficient for cytotoxicity when present in the cells used for fragment expression.  
**Limit.** Those cells contain proteins, lipids, ions, and organelles. Cellular sufficiency does not identify the injured structure, does not prove a pore, and does not exclude a cofactor. The carboxy-terminal portion’s activity is not reported. Exact fragment boundaries are not reported.

**E4.** No subsequent purified-protein membrane experiments are supplied. The packet states that the observations do not reveal the fragment’s physical action or whether another cellular component executes membrane injury.  
**I4.** Direct protein-autonomous membrane injury and indirect execution cannot be distinguished with the supplied evidence.  
**C.** The decisive next question is sufficiency of the amino-terminal fragment for injury of a membrane that contains no other protein.

Relationship implied by the packet, with the unknown step marked:

inflammatory caspase → cleavage of gasdermin D → amino-terminal portion → **unresolved physical action** → membrane injury and pyroptotic death.

The cytotoxic amino-terminal portion is the correct object of the next test because fragment expression, not only caspase activity, is already tied to cytotoxicity. A further genetic screen would not by itself show which molecule contacts the bilayer.

## Competing mechanisms and discriminating predictions

**M1. Direct, protein-autonomous membrane injury.**  
Cleavage liberates an amino-terminal fragment that binds lipid and creates an aqueous membrane lesion without another protein. The lesion could be a size-limited pore or a less selective disruption; those are subclasses, not separate first questions.  
**Prediction.** Quality-controlled amino-terminal fragment, and full-length gasdermin D only after inflammatory-caspase cleavage, permeabilize protein-free liposomes. Uncleaved full-length protein, the carboxy-terminal portion, and caspase alone do not. Activity co-fractionates with the amino-terminal species across an orthogonal purification. Membrane binding accompanies leakage. Another cellular protein is absent, so it cannot be required under those conditions.

**M2. Amino-terminal fragment necessary in cells, but another cellular component executes membrane injury.**  
The fragment may recruit, activate, or organize an unknown protein executor.  
**Prediction.** The same quality-controlled fragment does not permeabilize protein-free membranes across a pre-locked lipid and ionic panel, including cleavage performed on the liposomes. Adding protein-containing material from gasdermin D-deficient cells restores fragment-dependent permeabilization. Fragment-only binding without leakage is compatible with this model but does not prove it.

**M3. Primary cytotoxicity is not bilayer permeabilization.**  
Membrane failure in dying cells could be secondary to another fragment-driven injury.  
**Prediction.** Protein-free membranes are not permeabilized, cofactor reconstitution fails, and cellular death can be uncoupled from early aqueous membrane leakage. The packet does not report the timing or solute selectivity of cellular membrane failure, so M3 cannot be assessed from the packet.

**M4. Direct injury, but only in a membrane context not captured by a narrow liposome recipe.**  
This is the main false-negative version of M1, not a cofactor model. Missing variables could be anionic lipid, cholesterol, curvature, pH, or divalent ions. No cellular lipid composition is supplied, so none of these can be assumed to be the relevant context.  
**Prediction.** Permeabilization appears only in part of a pre-specified panel. That outcome supports conditional direct sufficiency, not general membrane injury, and does not identify the cellular membrane.

**M5. Cleavage relieves carboxy-terminal restraint, but the isolated amino-terminal polypeptide used in vitro is not in the active conformation.**  
**Prediction.** Recombinant amino-terminal fragment is inactive, whereas inflammatory-caspase cleavage of full-length gasdermin D on liposomes permeabilizes them, and a cleavage-resistant mutant, once the site is mapped, does not. This would still support direct injury by the cleavage product and would invalidate a negative conclusion drawn only from the recombinant fragment.

**M6. Fragment-expression cytotoxicity is an overexpression artifact unrelated to caspase-generated fragment.**  
Genetic loss already makes a pure artifact account unlikely for the cellular pathway, but it does not prove that the expressed fragment and the caspase-generated fragment act by the same physical route.  
**Prediction.** Caspase-cleaved gasdermin D and the expression-defined amino-terminal fragment disagree in the membrane assay, or the purified preparation that matches the expressed fragment no longer tracks cytotoxicity. Discordance makes a mechanistic conclusion ambiguous until the preparations are reconciled.

These mechanisms are not all mutually exclusive. A fragment could be sufficient to permeabilize some bilayers and still be regulated by cellular proteins. Sufficiency answers whether another protein is required. It does not answer whether other proteins modulate dose, location, or timing in cells.

## Why this experiment is first

A protein-free membrane is the only system in which “another cellular component” is actually absent. Cellular imaging can show lysis without identifying the executor. An interaction screen can nominate cofactors for M2 but cannot prove they are required for the physical lesion. Structure of the fragment in detergent can show oligomers without proving membrane injury. Lipid identity, pore size, and oligomer stoichiometry are consequential only after, or together with, a test of sufficiency. They are secondary measurements in the plan below, not the primary question.

What would change this recommendation: if purified-protein membrane experiments had already been supplied, the next question would be physical subclass or cellular regulation. If fragment expression were not cytotoxic, the next question would be which caspase substrate matters. If the packet showed that the amino-terminal portion never reaches membranes, a cofactor or non-membrane target hunt would outrank liposomes. None of those results are in the packet.

## Proposed research plan

All steps below are proposals. No result from this plan is claimed to have been observed. Author methods for purification, liposomes, cleavage-site mapping, and membrane assays are not in the packet and are not imputed to any prior study. Thresholds are proposed decision rules, to be locked before the decisive comparison, not measured values.

### 0. Lock the question and the decision rules

Before producing decisive data, write and date a one-page protocol lock:

- Primary question: is the amino-terminal gasdermin D fragment sufficient to permeabilize protein-free liposomes under the locked panel?
- Primary endpoint: fractional release of a small encapsulated aqueous tracer at one pre-specified time, normalized to matched detergent maximum and buffer baseline.
- Primary contrasts: amino-terminal fragment versus uncleaved full-length gasdermin D; amino-terminal versus carboxy-terminal fragment; full-length plus inflammatory caspase versus each alone.
- Secondary endpoints, not substitutes for the primary claim: large-tracer release, turbidity, membrane binding, and oligomer assays.
- Positive, negative, and ambiguous rules in section “Conditional conclusions” below.
- Exclusion rules: failed calibration, impurity, detergent contamination, or losses logged before unblinding.
- A separate calibration pilot may estimate variance and set the number of independent units. Pilot observations must not be entered into the primary contrast.

Assumption A1, labeled as an assumption: aqueous tracer release reports a bilayer lesion large enough for that tracer. It misses lipid scrambling without an aqueous path. Turbidity and a second tracer are included for that reason. Assumption A2: a protein-free liposome is a valid test of whether another protein is required. It is not a replica of the plasma membrane.

### 1. Prerequisites: define the molecular species

Unreported parameters that must be established rather than invented: ortholog; inflammatory caspase identity; cleavage-site residue; amino-acid boundaries of the cytotoxic amino-terminal portion; whether a tag was present in the expression experiments; cellular concentration; lipid composition of the injured cell membrane.

1.1 Recover the fragment boundaries used in the expression experiments that showed amino-terminal cytotoxicity. If those boundaries were not recorded, map the inflammatory-caspase cleavage products by Edman sequencing or mass spectrometry of the cleaved protein. Do not choose a site from outside the packet and treat it as known.

1.2 Prepare, as separate reagents:

- full-length gasdermin D of the ortholog used in the genetic and expression experiments;
- the amino-terminal polypeptide matching that cytotoxic portion;
- the complementary carboxy-terminal polypeptide;
- the inflammatory caspase preparation that cleaves this gasdermin D;
- after mapping only, a cleavage-resistant full-length point mutant. The substitution cannot be specified until the site is mapped.

1.3 Identity and purity gates, each preparation:

- expected intact mass and a single major species on SDS-PAGE;
- proposed purity gate: at least 90 percent of stain density in the intended band, otherwise the preparation cannot support a sufficiency claim;
- orthogonal second purification step so activity can be tested for co-fractionation with gasdermin D identity;
- detergent and contaminant screen of the final dialysis buffer, because residual detergent is a false-positive permeabilizer;
- solubility split: material that pellets after a defined clarifying spin is logged separately and is not used for a negative claim;
- aggregation check by light scattering or analytical sedimentation. A predominantly aggregated amino-terminal preparation cannot support M2.

1.4 Cellular correspondence gate, using the same experiment classes already in the packet, not a new claimed result: the amino-terminal reagent, expressed or introduced in the cellular system in which amino-terminal cytotoxicity was reported, should remain cytotoxic, and gasdermin D-deficient cells should remain the reference loss-of-function background. If the purified species cannot be bridged back to that cytotoxic activity, an in vitro negative is ambiguous (M5 or M6), not evidence for M2.

1.5 Cleavage gate in free solution: inflammatory caspase must convert full-length gasdermin D to the mapped fragments under the buffer later used with liposomes. Record cleavage extent. Incomplete cleavage cannot be interpreted as inactivity of the amino-terminal product.

Stop rule S1: if identity, purity, detergent, solubility, or cleavage gates fail, do not start the primary membrane comparison. Fix the reagent. Do not interpret a failed reagent as biology.

### 2. Calibration of the membrane assay

2.1 Liposome classes are experimental factors, not known gasdermin D preferences. Lock this panel before the primary comparison:

- neutral phosphatidylcholine liposomes;
- liposomes containing anionic phospholipid at two pre-set mole fractions;
- the same backgrounds with and without cholesterol;
- at least two curvature classes, large unilamellar vesicles and either small unilamellar or giant unilamellar vesicles;
- two ionic conditions: physiologic monovalent salt without added divalent cation, and the same salt with a pre-set divalent-cation concentration;
- two pH values, one near neutral cytosol and one more acidic, both buffered and osmolarity-matched.

No packet evidence selects among these lipids. A negative is a negative on this panel only.

2.2 Encapsulate two aqueous tracers: one small dye and one larger dextran of a pre-chosen size. The sizes are proposed parameters, not reported pore dimensions. Remove external tracer. Also prepare matched empty liposomes for binding assays.

2.3 Each experimental day, before coded samples are scored, the batch must pass:

- detergent releases a high, pre-locked fraction of encapsulated tracer;
- buffer spontaneous leak over the assay window stays below a pre-locked fraction of detergent maximum;
- tracer fluorescence is linear in the release range used;
- protein added without liposomes does not mimic the release signal by quenching or scattering;
- an optional external permeabilizing protein, known from work outside this packet, may be used only to show that the batch can report a true lesion. It is a calibration tool, not evidence about gasdermin D, and it is not required for the logical contrast among gasdermin D species.

Stop rule S2: any batch that fails detergent release or spontaneous-leak gates is invalid. Do not average it into the primary contrast.

Proposed starting gates, to be replaced by the calibration pilot if the pilot shows they are unworkable, and then re-locked: spontaneous leak under 15 percent of detergent maximum; detergent at least 80 percent of the theoretical encapsulated signal. These numbers are proposed operating criteria, not data.

### 3. Independent units, allocation, and blinding

The independent unit is one protein preparation crossed with one liposome batch and run on one day. Wells from the same mix are technical subsamples, not independent units.

Proposed minimum after the pilot, unless the pilot variance forces a larger lock: three independent preparations of each gasdermin D species, each tested on two independent liposome batches that passed calibration. Caspase preparations must likewise be independent across at least two preparations so a caspase-only artifact is not preparation-specific.

Allocation: generate the plate map before loading. Randomize conditions across positions. Include technical duplicates but analyze the pre-specified aggregate of each unit.

Blinding: code tubes so the person who adds protein and the person who quantifies fluorescence do not know which code is amino-terminal fragment, full-length, carboxy-terminal fragment, caspase, or mixture. Apply QC exclusions using calibration wells and protein-gel QC, then lock the exclusions, then unblind.

### 4. Primary comparison

Use equimolar protein across gasdermin D species. The molar series is a proposed dose range spanning well below to near the solubility limit. Cellular concentration is unreported, so no dose should be called physiological.

Locked conditions on the confirmation lipids, after a separate screen if needed:

1. buffer;
2. uncleaved full-length gasdermin D;
3. amino-terminal fragment;
4. carboxy-terminal fragment;
5. inflammatory caspase alone;
6. full-length gasdermin D plus inflammatory caspase;
7. amino-terminal fragment heated or protease-destroyed, as an activity-destruction control;
8. detergent maximum;
9. once the site is mapped, cleavage-resistant full-length gasdermin D plus caspase.

Screen versus confirmation, so lipid choice is not smuggled in after seeing the claim:

- Phase A, hypothesis-generating: amino-terminal fragment versus full-length across the full lipid and ionic panel, one preparation. Phase A nominates conditions; it does not confirm them.
- Phase B, decisive: nominated conditions plus one pre-specified mixed-phospholipid, cholesterol-containing, physiologic-salt condition, tested under blinding on the independent units in section 3. The mixed condition is a proposed approximation, not a measured cellular membrane.

In situ cleavage is mandatory in Phase B even if Phase A is negative, because of M5.

Stop rule S3: stop expansion if the calibration pilot cannot produce a valid liposome batch after a pre-set number of attempts. The problem is then technical, not biological. Stop rule S4, optional futility, pre-lock only: if the first two fully QC-passed independent amino-terminal preparations are indistinguishable from buffer on the entire panel and in situ cleavage is also negative, halt expansion and move to the ambiguous or negative interpretation rather than adding preparations to seek a positive.

### 5. Measurements

For every unit, record:

- small-tracer release kinetics and the locked endpoint;
- large-tracer release at the same endpoint;
- liposome turbidity before and after protein addition;
- fraction of each gasdermin D species bound to liposomes by flotation or sedimentation, judged on a gel, with a no-liposome protein control for pelleting of aggregates;
- cleavage extent in every caspase-containing reaction;
- protein concentration by an activity-independent assay;
- for binding-positive samples, a proposed crosslinking or native-gel oligomer readout. This is secondary. Failure to see an oligomer does not by itself negate leakage, and an oligomer in detergent is not the primary endpoint.

Do not add electron microscopy or structural determination to the decisive test. Those become useful only if M1 is supported and the subclass of lesion is still unresolved. They are contingent proposals, not missing author methods.

### 6. Analysis

Pre-specify the normalization: release equals (signal minus matched buffer) divided by (matched detergent maximum minus matched buffer), bounded by the QC gates.

Primary analysis unit is the independent preparation-batch-day unit, not the well. Report the estimate and interval for each primary contrast. A mixed model with preparation and batch as random effects is appropriate if the locked sample size supports it; with a small locked N, pre-specify that the decision uses the interval and the replication rule, not a discovered p-value.

Replication rule for a positive call: the amino-terminal advantage over full-length and over carboxy-terminal fragment must have the same direction in every QC-passed preparation, not only on average. A single active preparation is a lead, not a sufficiency claim, until orthogonal purification shows the activity co-fractionates with that species.

Exclusions logged before unblinding: failed S2 batches, protein preparations that failed S1, and documented spill or empty-well errors. No exclusion for “unexpected direction.”

No interim analysis for significance. The only early stops are S1–S4.

### 7. Troubleshooting, contingent on the failure mode

False positive:

- Dialysis buffer alone permeabilizes: detergent or contaminant in the prep. Do not use that prep.
- Activity does not co-fractionate with gasdermin D through the orthogonal step: contaminant, not gasdermin D.
- All proteins permeabilize at high concentration: nonspecific protein or osmotic artifact. Restrict the claim to concentrations where full-length and carboxy-terminal fragment remain near buffer.
- Caspase alone permeabilizes: do not interpret the caspase-plus-full-length well as gasdermin D activity.

False negative:

- Recombinant amino-terminal fragment aggregated: rely on in situ cleavage of soluble full-length protein; do not call M2.
- Tag present: repeat with the tag removed. The packet does not mention tags; this applies only if the proposed production uses one.
- Cleavage incomplete on liposomes: raise caspase or time within a pre-set range, with caspase-alone as control. If cleavage never proceeds, the in situ test is uninterpretable.
- Activity only outside the locked panel, discovered afterward: that is a new hypothesis, not a positive primary result. It requires a fresh locked confirmation.
- Dose may be below a local cellular concentration: that concentration is unreported. State the tested range as a boundary on any negative claim.

### 8. Contingent branch, only after a QC-passed negative

Do not run cofactor hunts in place of the minimal test.

If Phase B and in situ cleavage are negative under S1-passed reagents, add clarified cytosol or extracted membrane protein from gasdermin D-deficient cells to the liposome assay with and without the amino-terminal fragment. Restoration specifically by the combination would support M2 and justify fractionation. Restoration by cytosol without the fragment is a nonspecific permeabilizing contaminant. No restoration leaves M3, an untested membrane feature, or inactive protein still open.

A later cellular test, not part of the primary claim: after a candidate cofactor is actually identified, ask whether loss of that cofactor blocks amino-terminal cytotoxicity in the expression system already linked to death. No candidate is named here because none is in the packet.

## Conditional conclusions

These are interpretation rules for future outcomes. None has been observed.

**Positive for direct sufficiency.** Across QC-passed independent units, amino-terminal fragment and caspase-cleaved full-length gasdermin D release the small tracer above the locked margin over buffer, uncleaved full-length, carboxy-terminal fragment, and caspase alone; cleavage-resistant full-length plus caspase does not, if that reagent exists; activity co-fractionates with the amino-terminal species; destroyed fragment loses activity.  
**Justified conclusion.** The amino-terminal fragment can injure protein-free membranes. Another cellular protein is not required for that injury under the tested conditions.  
**Subclass, only if the secondary measurements agree.** Small-tracer release with large-tracer retention and preserved turbidity favors a size-limited aqueous lesion over wholesale solubilization. Release of both tracers plus loss of turbidity favors nonselective disruption or solubilization. Binding plus oligomerization without a size series does not by itself define a pore.  
**Not justified even by this positive.** That the liposome lesion is the lesion used in cells; that cellular proteins do not regulate it; any pore diameter, subunit count, or lipid specificity beyond the panel that actually worked; a therapeutic or toxic use.

**Negative for sufficiency on the locked panel.** No specific release by amino-terminal fragment or by in situ cleavage, reagents passed identity, solubility, aggregation, cleavage, and cellular-correspondence gates, and the panel was completed.  
**Justified conclusion.** Under the tested protein-free conditions, the fragment is not sufficient to permeabilize those bilayers. M1 is not supported for that panel. M2, M3, or an untested membrane feature remain open.  
**Not justified.** That a specific cofactor exists, that the fragment does not bind lipid, or that cellular cytotoxicity is indirect. Those require the contingent reconstitution result or cellular kinetic evidence, which are not supplied and would not yet have been interpreted as positive.

**Ambiguous outcomes, with the strongest statement each allows.**

- Recombinant amino-terminal fragment inactive, but caspase cleavage of full-length protein permeabilizes liposomes and the cleavage-resistant mutant does not: direct injury by the cleavage product is supported; the isolated fragment preparation is not an adequate negative. M5, not M2.
- Leakage only in one extreme lipid condition: conditional in vitro sufficiency only. Cellular relevance is unknown because the relevant lipid composition is unreported.
- Binding without leakage: the fragment can engage those liposomes but was not shown to open an aqueous path. Compatible with incomplete M1, with M2, or with the wrong ionic condition. No executor conclusion.
- Leakage without binding: suspect a non-protein contaminant or a post-binding dissociation assay failure. Repeat co-fractionation before any positive call.
- Amino-terminal and carboxy-terminal fragments both active: no gasdermin D-specific direct mechanism. Treat as nonspecific until dose and purity separate them.
- One preparation positive and others negative: unresolved. Do not average a contaminant into a biological claim.
- Small-tracer release only, with no cellular solute-selectivity data in the packet: in vitro small-solute permeability is real if the positive rules are met, but it may or may not account for pyroptotic lysis. The packet cannot connect those scales.
- High batch variance that crosses the decision margin: no mechanistic conclusion. Report the assay as not yet decisive.

## Strongest conclusion after the plan, by branch

If the positive rule is met, the strongest warranted statement is: the amino-terminal gasdermin D fragment is sufficient for protein-free membrane permeabilization, so another cellular protein is dispensable for membrane injury in that minimal system. If the negative rule is met, the strongest warranted statement is: the fragment was not sufficient on the locked protein-free panel, so the cellular executor remains unidentified. If the result is ambiguous, the strongest warranted statement is that the supplied cellular link still stands and the physical executor is still unknown. In every branch, the present packet’s conclusion does not change until those observations exist: processing is linked to pyroptotic death, the amino-terminal portion is cytotoxic in cells, and the molecule that executes membrane injury has not been identified.

## Assumptions and unreported parameters that bound use

Assumptions used to design, not to supply facts: tracer release reports aqueous membrane injury; protein-free bilayers can falsify a requirement for another protein; equimolar comparison is fairer than equal mass; the expression fragment and the caspase product should be compared directly because either alone can mislead.

Unreported, and therefore not filled in: species and isoform; caspase identity; cleavage site; fragment coordinates; cell type; death kinetics; membrane lipid composition; local fragment concentration; whether genetic loss removed gasdermin D, the caspase, or a cleavage site; whether the carboxy-terminal fragment was tested; any pore size or oligomer number.

The plan does not invent those inputs. A positive or negative result applies only to the ortholog, fragment boundaries, lipid panel, dose range, and time window that were actually locked and tested.
