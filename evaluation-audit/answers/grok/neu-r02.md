# Grok 4.7 / neu-r02

Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

The most valuable next question is whether patterned visual experience is causally necessary for the organized receptive fields and two-eye influence already described in visual cortex, and whether any such necessity is eye-specific, reversible, and restricted to a developmental window. Do not treat further description of normal organization as the next primary study: the packet already states that description does not establish experience’s developmental role, and it contains no developmental perturbation, recovery experiment, or later deprivation result. Any animal work below is a proposal only. It may begin only after welfare-committee approval, with an explicit model and intervention justification, and no result of this plan has been observed.

## Evidence → inference → conclusion

**Evidence.** The packet reports that recordings in visual cortex show organized receptive fields and neurons influenced by the two eyes.

**Inference.** At whatever ages and conditions were recorded—those parameters are unreported—visual cortex can contain spatially or feature-organized receptive fields and neurons whose activity is influenced by both eyes. That is a descriptive claim only.

**Evidence.** The same packet states that this description does not establish how visual experience contributes to development, and that the supplied observations contain no developmental perturbation, recovery experiment, or later deprivation result.

**Inference.** Presence of organization and binocular influence is compatible with experience-independent specification, with a permissive requirement for visual drive, with competitive instruction by the two eyes, with a limited sensitive window, and with continuous experience dependence. These alternatives make different predictions only when early perturbation, restoration, and matched later deprivation are compared with procedure-matched controls.

**Conclusion.** The causal developmental contribution of visual experience is unresolved. The highest-value next experiment is the smallest welfare-approved design that can separate those alternatives for pre-registered endpoints, not another map of normal cortex and not a molecular screen launched before necessity and timing are tested.

**Assumption, not packet evidence.** “Organized” is not defined as retinotopy, orientation, spatial frequency, ON/OFF structure, or map continuity, and “influenced by the two eyes” is not defined as spiking, subthreshold, balanced, or columnar. Species, age, sample size, stimuli, and recording method are unreported. Those gaps are reasons for a calibration phase, not facts to invent.

## Unresolved question

For the features that replicate the packet’s descriptive phenomena, is patterned visual experience during development necessary, is relative experience of the two eyes competitive, is disruption reversible by restoring vision, and is an equivalent manipulation ineffective after organization is already present?

A narrower alternative—asking only whether adult maps are stable—is less valuable, because a late-only result cannot show how development produced the organization. Jumping to genes, spontaneous retinal waves, or synaptic plasticity mechanisms is also premature: those are candidate implementations, not discriminators of whether postnatal visual experience matters.

## Competing mechanisms and discriminating predictions

All predictions below are prospective. None has been observed in the packet.

**M1 — Experience-independent specification.** Molecular cues and/or prenatal spontaneous activity build organized receptive fields and two-eye influence; postnatal patterned vision is unnecessary for the measured features.

*Signature:* Early binocular deprivation, early monocular deprivation, immediate recovery, delayed recovery, and duration-matched late deprivation all remain equivalent to normally reared and sham animals on locked endpoints, provided deprivation actually removed patterned vision and animals met health criteria. This would not prove a molecular mechanism. It would only reject necessity inside the tested windows.

**M2 — Permissive, non-competitive visual drive.** Some patterned vision is required to stabilize a pre-specified circuit, but the specific eye and the spatial pattern are not instructive.

*Signature:* Early binocular deprivation degrades the locked endpoints; the open-eye representation after early monocular deprivation remains equivalent to normal rearing, without a competitive gain in open-eye drive; restoring binocular vision reverses the binocular-deprivation deficit; duration-matched late binocular deprivation does not. A pure loss of deprived-eye responses, with no open-eye expansion, is not enough to claim competition.

**M3 — Competitive instruction during a sensitive window.** Relative patterned input from the two eyes instructs ocular influence, and patterned vision during a limited window is required for receptive-field organization to reach or keep the described state.

*Signature:* Early monocular deprivation shifts influence toward the experienced eye and can degrade binocular matching; early binocular deprivation increases poorly organized or weakly driven neurons without favoring either eye; restoration soon after early deprivation reverses these effects; the same deprivation started only after organization is established in normally reared animals does not; delayed restoration, matched in duration to successful early restoration, fails or is substantially weaker. A later, separate pattern-alteration test would be needed to show instruction of receptive-field shape rather than mere competitive gain. That test is not justified until M3’s timing signature appears.

**M4 — Continuous experience dependence.** The same features remain experience-dependent whenever vision is removed; there is no special developmental window.

*Signature:* Duration-matched late deprivation produces a deficit comparable to early deprivation, and restoration works on the delayed schedule as well as on the immediate schedule, given equal restoration time and verified optics.

**M5 — Nonspecific procedural or developmental harm.** Apparent effects come from handling, anesthesia, surgery, stress, optics damage, or illness, not from loss of patterned vision.

*Signature:* Sham animals differ from normally reared animals in the same direction as deprived animals, or late procedure-matched groups match early deprived groups even when vision was not restricted, or health covariates account for the group differences. M5 is a confound model and must be rejected before any biological mechanism is claimed.

M1 is uniquely all-null. M4 is uniquely early≈late and immediate recovery≈delayed recovery. M3 is uniquely early-specific, competitive, and reversible only on the early restoration schedule. M2 matches M3 on early binocular deprivation and late sparing but not on open-eye competitive gain. M5 matches any deficit pattern if shams are also impaired. If organization and two-eye influence follow different signatures, the conclusion must be feature-specific. The packet gives no reason to assume one mechanism covers both.

## What would change this recommendation

Change the plan if any of the following is established from actual records, not from memory of the literature: the source study already contains a deprivation, recovery, or late-deprivation contrast; the phenomena do not replicate under calibrated recording; welfare review prohibits developmental sensory restriction; or Phase 0 shows that the only reliable “organization” is an artifact of one stimulus set. If sensory restriction is not approvable, the next question should narrow to non-causal constraints—human natural-history comparisons or models fit only to descriptive data—and those cannot support a causal developmental claim.

## Proposed protocol

This is a proposal. It does not report author methods, completed calibration, sample sizes, or outcomes. Numeric thresholds, ages, and N are unreported parameters to be locked from Phase 0 and the welfare approval before unblinding.

### 0. Prerequisites, before any animal is allocated

1. Written approval from the competent animal-welfare committee, including humane endpoints, monitoring frequency, maximum duration of sensory restriction, and a stop authority independent of the scientific team.
2. Explicit justification, in that application, for species and intervention: the species must have a visual cortex in which both organized receptive fields and two-eye influence can be measured in a way commensurate with the packet; each eye’s patterned vision must be controllable independently; developmental age must be definable relative to eye opening and to the age at which the phenomena are reliable in normally reared animals. The packet names no species. Do not assume cat, ferret, mouse, or primate. Prefer the species of the source recordings if and only if that species is documented outside this packet and the welfare review accepts it. Otherwise select the least invasive approved species that can answer the question.
3. Justification for intervention over further description: the packet already says description cannot establish experience’s role.
4. Replacement, reduction, and refinement statement: descriptive and computational work cannot replace the causal contrast, but sequential stopping can reduce animal numbers; refined housing, analgesia, and approved restriction methods are mandatory. No surgical recipe is specified here because none is in the packet and inventing one is unnecessary for the logic.
5. Preregistration of endpoints, equivalence bounds, randomization, interim rules, and the interpretation matrix before the first deprivation.
6. Role separation: intervention staff, recording staff, and analysts are different people. The physiologist and primary analyst remain blinded to rearing condition until the analysis lock.

If approval is refused, stop. Do not substitute an unapproved restriction method.

### 1. Phase 0 — operationalize and calibrate, normally reared animals only

Purpose: define measurable versions of the packet’s two phenomena and prove the lab can detect them before any perturbation. A later null is otherwise uninterpretable.

**Unreported parameters to estimate, not assume:** age at which organized receptive fields and two-eye spiking influence are each reliably present (call these ages \(T_{RF}\) and \(T_{bin}\); \(T_{org} = \max(T_{RF}, T_{bin})\)); recording modality; cortical target coordinates; stimulus set; quality criteria.

**Proposed measurement battery, labeled proposal:** monocular and binocular visual stimulation with blank trials; eye-of-origin tagging; spike or imaging responses, matching the source modality if known, otherwise the modality that in Phase 0 recovers both phenomena with pre-set reliability. Because the packet may mean either single-neuron selectivity or cortical map order, score both.

- Neuron-level organization: fraction of response variance explained by a spatial or feature receptive-field model versus spike-time-shuffled and stimulus-shuffled controls.
- Population organization: nearby-site similarity of preferred position or feature above a spatial null.
- Two-eye influence: an eye “influences” a neuron only if monocular evoked response exceeds spontaneous activity by a criterion locked before deprivation. Report ocular-dominance index, fraction of neurons influenced by each eye, and fraction influenced by both. Extracellular spikes cannot establish subthreshold-only influence. If the source claim might have been subthreshold, state that limit; do not add intracellular recording unless Phase 0 and welfare review justify it.

**Calibration checks:** stimulus luminance, contrast, timing, and eye optics; electrode or imaging stability; histological or functional confirmation that sites are in visual cortex and, for binocular endpoints, not exclusively in a monocular zone; test-retest of animal-level metrics on a held-out normally reared subset.

**Lock rule, before any deprived animal:** the primary organization metric is the candidate with acceptable test-retest reliability that is present in at least the pre-set fraction of normally reared neurons or sites. The other organization metric is secondary. Two-eye influence is co-primary, not collapsed into organization, because the mechanisms can dissociate. Equivalence bounds and the smallest effect size of interest are set from Phase 0 variance and a scientifically justified biological difference, then frozen.

**Phase 0 stop:** if normally reared animals do not show both phenomena reliably, do not start deprivation. That outcome would challenge replicability of the descriptive claim under the chosen methods. It would not be a developmental result.

### 2. Independent units, allocation, and blinding

The independent experimental unit is the animal. Neurons and sites are nested observations and must not be treated as independent N. Litter is a blocking factor because littermates share prenatal environment. Sex is an unreported factor: stratify or balance it; do not claim a sex effect unless tested as pre-registered.

Randomize animals within litter to the open stage’s groups with allocation concealment. Use unique IDs that do not encode group. Recording, receptive-field fitting, and primary analysis use only those IDs. Verification that patterned vision was actually restricted is stored in a locked file by intervention staff and merged only at analysis. Blinding of the person who applies the restriction is not fully possible; that is why role separation and sham procedures exist.

### 3. Sequential interventions

Use only the institution’s approved method for restricting patterned vision through one or both eyes. Duration, not an invented age, is matched across early and late arms. “Early” ends at \(T_{org}\). “Late” starts at \(T_{org}\) plus a pre-registered margin after organization is already reliable in normally reared animals and lasts the same number of days as early restriction. Assessment age is matched across groups that are directly compared, or age is a pre-registered covariate if matching is impossible. Both choices must be locked before unblinding.

**Stage A — necessity and timing, binocular restriction**

- Normally reared.
- Sham-early: procedure-matched handling without visual restriction.
- Binocular patterned-vision restriction, early.
- Sham-late.
- Binocular patterned-vision restriction, late, duration-matched.

**Stage A decision, pre-registered**

- If early binocular restriction is equivalent to normally reared and sham-early, stop the causal program for that endpoint. M1 is supported within the tested window. Do not automatically run monocular groups: competitive instruction is unlikely if removing both eyes’ patterned vision does nothing. A small confirmatory monocular cohort is optional only under an approved amendment and is not required for the M1 conclusion.
- If early restriction differs from normal and sham, but late restriction and sham-late do not, proceed to Stage B. Necessity plus timing are still compatible with M2 or M3.
- If early and late both differ from their shams, the window claim fails; Stage B may still test competition, but M4 remains viable until recovery timing is known.
- If shams differ from normally reared, stop biological interpretation and troubleshoot M5.

**Stage B — competition and recovery, only if Stage A shows an early, sham-controlled deficit**

- Monocular restriction, early, eye side randomized.
- Monocular restriction, late, duration-matched.
- Immediate restoration: early monocular or binocular restriction, using the Stage A effective manipulation, then patterned binocular vision restored until assessment.
- Delayed restoration: same early restriction, restoration withheld until the late-start age, then the same restoration duration as the immediate arm.
- Procedure-matched shams for each restoration schedule if those procedures add manipulations not already controlled.

Do not add stripe-rearing, blur-rearing, or dark-versus-lid contrasts in Stage B. Those discriminate pattern instruction from mere loss of drive only after a competitive, time-limited deficit is established. They require a separate welfare justification.

### 4. Controls

- Normal rearing: reference for the packet’s phenomena.
- Sham-early and sham-late: procedural harm and age-linked stress.
- Both-eyes versus one-eye restriction: necessity versus competition.
- Immediate versus delayed restoration: reversibility versus a closed window.
- Duration-matched late restriction: developmental timing versus continuous dependence or cumulative damage.
- Blank stimuli, shuffle controls, and monocular-zone histology: measurement artifacts.
- Deprivation-verification log: failed restriction is a protocol deviation, not silent exclusion.
- Health and optics covariates: weight, wound score, media clarity, pupil response, recorded under blinding where possible.

There is no untreated “author control group” to copy: the packet reports no such methods.

### 5. Measurements at assessment

For every animal, use the locked Phase 0 protocol: the same stimuli, quality gates, cortical-location checks, and metrics. Pre-define exclusion of a neuron for poor isolation or unstable optics before knowing its ocular-dominance index. Do not exclude neurons because they are monocular or poorly organized; those are outcomes. Record spontaneous rate, monocular evoked rates, binocular rate, model-explained variance, and site location. Keep raw traces sufficient for an independent re-fit.

### 6. Analysis

Primary analysis is at animal level: each animal contributes one value per locked endpoint, derived from its neurons under the quality rules. Use a hierarchical model with neuron nested in animal and litter as a random effect, or animal-level summaries if the hierarchical model is under-identified. The litter term matters more than neuron count.

Pre-registered contrasts, in order:

1. Early binocular restriction versus normally reared and versus sham-early: necessity, confound-checked.
2. Late binocular restriction versus normally reared and versus early restriction: timing.
3. If Stage B runs: early monocular ocular-dominance shift versus normally reared, tested as deprived-eye loss and open-eye gain separately.
4. Immediate restoration versus sustained early restriction, and versus normally reared.
5. Delayed restoration versus immediate restoration.

Use estimates and confidence or credible intervals, plus pre-registered equivalence tests against the frozen bounds. A non-significant difference is not evidence of no effect. Multiplicity control applies to this primary family only. Secondary metrics are exploratory. Report intention-to-treat by allocated group and a per-protocol sensitivity analysis that separates verification failures. Do not peek to choose the primary metric. Interim looks are allowed only for welfare and for the futility rule below.

### 7. Stop rules

- Welfare: cross a humane endpoint, or exceed the approved adverse-event rate → stop enrollment and obtain veterinary review.
- Calibration: Phase 0 failure → stop; do not interpret as experience independence.
- Verification: restriction-failure rate above the pre-set threshold → stop enrollment and fix the intervention before more animals.
- Futility: if the predictive probability of an early-versus-normal difference larger than the smallest effect of interest falls below the pre-set threshold, stop for absence of necessity within this design.
- Sham failure: sham diverges from normal beyond the equivalence bound → stop causal claims.
- Reduction: do not keep adding animals to a contrast that is already decisive; any reallocation needs an amendment.
- No optional stopping for a favorable p-value.

### 8. Troubleshooting, decision tree rather than expected data

- No organized fields in normally reared animals: check optics, stimulus timing, anesthesia or state, and cortical location. Do not deprive.
- Evoked responses absent but spontaneous activity present: suspect stimulus delivery or unintended occlusion.
- All neurons monocular: suspect monocular-zone recordings; confirm sites before interpreting two-eye influence.
- Early binocular deficit without an open-eye gain after monocular restriction: favors M2, or an ineffective open eye. Inspect open-eye optics before claiming M2.
- Restoration fails: check whether restoration duration and optical quality met the locked criteria. If they did, failed immediate restoration argues against rapid reversibility and weakens M3.
- Late deficit equals early deficit: M4 or M5. Separate them with sham-late, not with more neurons from the same deprived animals.
- Litter variance dominates: add litters, not cells per animal.
- One endpoint affected and the other spared: report dissociation. Do not average them into a single “plasticity” score.

## Conditional conclusions

These are rules for interpreting future data. They are not results.

**Positive for a sensitive-window competitive mechanism (M3), organization and binocular influence together.** Would require: early restriction degrades the locked endpoints relative to normal and sham; monocular restriction shifts influence toward the open eye, including open-eye gain or a clearly competitive redistribution, not only silenced deprived-eye responses; immediate restoration reverses the deficit toward normal; duration-matched late restriction and delayed restoration do not produce the same reversal or the same late deficit; verification and health logs are adequate. Strongest justified conclusion: in this species and window, patterned visual experience is necessary for the pre-registered features, two-eye influence is competitively shaped, and that dependence is stronger early than late. Not justified: a named molecular pathway, a claim about unmeasured subthreshold influence, or generality to every receptive-field feature.

**Positive for necessity without competition (M2).** Early binocular restriction degrades organization; late matched restriction does not; shams are normal; monocular restriction spares open-eye organization and does not produce competitive open-eye gain. Strongest conclusion: patterned vision during the early window is necessary, but the data do not show competitive instruction of two-eye influence.

**Positive for experience independence within the test (M1).** All verified restrictions and both restoration schedules are equivalent to normal and sham, with intervals inside the equivalence bounds and Phase 0 having shown the phenomena are detectable. Strongest conclusion: postnatal patterned vision in the tested windows is not necessary for these endpoints. Not justified: “innate molecular specification,” because prenatal activity was not manipulated.

**Positive for continuous dependence (M4).** Early and duration-matched late deficits are comparable, shams are spared, and delayed restoration succeeds to the same degree as immediate restoration. Strongest conclusion: the endpoints still require patterned vision after \(T_{org}\); a closed critical period is not supported.

**Negative relative to the packet’s implied developmental hypothesis.** A well-powered, verified, sham-controlled null at early and late ages is a negative result for experience dependence. It is informative, not a failed experiment. The justified statement is absence of a detectable necessary role inside this design, not proof that development is experience-free at all stages.

**Ambiguous outcomes, and what they forbid.**

- Early, late, and sham groups all impaired: do not claim a visual critical period; M5 is unresolved.
- Early deficit with failed immediate restoration: necessity is possible, reversibility and window closure are not established.
- Deprived-eye loss without open-eye gain: do not call this competitive instruction.
- Organization disrupted but two-eye influence spared, or the reverse: do not issue one mechanism for both packet phenomena.
- High animal-level variance, failed verification, or assessment-age mismatch: no causal conclusion; the study is inconclusive, not positive or negative.
- Effect only in neuron-level pooling that disappears at animal level: do not claim a population developmental result.
- Pattern-specific receptive-field changes were not tested in Stage A/B, so even a clean M3 signature does not prove that visual pattern instructs receptive-field geometry. That remains a later question.

## Strongest conclusion the packet itself allows

The packet supports only this: visual cortex recordings can show organized receptive fields and neurons influenced by both eyes, and that organization, as described, does not reveal whether or how visual experience builds it. No developmental mechanism is selected by the supplied observations. The proposal above is the most direct way to select among M1–M5 for explicitly operationalized endpoints. Until those contrasts exist, the justified scientific position is uncertainty, not a default critical-period story and not a default innate-map story.

## Limits

The plan cannot identify molecular implementers, prenatal wave-dependent scaffolding, or instruction by specific spatial statistics unless later approved stages are added. It cannot repair ambiguity in the word “organized” except by the Phase 0 lock, and that lock defines the claim’s scope. Results would apply to the approved species, ages, restriction method, and spiking or imaging measure actually used. Welfare limits are scientific limits: an unapprovable manipulation is outside the question, not a detail to work around. No author method, sample size, or outcome has been inferred beyond the packet, and no proposed result should be cited as observed.
