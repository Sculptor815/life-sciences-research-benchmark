# Grok 4.7 / mol-p01

Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

The citation does not identify the registered work, and the supplied experiments do not support “A directly binds B in unmodified cells.” Treat these as separate failures: a record-identity mismatch, and an over-strong biological inference. Neither a mismatched notice nor the experimental gap establishes intent.

## Conclusion
Submitted citation C1 (I. Vale, “Native A-B recognition,” 2024, record LS-M17) is not the same item as registry entry C2 for LS-M17 (J. Reed, “Tagged protein association,” 2022; no correction or retraction in this snapshot). The biological claim is also unsupported by E1–E2. E1 shows only that tagged, overexpressed A and B co-immunoprecipitate. Co-IP under those conditions is compatible with association in a complex; it does not show direct binding, and it was not done in unmodified cells. E2 states that no endogenous co-IP and no purified-protein binding experiment are supplied.

## Citation identity, separated from the claim
Evidence: C1 assigns author Vale, title “Native A-B recognition,” year 2024, and identifier LS-M17. C2 assigns the same identifier to author Reed, title “Tagged protein association,” year 2022, with no correction or retraction recorded.

Inference: author, title, and year all disagree. Identifier match alone does not make the works identical. The submitted citation therefore does not correctly name the complete registry entry in this dossier.

Conclusion on identity only: LS-M17, as frozen here, is Reed 2022, not Vale 2024. This is a citation error relative to the registry. It is not evidence that A binds B, and it is not proof that anyone intended to mislead.

## Evidence-to-inference-to-conclusion for the binding claim
Claim to test: A directly binds B in unmodified cells.

Evidence actually supplied:
- E1: tagged A and tagged B were overexpressed and co-immunoprecipitated in three independent cultures, with input and IgG controls.
- E2: no endogenous co-IP and no purified-protein binding experiment are supplied.
- C2’s title, “Tagged protein association,” is consistent with E1 and does not add a direct-binding or unmodified-cell result. Title wording is not a result.

Inference, axis by axis:
- Association versus direct binding. Recovery of B with A (or the reverse) after co-IP can reflect a direct contact or an indirect complex through other proteins. Input controls show that both proteins were present; IgG controls argue against nonspecific bead or antibody capture. Neither control identifies the contacting surfaces or excludes bridging factors. Without a purified-protein binding result, or another direct-contact assay, “directly binds” is not earned.
- Modified versus unmodified cells. Tags and overexpression are modifications of sequence context, abundance, and often localization or stoichiometry. E1 therefore speaks to engineered cultures, not unmodified cells. E2 explicitly withholds the endogenous experiment that would be relevant to the unmodified-cell part of the claim.
- Replication. Three independent cultures strengthen the co-IP observation under the stated overexpression conditions. Replication does not convert an indirect, modified-cell assay into a direct, unmodified-cell result.

Conclusion on the claim: the dossier supports tagged, overexpressed A–B association by co-IP with basic controls. It does not support direct binding, and it does not support binding in unmodified cells. Using C1’s title, “Native A-B recognition,” to stand in for that stronger claim would mismatch both the registry identity and the reported design.

## Benign alternatives
A suspicious mismatch is not intent. Plausible non-deceptive explanations include:
- Clerical substitution: wrong author, year, or title pasted onto the correct record ID, or the correct paper cited under the wrong ID.
- Paraphrase: “Native A-B recognition” written as an interpretation of “Tagged protein association,” not as the registered title.
- Snapshot or training-dossier limits: this packet is frozen as of 2026-10-03 and says no correction or retraction is recorded. A later correction, a different Vale paper, or data absent from the dossier could exist outside this packet. That possibility is untested here; it is not evidence already in hand.
- Loose scientific wording: “binds” used for co-IP association, and “cells” used without stating that the cells were modified by tags and overexpression. That can be a reporting error rather than fabrication.
- Registry error: C2 could itself be wrong. The packet gives no independent full text to confirm Reed 2022’s contents beyond E1–E2 and the registry statement.

These alternatives can explain the citation defect or the wording. They do not fill E2. They do not turn E1 into direct binding in unmodified cells.

## What would disprove the concern
Two checks, kept separate:

1. Citation check. Resolve LS-M17 and the Vale 2024 description in a source independent of this dossier. The identity concern is disproved if that source shows LS-M17 is Vale, “Native A-B recognition,” 2024, or shows a recorded correction equating the two descriptions. It is also resolved, in the other direction, if Vale 2024 is a different record and the submission simply attached the wrong identifier. A notice that the strings differ is not enough; the check is a full bibliographic match, including author, title, year, and record ID.

2. Claim check. The biological concern is disproved only by data that are not in this packet: endogenous co-IP, or an equivalent interaction assay, in unmodified cells, plus a direct-binding assay with purified A and B (or a method that specifically reports direct contact), with input, negative-binding, and specificity controls. Finding only another tagged overexpression co-IP would replicate E1 and would not disprove the overclaim. If those direct and endogenous results are absent from the actual paper, the claim remains unsupported even if the citation is later corrected.

## Limits
No full text, methods, figure, or quantitative co-IP result is in the packet, so effect size, tag identity, cell type, and antibody specificity cannot be judged. “Unmodified” and “directly” are assessed only against E1–E2. No motive can be assigned. The operational recommendation is to reject the submitted citation as a pointer to LS-M17 and to reject the sentence “A directly binds B in unmodified cells” until the two checks above succeed.
