# Grok 4.7 / bio-d02

Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

All experiments below are proposed. The recommended next action is to run a matched reconstitution assay, not to infer chemical identity from the 1906 compensation result. The supplied record supports only an operational hypothesis: a heat-stable factor can be separated from a heat-labile fermentative preparation and is required, together with that preparation, for activity. It does not identify the factor and does not establish NAD identity.

**Conditional conclusion this protocol is allowed to reach.** If, and only if, the acceptance criteria below are met, conclude: under phosphate-, salt-, pH-, protein-, and volume-matched conditions, fermentative activity requires a heat-stable activity that can be separated from the heat-labile fraction by more than one physical method, and that activity is not replaced by inorganic residue, defined ions, phosphate, dilution, or a nonspecific protein additive. Do not name the factor, assign a structure, or equate it with NAD. If any acceptance criterion fails, stop at the failed alternative; do not upgrade the claim.

## Evidence → inference → conclusion

**Evidence (fixed packet only).** The source summary states that a 1906 yeast-juice separation into filtrate and retentate, followed by recombination and by compensation with boiled extract, gave inactive single fractions and restored activity in combination. The curator limit is explicit: compensation is not chemical structure and does not determine NAD identity. Named artifacts are pH, inorganic salt, and phosphate changes during separation, plus enzyme damage or dilution that nonspecific stabilizers could mimic. Hypothetical constraints: a yeast-juice-derived fermenting preparation can be split into filtrate and retentate; boiled extract and candidate fractions are available; the factor is structurally unidentified; buffer, ion, phosphate, protein, volume, and dilution values are unreported and must be calibrated, not invented.

**Inference this evidence does license.** Individual inactivity plus restoration on recombination is the pattern expected if activity requires at least two separable components. Survival of the compensating activity in boiled extract is the pattern expected if at least one required component is heat-stable and the other is not. That is an operational, not a chemical, inference.

**Inferences this evidence does not license.** The same pattern is also expected if separation changes pH, salts, or phosphate; if one fraction is diluted below a kinetic threshold; if the enzyme is damaged and a boiled additive merely stabilizes it; if one fraction contains an inhibitor that the other dilutes; or if inorganic phosphate alone is the missing reactant. Compensation by boiled extract shows heat-stable restorative activity in that extract. It does not identify the molecule.

**What would change the recommendation.** If a phosphate dose–response on the retentate reaches the recombined rate, drop the “additional factor” claim and report phosphate sufficiency. If buffer dilution alone restores the retentate, treat the result as dilution or inhibitor relief, not factor requirement. If add-back fails on already inactive retentate but “works” only when present during separation, treat it as protection from damage, not as a required participant. If boiled filtrate loses restorative activity, reject heat-stability. If only one separation method works, do not claim general separability. If restorative activity cannot be destroyed or removed without changing the matched inorganic background, do not claim a distinct factor.

## Assumptions and unreported parameters

Assumptions, all explicit and testable: fermentative activity can be read as an initial rate (gas production or sugar disappearance) under fixed sugar and temperature; the preparation can yield a retentate and a filtrate; boiled extract is available; fractions can be assayed within a time window where microbial growth is negligible. None of the numerical set-points exist in the packet. Do not import historical recipes. Every set-point below is a calibration target, not a reported value.

Unreported and therefore not specified here: buffer identity and concentration, pH set-point, ion list and concentrations, phosphate concentration, protein concentration, dilution factor, membrane cutoff, boil duration, assay temperature, sugar concentration, and numerical inactivity or restoration thresholds. Each is to be set by the calibration named in the protocol.

## Proposed protocol

### 1. Preparation and quality checks

Prepare one yeast-juice fermenting stock per independent unit. Before fractionation, record pH, conductivity, inorganic phosphate, protein, and initial fermentative rate of that stock, plus a no-sugar blank and a fully boiled aliquot of the same stock. Proceed only if the unboiled stock rate exceeds the boiled and no-sugar blanks by a margin set in calibration C8. If the stock is already inactive, stop; fractionation cannot test a requirement for activity that is absent.

Split the stock: one portion is sham-processed (same time, temperature, vessels, and dilutions as fractionation, but not separated); one portion enters separation method A; one enters separation method B. Hold all portions cold for the same total time. Record clock time from juice preparation to assay.

**Calibration, not invented constants.**

- **C1, sugar.** On sham juice, vary sugar until the initial rate plateaus. Use a saturating concentration inside that plateau for every arm.
- **C2, temperature and duration.** Choose a temperature at which sham-juice initial rate is stable across the planned assay window and boiled juice stays at blank. Use that temperature for all arms.
- **C3, linear range.** Dilute sham juice and plot rate versus relative enzyme amount. Restrict every comparison to the linear portion. The common dilution used in the assay must lie in that portion.
- **C4, pH window.** Measure stock pH. Titrate sham juice across a narrow window around that pH. Set the working pH to the stock value, and set the acceptance tolerance to the range over which sham rate changes by less than a pre-declared small fraction of itself. After every mix, measure pH and adjust into that window with the same buffer acid/base. Report post-mix pH.
- **C5, ions.** Measure stock conductivity and, if assays are available, the major inorganic cations actually present. Define a buffer that brings every arm to the same conductivity and the same measured cation set. Do not assume a salt recipe.
- **C6, phosphate.** Measure inorganic phosphate in stock, filtrate, and retentate. On retentate alone, add phosphate across a curve extending above the higher of stock and filtrate phosphate, at matched pH and conductivity. Find whether rate plateaus. The assay phosphate is that plateau concentration if a plateau exists; otherwise the higher of stock and filtrate, with the curve reported. All experimental arms receive this same phosphate concentration. This is mandatory because phosphate may be a reactant, not only a separation artifact.
- **C7, heat.** Boil-time series on whole juice and on filtrate. Choose the shortest treatment that drives whole-juice fermentative activity to the boiled blank and that, in parallel, leaves filtrate restorative activity unchanged across a further boil interval. Use that treatment for every “boiled” arm. A harsher uncalibrated boil is not allowed, because over-boiling could destroy a heat-stable factor or create inhibitors.
- **C8, thresholds.** From sham-versus-blank replicates in the calibration runs, pre-declare: inactivity = rate not distinguishable from the boiled blank by the equivalence margin set from blank variance; restoration = a pre-declared fraction of the paired sham rate, chosen so that it exceeds both blank and the phosphate-plateau retentate rate. Write these numbers down before the definitive runs. They are laboratory calibration outputs, not historical constants.
- **C9, protein assay.** Select one protein method, show linearity on juice dilutions, and use it for all matching. If filtrate protein is near the assay floor, report that and match volume rather than inventing a protein value.

### 2. Independent units

An independent unit is a separately prepared juice stock, fractionated independently. Technical replicates from one fractionation are not independent units. Plan at least three units before any confirmatory language. Method A and method B are both applied to each unit if volume allows; if not, balance methods across units and do not pool them as if they were one method.

Method A is the filtrate/retentate separation corresponding to the packet (a dialysis or ultrafiltration cut). Method B must be a physically different cut (for example precipitation versus a membrane, or gel filtration versus dialysis). Method B is proposed, not attributed to the 1906 record. Concordance is required for a separability claim. Cutoffs and precipitation conditions are acceptable only after a pilot shows one fraction carries restorative activity and the other carries heat-labile enzymatic activity, with both inorganic backgrounds re-matchable. Discard cuts that cannot be phosphate- and conductivity-matched.

### 3. Allocation and blinding

From each retentate stock, prepare coded aliquots for every arm before adding test solutions. Assign aliquot order by a random sequence generated before the run. The person who records the primary rate should not know the arm code. Unblind only after rates, pH, phosphate, conductivity, protein, and volumes are locked. Pre-declare the primary contrast in writing before the definitive assay. Calibration runs are not the definitive assay and are not to be reclassified as such after seeing the result.

### 4. Intervention and sampling

Build every assay tube to the same final volume, same sugar (C1), same temperature (C2), same pH (C4), same matched ions (C5), same phosphate (C6), and same retentate protein except in arms that intentionally omit retentate. Additions that differ in volume are balanced with the defined buffer. Sample the initial rate over the linear window from C3, not a single endpoint, so lag can be distinguished from rate.

**Arms, all proposed, all matched unless noted.**

1. Sham-processed unfractionated juice, diluted into the linear range. Reference for recoverable activity.
2. Boiled sham juice. Heat-inactivated enzyme blank.
3. No-sugar sham juice. Substrate blank.
4. Retentate alone, volume-balanced with buffer.
5. Filtrate alone, volume equal to the filtrate volume used in reconstitution, retentate omitted and volume-balanced.
6. Retentate + unboiled filtrate. Recombination.
7. Retentate + boiled filtrate. Heat-stable factor test. Primary biological intervention.
8. Retentate + boiled whole extract, volume-matched. Packet-style compensation arm. Interpret only as heat-stable restorative activity, not as identity.
9. Filtrate + boiled retentate. Assigns heat-lability to the retentate side. Expected under the two-component model; if this arm is active, the heat assignment fails.
10. Retentate + boiled defined buffer. Heat-procedure control.
11. Retentate + ashed filtrate, redissolved, then re-matched to the common phosphate, pH, conductivity, and volume. Inorganic inactive analog. Ashing is acceptable only after calibration shows loss of organic mass or loss of a heat-stable organic marker if one is measured, with phosphate recovered and then reset to C6 so ash is not a hidden phosphate dose.
12. Retentate + extensively dialyzed filtrate, returned to original volume with defined buffer and re-matched for phosphate and ions. Organic-depleted inactive analog. Dialysis extent is set by loss of restorative activity in a pilot, then locked.
13. Retentate + boiled washed retentate. Yeast-derived heat-treated inactive analog that should lack the filtrate activity.
14. Retentate + protein-matched heat-denatured non-yeast protein, volume-matched. Nonspecific protein/stabilizer control. Protein level equals measured protein in the boiled-filtrate addition.
15. Retentate diluted with buffer to the same extent as the largest filtrate addition, with no filtrate. Dilution and inhibitor-relief control.
16. Retentate + a small volume of concentrated filtrate, concentration set so the restorative activity dose matches arm 6 while added volume is much smaller, buffer-balanced. Distinguishes a positive factor from dilution of an inhibitor.
17. Phosphate series on retentate alone, already required by C6, reported beside the primary contrast. Not a substitute for it.
18. Optional protection-versus-restoration check: one aliquot fractionated with filtrate activity present throughout, versus the standard order (separate first, add back after the retentate is already inactive). This is a control, not the primary contrast.

If volume forces a reduction, keep arms 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 14, 15, and 16, plus the C6 phosphate series. Dropping 11, 15, or 16 invalidates the corresponding exclusion and must narrow the conclusion.

### 5. Measurements

Primary measurement: initial fermentative rate per unit retentate protein (or per unit volume if protein is at the assay floor, with that choice pre-declared). Record lag, rate, and extent separately. Do not use extent alone; a phosphate-limited reaction can change extent without showing a separable organic factor.

Every tube, before and after the assay window: pH, conductivity, inorganic phosphate, protein, final volume, temperature. A tube that leaves the C4–C6 windows is missing, not a silent success.

Enzyme-integrity monitors, because no factor-independent marker enzyme is supplied in the packet: sham-processed juice must retain activity (arm 1 versus a freshly assayed stock aliquot held for the same time); boiled retentate must be inactive (arm 9); restoration must occur when filtrate is added to already inactive retentate (arm 6), which is evidence against irreversible enzyme destruction. If sham activity collapses, integrity has failed. Do not invent a second enzyme assay.

Contamination monitor: rates should start without a growth lag; boiled and no-sugar blanks should stay near baseline. If activity appears only after a long lag in otherwise blank tubes, stop and treat the signal as microbial, not juice fermentation.

### 6. Controls mapped to alternatives

| Alternative | Control that can exclude it | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pH shift | C4 post-mix match | Rate tracks residual pH, not arm |
| Inorganic salt / ionic strength | C5 match; arm 10 | Defined salts restore retentate |
| Phosphate as the missing reactant | C6 curve; arm 11 re-matched | Phosphate plateau equals recombination |
| Dilution | Equal final volume; arm 15 | Buffer dilution restores |
| Inhibitor concentrated in retentate | Arm 16 small-volume concentrate | Only large-volume addition works |
| Enzyme damage | Sham retention; add-back after inactivation; arm 18 | Add-back fails; only co-processing “works” |
| Nonspecific stabilizer | Arm 14 | Foreign protein restores |
| Heat procedure artifact | Arm 10; C7 | Boiled buffer restores or boil destroys filtrate activity |
| Residual enzyme in filtrate | Arm 5; boiled filtrate still works while boiled retentate does not | Filtrate alone is active |
| Wrong side heat-stable | Arm 9 | Boiled retentate + filtrate is active |
| Single-method artifact | Method B concordance | Only method A shows the pattern |
| Identity overclaim | Pre-written ban | Any structural or NAD wording |

### 7. Analysis

Primary contrast, pre-declared, paired within juice unit: rate(retentate + boiled filtrate) minus rate(retentate + matched buffer), compared with rate(retentate + ashed, re-matched filtrate) minus rate(retentate + matched buffer), and with rate(retentate + phosphate-matched buffer) from the C6 plateau.

Report absolute rates, the paired difference, and the ratio to paired sham rate. Use the unit as the replicate. Technical replicates are averaged within a unit before inference. Do not pool method A and method B.

Specificity contrasts, secondary: arm 6 versus arm 7 (heat-stability equivalence, pre-declared margin from C7); arm 4 and arm 5 versus boiled blank (inactivity equivalence); arm 7 versus arm 11 and versus arm 14 (inorganic and protein analogs); arm 16 versus arm 15 (factor versus dilution); arm 9 versus arm 7 (which side is heat-labile).

No structural analysis is planned. No chromatographic identification, elemental formula, or NAD comparison is part of this protocol. Adding one later would be a new study and still would not be licensed by the 1906 compensation result alone.

### 8. Acceptance and stopping

Accept the operational claim only if all of the following hold on at least three independent units and on both separation methods:

- Sham juice remains active; boiled juice and no-sugar blanks do not.
- Retentate alone and filtrate alone meet the inactivity equivalence margin.
- Recombination and boiled-filtrate add-back each meet the restoration margin relative to paired sham, and boiled filtrate is equivalent to unboiled filtrate within the C7 margin.
- Boiled retentate plus filtrate does not restore.
- Phosphate-matched buffer, ashed re-matched filtrate, boiled buffer, dialyzed depleted filtrate, and protein-matched foreign protein all fail to restore within the inactivity margin.
- Small-volume concentrated filtrate restores, and equal-volume buffer dilution does not.
- Post-mix pH, phosphate, conductivity, protein, and volume are matched inside calibration windows.
- Add-back restores already inactive retentate, so the result is not only protection during separation.

Stop or narrow the claim if:

- Sham activity is lost: enzyme-damage confound; redesign a gentler separation. Do not interpret fractions.
- Phosphate alone reaches the restoration margin: report phosphate sufficiency; do not claim an additional factor.
- Only unboiled filtrate restores: heat-stable claim fails.
- Only one method works: separability is method-bound.
- Filtrate alone is active: separation is incomplete; tighten the cut and repeat.
- Boiled filtrate inhibits relative to buffer: look for a boil-generated inhibitor before any factor claim.
- Restoration occurs only with large dilution volume: prefer inhibitor relief; the factor claim fails.

### 9. Troubleshooting

If recombination is partial, first check phosphate and pH windows and the linear range. Partial restoration can mean a third required component, residual damage, or an inhibitor in one fraction. Distinguish those by arm 16 and by whether a second extract dose increases rate at constant volume. Do not name the missing component.

If method B cannot be matched for phosphate, do not force concordance. Report method A with the phosphate limitation explicit, and withhold the general separability sentence.

If protein in boiled extract exceeds the foreign-protein control and that control is partially active, the stabilizer alternative is not excluded. Increase the protein match or reduce extract protein without removing restorative activity (for example by the same depletion used for arm 12, calibrated only by activity). If that cannot be done, the conclusion must say nonspecific protein stabilization was not excluded.

## Limits

This protocol tests necessity of a separable heat-stable activity under matched conditions. It does not test sufficiency in a defined chemical system, purity, molecular weight, structure, biosynthesis, or identity with NAD or any coenzyme. The packet’s boiled-extract compensation remains, even if replicated, non-identifying. Phosphate matching removes phosphate as a confounder of the contrast; it does not deny that phosphate may also be required. Concordance of two separations still does not prove a single molecular species. Three units can support a laboratory operational claim; they cannot repair the historical limit on chemical identity.

**Proposed conclusion language, to be used only after acceptance.** “In this matched reconstitution, a heat-stable activity separable from the heat-labile yeast-juice fraction was required for fermentative rate. Inorganic phosphate, salts, pH, dilution, and nonspecific protein did not replace it. The activity was not chemically identified and is not shown to be NAD.”
