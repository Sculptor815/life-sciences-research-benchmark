# Grok 4.7 / bio-p01

Original final answer, shared by both scoring rounds.

The claim is not supported. Do not treat Q as a direct, selective inhibitor of ribosomal peptide-bond formation on this packet. The cellular signal is confounded, the decisive assays are missing, and the citation is unverified rather than shown to be fraudulent. Next action: separate bibliographic verification from a cell-free peptidyl-transferase test; neither a catalog miss nor the cellular data establishes mechanism or intent.

## Claim versus what the packet can support

The claim has three linked requirements: Q acts on the ribosome itself (direct), it prefers peptide-bond formation over other ribosomal and non-ribosomal targets (selective), and the inhibited step is peptidyl transfer rather than translation in general. C1’s title, “Q regulates translation,” does not state those requirements. E1–E3 do not establish them.

## Evidence → inference → conclusion

E1. In cells, puromycin incorporation falls by 60% after Q.  
Inference: nascent-chain labeling is lower in the treated population. Puromycin is an aminoacyl-tRNA mimic, so this is a bulk proxy for active translation or for puromycin access to nascent chains. It does not identify the peptidyl-transferase center, and it does not show that Q binds the ribosome.  
Limit: cell type, concentration, exposure time, pulse length, normalization, and whether uptake of puromycin itself changed are unreported. Do not invent them.

E2. ATP falls by 50% and viability is reduced.  
Inference: energy charge and the contributing live-cell population both change in the same experiment that shows the translation proxy falling. Aminoacylation requires ATP; initiation and elongation depend on nucleotide charge; dying cells contribute less translation signal. A 60% drop in incorporation is therefore compatible with secondary suppression.  
Relationship: E2 undercuts any move from E1 to a ribosome-specific mechanism. The two results are not independent support for the claim; E2 is a confounder of E1.

E3. No purified-ribosome assay and no selectivity panel are available.  
Inference: direct action and selectivity were not tested. Absence of those assays is absence of the evidence the claim needs, not evidence that the mechanism is false.  
Conclusion: “directly and selectively inhibits ribosomal peptide-bond formation” is an overclaim. The packet supports only “cellular puromycin incorporation falls under conditions where ATP and viability also fall.”

## Source status

C1 is a local record (BC-28) for P. Orr, 2025, with no DOI. That is incomplete provenance, not a demonstrated fabrication.  
C2: one catalog search found no matching title, but coverage is unspecified. A single miss cannot show that the work does not exist.  
N1: correction and retraction status have not been established. Unknown status is not a retraction.  
A missing DOI, a non-diagnostic catalog miss, or an unevaluated retraction check is a suspicious or incomplete feature only. It does not establish deceptive intent, plagiarism, or that the underlying measurements were invented. The scientific overclaim can be judged from E1–E3 even if C1 is later found intact.

## Benign alternatives

These are alternatives to the claimed mechanism, not findings:

1. Energy stress: the ATP drop secondarily reduces charging and elongation, so puromycin incorporation falls without Q touching the peptidyl-transferase center.
2. Cytotoxicity: fewer viable cells lower the population signal.
3. Upstream translation block: initiation, elongation factors, or aminoacyl-tRNA supply could be inhibited; the title’s weaker claim, “regulates translation,” would fit this and would still not prove peptide-bond inhibition.
4. Assay artifact: Q could reduce puromycin uptake or labeling without changing peptide-bond formation.
5. Bibliographic benign case: the paper exists, is poorly indexed, and either does not make the strong claim or makes it only as an interpretation beyond the assays listed here.

Parallel toxicity and a true ribosome effect are also possible. The packet cannot separate them.

## Check that could disprove the concern

Concern to be tested: that the strong mechanistic claim is unwarranted. A result that would disprove that concern, if obtained and adequately controlled, would be concentration-dependent inhibition of peptide-bond formation on purified ribosomes, at concentrations that do not rely on cellular ATP or viability, with no comparable inhibition in a pre-specified selectivity panel.

Proposed experiment, not a completed result:

- Run a purified-ribosome peptidyl-transferase assay (puromycin or a fragment reaction) with an energy-regeneration system whose nucleotide levels are measured, plus a matched no-ribosome blank.
- In parallel, test aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase charging and at least one initiation or elongation factor activity.
- In cells, measure puromycin incorporation, ATP, and viability at the same times, and report incorporation per viable cell. Also test whether Q changes puromycin uptake.
- Interpret only after those controls. If purified peptidyl transfer is inhibited while ATP, viability, charging, and factor assays are spared, and the cellular effect remains when energy and viability are clamped, the overclaim concern would be disproved for that condition. If inhibition appears only when ATP or viability falls, or only in cells, the concern stands.
- Separately, retrieve BC-28, record any DOI or publisher record, and check correction/retraction status. Finding an unretracted paper would disprove non-existence and unknown-retraction worries; it would not by itself prove the mechanism. Finding that the paper never claimed direct selective peptidyl-transferase inhibition would confirm extrapolation. Failure to find the paper in one incomplete catalog would not.

## What would change the recommendation

Adopt the strong claim only if a direct peptidyl-transferase result and a selectivity panel exist and the cellular confounds in E2 are shown not to explain E1. Clear the source concern only by retrieving the record and its correction status. Until then, cite Q only as associated with reduced cellular puromycin incorporation alongside lower ATP and viability, and treat both mechanism and authorial intent as unresolved.
